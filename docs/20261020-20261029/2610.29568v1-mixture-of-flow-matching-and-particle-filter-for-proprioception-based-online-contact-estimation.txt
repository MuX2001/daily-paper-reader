Mixture of Flow Matching and Particle Filter for
Proprioception-Based Online Contact Estimation
Seo Wook Han , and Min Jun Kim , Member, IEEE,
Abstract—In this article, we propose ContactFlowFilter, a novel
approach for contact estimation that determines the number of
active contacts, their locations, and the associated force vectors.
We consider a robot manipulator equipped with joint torque
sensors and a base force/torque sensor; such a proprioceptive-
only setup presents a challenging inverse problem characterized
by (i) multi-modality from non-unique solutions, (ii) temporal
dependencies in state evolution, and (iii) discrete contact events
involving contact addition/removal. Existing approaches, such as
particle filters (PFs) and learning-based methods, often struggle
to address these challenges simultaneously. To overcome this,
ContactFlowFilter integrates a generative model into the PF
framework. The recursive structure of the PF captures temporal
dependencies, while the flow matching model serves as an
auxiliary proposal distribution to track multi-modal beliefs and
detect contact events. Extensive validation in both simulation and
real-world experiments confirms the effectiveness of the proposed
method. In real-world settings, ContactFlowFilter achieves the
lowest dissimilarity from the target distribution, with errors of
only 0.36 cm and 0.88 cm for single- and dual-contact scenarios,
respectively, while operating at 0.69 ms and 2.69 ms.
Index Terms—Contact estimation, particle filters, generative
models, flow matching, proprioceptive sensors.

the contact state that best explain these sparse, internal sensor
measurements. As shown in Fig. 1, our objective is to estimate
the contact state, which includes the contact locations on
distinct links (ri ∈R3), the corresponding three-dimensional
(3D) force vectors (Fi ∈R3), and the number of contacts (up
to two). By constraining our scope to scenarios with up to two
point contacts, we isolate and tackle the core challenges of the
contact estimation problem:
• Multi-modality (Singularity): The primary challenge stems
from the inherent ambiguity of the inverse problem. While
the forward mapping (from contact to sensor reading) is
deterministic, the inverse mapping is ambiguous; as illus-
trated in Fig. 2(a), distinct contact states may correspond
to identical sensor measurements. This singularity [24],
[25] implies that the contact state might not be reliably
determined from a single observation, leading to inherent
uncertainty in the estimation. Consequently, the probability
distribution of possible solutions becomes multi-modal. Due
to the uncertain nature of the problem, it is more informative
and important to predict the multi-modal distribution, rather
than yielding a single deterministic solution.
• Temporal Dependency: Although the ambiguity of inferring
contact states from single observations remains an inher-
ent problem, it can be mitigated in specific contexts by
leveraging temporal dependency. For instance, consider the
discrete state evolution depicted in Fig. 2(b). In this scenario,
the ambiguity is significantly compounded as the number
of contacts increases (e.g., when simultaneously estimating
two contacts). Nevertheless, estimating the second contact
becomes more reliable by incorporating past information
about the first contact [21]. In the case of continuous evo-
lution such as the sliding contact force shown in Fig. 2(b),
knowing the previous contact location simplifies the estima-
tion. Performing localization around the previous estimate
improves efficiency by avoiding the need to explore the
entire robot surface [22]. Consequently, leveraging temporal
dependency, specifically utilizing past estimates, provides a
prior context for inferring the current state.
• Contact Event Detection: Determining the number of active
contacts requires identifying discrete transitions, specifically
the addition or removal of contacts (see Fig. 2(c)). While
providing a prior context enables effective temporal track-
ing, its validity strictly depends on the correct discrete state;
otherwise, undetected contact events cause the estimator
to propagate incorrect past information. Therefore, robust
contact event detection serves as a critical prerequisite for
reliable estimation of the complete contact state, including
the number, location, and force.
Furthermore, these challenges need to be addressed while
remaining computationally efficient, as practical applications
often require real-time estimation of the contact state.
As detailed in Section II, prior work on proprioception-
based contact estimation can be broadly categorized into
model-based and learning-based approaches. The former is
dominated by particle filter (PF)-based methods [22], [26]–
[29], while the latter includes deep learning techniques, from
probabilistic mapping [30] to diffusion models [21]. Each of
these categories exhibits distinct strengths and weaknesses.
While PF-based approaches are well-suited for addressing
temporal dependency through recursive belief updates, they
often fail to handle multi-modality due to particle starvation
[31]–[33]. This well-known issue of PFs arises when the parti-
cles fail to populate high-belief regions. In contrast, learning-
based approaches excel at estimating multi-modal distribu-
tions. For instance, to capture multi-modality, probabilistic
mapping [30] evaluates probabilities for each region of a
discretized state space, and generative models [21] learn the
underlying data distribution. These feed-forward approaches,
however, are typically non-recursive and trained on tempo-
rally independent samples, thus lacking a native mechanism
for leveraging temporal dependency. As for contact event
detection, it remains a challenge for both categories of ap-
proaches. Although PFs recursively track contact states, they
rely on hard-to-tune thresholds to identify discrete transitions.
Feed-forward learning-based methods struggle to model these
discrete transitions, as their non-recursive nature inherently
restricts temporal reasoning.
In this paper, we propose a novel framework called Con-
tactFlowFilter that tackles the aforementioned challenges: our
approach is built upon a recursive structure of PFs for captur-
ing temporal dependency, while a generative model serves as
an auxiliary proposal distribution to track multi-modal beliefs
and detect contact events.

A. Contributions of the Article
As depicted in Fig. 3, ContactFlowFilter updates the con-
tact belief by drawing particles from two complementary
proposal distributions: the PF’s temporal prediction and the
generative auxiliary proposal. By preserving the PF’s recursive
structure, the proposed method effectively captures temporal
dependency, while the generative component complements
the PF by representing multi-modal distributions. Building
upon this hybrid framework, we propose a robust method for
contact event detection that augments conventional criteria by
combining temporal context with generative estimates.
To formally implement the mixture of two proposal distri-
butions, we adopt the dual-sampling concept [31]. We first
formulate the PF motion model as Gaussian sampling on the
robot surface, providing a basis for integrating the generative
model and deriving its importance weights.
The generative component is built upon a novel data rep-
resentation designed to capture the underlying distribution of
contact states. We utilize a fixed-size array of paired contact
points to represent up to two simultaneous contacts, which
allows the model to infer the spatial relationships between the
points. This is supported by our proposed observation repre-
sentation, where spatially aligned robot kinematics data, JTSs
data, and base F/T sensor data are mapped as a sequence along
the kinematic chain. A transformer encoder then processes this
sequence to extract cross-modal correlations.
To achieve real-time performance for high-fidelity temporal
tracking, we employ flow matching [34], [35] as the generative
backbone, leveraging linearized probability paths for few-step
inference rather than curved diffusion trajectories [36]–[38].
We also implement a computationally efficient motion model
using pre-processed mesh data [26].

framework (C1), which is realized through key technical

• C1) A novel hybrid framework, ContactFlowFilter, that
synergistically combines a generative model and a particle
filter to address the key challenges of the contact estimation
problem (multi-modality, temporal dependency, and con-
tact event detection) while ensuring real-time performance.

II. RELATED WORK
Characterizing the state of external contacts can be divided
into four categories. The first two, contact detection (deter-
mining if a contact exists) and contact isolation (identifying
the affected link), confirm the presence of a contact. The latter
two provide more detailed information: contact localization
focuses solely on the contact’s position, while contact esti-
mation aims to determine both location and force simulta-
neously. This section covers the literature on proprioception-
based contact localization and estimation, and a comparative
summary is provided in Table I. A primary approach for these
problems utilizes external force/torque values, which originate
exclusively from contacts rather than the robot’s own dynamics
(see Section III-C for details).
A. Model-Based Approaches
The line-of-force (LoF) method is a widely adopted tech-
nique that geometrically localizes a contact and identifies the
corresponding force from the external wrench [23], [39]–
[41]. Because this geometric construction yields a single,
deterministic solution based solely on the current observation,
it is inherently limited to single-contact scenarios. Although
[23] demonstrates dual-contact estimation on a robot with
redundant sensing (i.e., JTSs and F/T sensors at the base and
end-effector), the LoF method lacks temporal reasoning; the
estimation is instead enabled by the dual F/T sensor setup,
which prevents the spatial mixing of forces. Intuitively, the
base F/T sensor localizes a contact along the robot’s main
kinematic chain, while the end-effector F/T sensor identifies a
second contact on any attached tool.
Another family of methods is based on the PF [22], [26]–
[29], [42]–[44]. As a recursive approach, the PF naturally
incorporates temporal information, allowing it to track contact
states over time. In this framework, a set of particles, repre-
senting contact points, is updated and resampled at each time
step to minimize an error metric, defined via quadratic pro-
gramming (QP). However, a well-known issue with standard
PF-based algorithms is particle starvation [31]–[33], where the
greedy resampling process causes all particles to converge
to a single high-belief region (even to a local minimum),
which prevents the particle set from representing a multi-
modal distribution.
To address the challenge of multi-modality (i.e., the sin-
gularity problem), several approaches have been proposed.
For instance, the methods in [24], [25] employ a non-greedy
strategy to narrow down contact point candidates, evaluating
each using a QP-based error metric. However, extending these
approaches to scenarios with more than one contact point is
not straightforward.
While such non-greedy schemes effectively handle multi-
modality, both they and the LoF method rely on a static
formulation that cannot capture the time-dependent charac-
teristic of the contact estimation problem. In contrast, the PF
framework inherently supports temporal reasoning, making it
a more promising foundation. Therefore, this work focuses
on advancing the PF-based approach by overcoming its own
limitations.
A key limitation of existing PF-based approaches for sce-
narios with more than a single contact [22], [26]–[29] is
their reliance on a hard-to-tune threshold for contact event
detection. While these methods track each contact point with
a dedicated particle set, managing the number of sets depends
on a highly sensitive threshold. Such dependency limits the
adaptation to contact changes, leading to detection errors such
as false positives or missed contacts. To overcome this, we
introduce a robust particle set management scheme that utilizes
the output of flow matching, as detailed in Section V-D.
Additionally, PF-based methods are mainly distinguished
by their strategies for representing and updating particles on
the robot’s surface. These include projecting particles repre-
sented in Cartesian space onto the robot surface [22], [43],
parameterizing the surface with a single variable [29], [42],
and using graph-based searches directly on the mesh [26]–
[28]. In Section IV-B, we introduce a highly efficient particle
update method that performs Gaussian sampling directly on
the robot’s surface by using pre-computed geodesic distances
from the mesh data.
B. Learning-Based Approaches
Recently, several learning-based approaches have been pro-
posed for contact localization [4], [21], [30], [45], [46], which
vary in their formulations. For example, the probabilistic
mapping approach in [30] treats the robot’s link surfaces as 2D
images and estimates the contact probability for each region.

Other formulations include binary classification over prede-
fined points on the robot’s surface [45], [46], or regression of
the contact location, either as a 3D point in Cartesian space
[4] or as a single 1D parameter on the robot’s surface [46].
However, a common limitation of these approaches is that
they do not estimate the contact force and inherently lack a
mechanism for temporal reasoning.
Among these learning-based methods, the work most rele-
vant to ours is the diffusion model-based approach presented
in [21]. While it employs a recursive inference scheme similar
to the PF, relying on temporally independent training data
forces the model to assume that the first contact point remains
fixed. It also shares the common limitation of other learning-
based approaches in its inability to estimate contact force.
In contrast, by integrating the output of a generative model
into the PF framework, the proposed method performs both
temporal reasoning and contact force estimation, while main-
taining the representational power of the generative model. By
utilizing a flow matching model, our approach further achieves
significantly faster inference (15.97 ms →2.69 ms) while even
demonstrating substantial improvements in performance.

B. Flow Matching Model
Generative models, such as diffusion models [36]–[38] and
flow matching models [34], [35], are designed to generate

F. Limitation of CPF
1) Particle Starvation: A fundamental challenge in PFs
is the failure of particles to populate high-belief regions.
This particle starvation [31]–[33] primarily stems from the
mismatch between the proposal and target distributions, as
captured in (9). The motion model is a simple random walk
that does not guide particles toward high-belief regions. Con-
vergence relies solely on the importance resampling process,
requiring a high α value in (25) to ensure that only particles
with higher weights survive in a more greedy manner. How-
ever, coupled with the intrinsic sparsity of the solution space,
this setting further constricts p(Ot|·), resulting in isolated, nar-
row high-belief regions surrounded by low-probability areas
(e.g., see Fig. 7(a)). This prevents particles from traversing
to other modes, causing the PF to become trapped in local
minima or fail to capture the multi-modality. For example, as
depicted in Fig. 7(b), in a scenario with two belief modes,

evaluations within the measurement model. Although lowering
the threshold (¯ϵ in Algorithm 1) can mitigate the delay, it often
induces false positives, such as initializing new particle sets
in the absence of actual contact events.

E. ContactFlowFilter
As detailed in Algorithm 4, ContactFlowFilter integrates
two complementary sampling strategies: standard sampling
(incorporating motion and measurement models) for tempo-
ral prediction (line 1) and dual-sampling to address multi-
modality (line 2). The particle sets generated from these
procedures are subsequently merged (line 3), where each
unified set Xt,i ∈Xt contains a varying number of standard
and dual particles. To ensure a consistent size for each particle
set throughout the recursive estimation, a fixed number of
particles M is drawn via importance resampling (line 4).
Finally, the number of particle sets is adjusted to match the
actual number of contacts (line 5).
VI. IMPLEMENTATION DETAILS
The proposed method, ContactFlowFilter, was implemented
using both Python and C++. The flow matching model was
implemented in Python using JAX [58], while all other com-
ponents were written in C++. To bridge these components, we
utilized Python bindings for the C++ modules. The QP in (23)
was solved using qpSWIFT [59]. Training and evaluation were
performed on a system with an Intel Core i7-12700 CPU, an
RTX 4090 GPU, and 64GB of RAM. The experimental setup
consisted of a 7-DOF Franka Emika Panda robot arm equipped
with JTSs and a base-mounted F/T sensor (ATI Omega191)
(see Fig. 1).
A. Dataset for Dual-Sampling
The dataset for training the flow matching model was
collected in a simulation environment using MuJoCo [60]. A
robot with a random configuration was simulated, operating at
1000 Hz. During each simulation, one or two contact forces
were applied, and a data point (ξ ∈D) was collected once
the system reached a stable state. Each contact force, lying
within the friction cone, had a magnitude randomly selected
between 10 and 25 N. To facilitate sim-to-real transfer, noise
was added to the JTSs and base F/T sensor measurements (as
demonstrated in Section VII-D). A total of 102.4M data points
were collected, of which 80% were used for training and the
remaining 20% for evaluation. Empirically, we observed that
constructing the dataset with 70% dual-contact cases yielded
optimal performance.
B. Training Details for Dual-Sampling
We utilized the shortcut flow matching model [35] with
K = 128 to enable fast inference with reduced ODE steps. As
demonstrated in Section VII-A, two ODE steps were found to
be sufficient; thus, this setting was adopted in the remainder
of this paper. For stable training, the dataset was normalized
to have zero mean and unit variance. The vector field network,
vθ, was trained for 5M steps with a batch size of 1024, taking
approximately 66 hours. The network comprises a total of
18.1M parameters: 12.6M for the global feature encoder, 1.6M
for the pointwise feature encoder, and 3.9M for the decoder
(see Fig. 11).

C. Asynchronous Sampling and Execution Time
In our implementation, the proposed algorithm performs
sampling and dual-sampling asynchronously, as illustrated
in Fig. 12. The flow matching inference is executed on a
dedicated thread, separate from the main algorithm. The last
available sample is incorporated into the PF through sample
allocation and weight computation.
We configure each particle set to contain M = 100 particles
with 64 dual particles (Ns = 32) to balance execution time
with the particle density required to represent the contact
distribution. Under these settings, the flow matching inference
takes approximately 2.57 ms, regardless of the number of
contacts. For the main algorithmic loop, the execution time
varies depending on the number of contacts: 0.69 ms for
the single-contact scenario and 2.69 ms for the dual-contact
scenario.

C. Comparative Study of Contact Estimation Algorithms
In Experiment C, we conduct a comparative evaluation to
assess the contact estimation performance of the proposed
ContactFlowFilter. Our evaluation focuses on two primary
aspects: the dissimilarity between the particle distribution and
the ground-truth distribution, and the estimation error with
respect to the ground-truth contact state (rt,i and Ft,i). As in
the previous section, we consider a sequential contact scenario,
i.e., the period until the end of Phase 1 (see Fig. 14). We
benchmark our approach against the following three baseline
methods:
• CPF [22]: This baseline is identical to Algorithm 2 except
for its motion model. Instead of using pre-processed mesh
data, the motion model first adds 3D Gaussian noise to the
particles and then projects them onto the robot’s surfaces.
This projection step introduces computational overhead,
increasing the execution time to 33.93 ms and 69.08 ms
for single- and dual-contact scenarios, respectively.
• MCP-EP [26]: In this baseline, the dual-sampling step in
Algorithm 4 is replaced with a random sampling strategy.
When the current particle set fails to explain the observation
(as indicated by a high QP error), the algorithm generates
random particles to explore neighboring links. Its execution
time is identical to that of the proposed method: 0.69 ms
and 2.69 ms for single-contact and dual-contact scenarios,
respectively.
• Dual-Particle only: This baseline utilizes samples generated
via the flow matching model. To enable comparison with PF-
based approaches, we use the dual particles for this baseline,
i.e., the particles allocated to the particle set (output of
Algorithm 3).

VIII. SUMMARY, CONCLUSION, AND DISCUSSION
In this paper, we propose a novel method called Contact-
FlowFilter for proprioception-based online contact estimation.
Our method integrates a generative model with a particle filter
(PF) by adopting a dual-sampling concept. In this framework,
particles are drawn from a mixture of two proposal distribu-
tions: (i) “standard particles” from the PF’s prediction step and
(ii) “dual particles” from the generative model. The mixture
proposal enables ContactFlowFilter to simultaneously tackle
key challenges in contact estimation: multi-modality, temporal
dependency, and contact event detection.
To address temporal dependency, we utilize the PF frame-
work as the foundation of our approach, leveraging its re-
cursive nature to handle time-dependent belief distributions.
However, PFs alone encounter two fundamental limitations
that restrict their ability to address the remaining challenges.
To handle multi-modality, we resolve “particle starvation”
(Section IV-F1) by incorporating dual particles from the
generative model. This dual-sampling step enables the direct
generation of samples in high-belief regions, even where
the particles fail to populate. To mitigate the sensitivity of
conventional criteria for contact event detection, we propose a
method leveraging generative estimates. Our approach main-
tains robustness against “particle drift” (Section IV-F2), which
typically leads to contact state tracking failure; e.g., significant
errors in localization or erroneous contact event detection.
To implement our dual-sampling strategy, we employ a
flow matching model, specifically exploiting its linearized
probability path. This feature enables rapid state estimation,


HAN AND KIM: MIXTURE OF FLOW MATCHING AND PARTICLE FILTER FOR PROPRIOCEPTION-BASED ONLINE CONTACT ESTIMATION
critical for high-fidelity temporal tracking, while remaining
highly effective at capturing the multi-modal distribution of
contact states. Such capabilities are underpinned by our novel
architecture, which incorporates a spatially aligned data rep-
resentation and a tailored transformer-based neural network.
We validate ContactFlowFilter through extensive compara-
tive studies in both simulation and real-world settings. Quan-
titative and qualitative analyses confirm that the proposed
method effectively resolves the aforementioned challenges.
We validate the proposed generative architecture for dual-
sampling (Section VII-A) and the robustness of our method
in handling discrete contact transitions (Section VII-B). Ul-
timately, our approach outperforms the baselines in contact
estimation, achieving the highest accuracy and the lowest
dissimilarity to the target posterior (Sections VII-C and VII-D,
and supplementary video).
Despite the promising results, the current implementation
of our method is limited to estimating a maximum of two
contacts. This constraint stems from the generative component,
which relies on a fixed-size array of training samples with
up to two contacts (Section V-A1). Although it readily scales
to additional contacts, we constrained the scope to isolate
the core challenges, particularly multi-modality. For up to
two contacts, the generative model captures multi-modality
within the solution space bounded by the base F/T sensor.
Beyond two, estimating multiple contacts from single obser-
vations becomes ill-posed, as the solution space for the time-
independent model contains numerous distinct contact states
yielding identical sensor measurements. We plan to extend the
method to multi-contact scenarios by incorporating additional
sensing modalities (e.g., F/T sensors or a dual-arm setup) or
by conditioning the generative component on temporal context
to disambiguate the solution space.
