ABSTRACT 

Vision–language–action (VLA) models have shown promise in robotic manipu-lation, but imitation learning (IL) alone often struggles to master complex long-horizon skills. Reinforcement learning (RL) offers an effective avenue for further improving VLA policies. However, existing VLA-RL methods typically suffer from delayed and biased credit assignment due to trajectory-level sparse rewards, while imbalanced subtask mastery creates bottlenecks that limit overall perfor-mance. To address these challenges, we propose AtomicRL, an RL post-training framework that leverages atomic task structures for reliable credit assignment and adaptive exploration. AtomicRL introduces Semantic-Aware Policy Optimization (SAPO), which uses semantic-aware gating to constrain TD residuals from failed atomic subtasks from propagating across task boundaries, mitigating credit contam-ination while preserving intra-subtask credit assignment and value bootstrapping. In addition, Bottleneck-Adaptive Target Exploration (BATE) dynamically coor-dinates task sampling and local exploration incentives based on the mastery of individual atomic subtasks, directing learning toward the remaining bottlenecks. Finally, we develop a Real2Sim2Real pipeline that bridges online RL in simulation with real-world deployment, facilitating practical transfer to physical robots. On LIBERO-LONG, AtomicRL achieves absolute performance gains of 3.6% and 4.0% over PPO across two VLA architectures. On CALVIN ABC-D, it improves full-task performance by up to 9.5% over PPO. Combined with the Real2Sim2Real pipeline, AtomicRL further improves real-world task performance by an average of 21.3%. These results demonstrate that AtomicRL effectively improves the learning efficiency and manipulation performance of VLA policies on long-horizon tasks. 

1 INTRODUCTION 

Recent advances in Vision-Language Models (VLMs) (Beyer et al., 2024; Deitke et al., 2024; Yang et al., 2025) have established Vision–Language–Action (VLA) (Intelligence et al., 2025b; Kim et al., 2025; Liu et al., 2025b) models as a prominent paradigm for robotic manipulation. Although imitation learning (IL) from large-scale robot demonstrations enables VLA models to acquire diverse manipulation skills, its performance is inherently constrained by the coverage of the demonstration data. Reinforcement learning (RL) overcomes this limitation by directly optimizing policies with task feedback obtained through environment interaction, and recent work (Li et al., 2026; Xu et al., 2026) has successfully applied RL to VLA models. However, existing VLA-RL methods still face substantial challenges in long-horizon manipulation tasks. First, most methods rely on terminal-only success rewards. In an episode that completes the first k

subtasks but fails at subtask k + 1 , the absence of a terminal success reward causes generalized advan-tage estimation (GAE) to propagate failure-related temporal-difference (TD) residuals backward to 

t0, suppressing the advantages of correctly executed actions. We refer to this as credit contamination, where useful behavior in failed trajectories is systematically devalued, with the effect worsening as the horizon grows. Uniform task sampling and fixed exploration then waste interaction budget on saturated subtasks while providing insufficient exploration for the bottlenecks. To address these challenges, we propose AtomicRL, as illustrated in Fig. 1, an RL post-training framework built around atomic task structure. Our key insight is that semantic subtasks provide a unified basis for both the propagation of learning signals and the allocation of exploration resources. AtomicRL couples Semantic-Aware Policy Optimization (SAPO) with Bottleneck-Adaptive Target Exploration (BATE), supported by a lightweight Progress–Completion Critic (PCC) that estimates atomic subtask progress and completion boundaries. PCC also provides progress estimates for con-structing dense progress-based shaping rewards (PBSR). SAPO modulates TD-residual propagation in advantage estimation with a soft gate conditioned on semantic boundaries. Within each atomic subtask, it preserves standard GAE trace decay; across subtask boundaries, it maintains propagation between successful subtasks while attenuating residuals from failed subtasks. By leaving one-step TD residuals and value bootstrapping unchanged, SAPO reduces failure interference while preserving intra-subtask credit assignment and successful subtask dependencies. BATE tracks the mastery of each subtask via a moving average of its completion rate, jointly adjusts the sampling weight of full tasks and the strength of subtask-level entropy regularization, directing interaction and exploration resources toward bottleneck subtasks. Finally, to support physical deployment, we develop an auto-mated Real2Sim2Real pipeline. Given a single real-world multimodal demonstration and the required auxiliary information, the pipeline reconstructs an interactive simulation environment. It connects simulation-based RL with real-robot deployment, shifting costly physical trial-and-error to parallel simulation with reproducible resets. We conduct extensive experiments in both simulation and the real world. On LIBERO-LONG (Liu et al., 2023) AtomicRL achieves success rates of 99.0% with π0.5 (Intelligence et al., 2025b) and 98.8% with OpenVLA-OFT (Kim et al., 2025), improving over PPO by 3.6% and 4.0%. On CALVIN ABC-D (Mees et al., 2022), the average completed sequence length improves from 3.56 to 4.56, while the success rate of completing full five-subtask chain improves by 9.5% over PPO. Combined with the Real2Sim2Real pipeline, AtomicRL further yields an average improvement of 21.3% on real-robot long-horizon tasks. These results demonstrate the effectiveness of AtomicRL for long-horizon VLA reinforcement learning. Overall, our contributions are as follows: • We introduce AtomicRL, an RL post-training framework built around atomic task structure for long-horizon robotic manipulation. • We propose SAPO to mitigate cross-subtask credit interference and BATE to adaptively direct task sampling and exploration toward bottleneck subtasks. • We develop a Real2Sim2Real pipeline that streamlines simulation construction and RL training for efficient real-robot deployment. 2• We validate the effectiveness of AtomicRL through extensive experiments conducted in both simulated environments and real-world robots. 

2 RELATED WORK 

2.1 REINFORCEMENT LEARNING FOR VLA M ODELS 

Reinforcement learning (RL) optimizes policies through environment interaction and task-level feedback. Recent work has explored both offline and online RL paradigms for VLA policies. Offline RL approaches leverage task feedback to enable continued policy improvement (Huang et al., 2025; Zhang et al., 2025b;a). π∗ 

> 0.6

(Intelligence et al., 2025a) integrates expert demonstrations, autonomous interaction data, and human corrections through advantage-conditioned learning. For scalable online training, VLA-RL (Lu et al., 2025) and SimpleVLA-RL (Li et al., 2026) explore frameworks for continually improving VLA policies through environment interaction, while RL4VLA (Liu et al., 2026) systematically investigates how different RL algorithms affect VLA generalization. Furthermore, Flow Q-Learning (Park et al., 2025), Flow-GRPO (Liu et al., 2025a) and πRL (Chen et al., 2026a) investigate online RL fine-tuning for flow-matching policies, addressing challenges in action likelihood computation and exploration. RL for long-horizon manipulation introduces additional challenges due to sparse and delayed task feedback (Chen et al., 2026b; Li et al., 2025c; Dalal et al., 2024). STARE-VLA (Xu et al., 2025) exploits the semantic stage structure of trajectories to perform stage-level preference optimization and reward shaping, while IG-RFT (Su et al., 2026) combines trajectory-level and subtask-level rewards to provide intermediate guidance. However, dense reward shaping provides useful process guidance over long-horizon trajectories, but cannot eliminate the spurious interference caused by later-stage failures. In contrast, AtomicRL gates the propagation of advantage signals at the semantic boundaries of atomic tasks to prevent cross-task interference from unreliable signals, while allocating the exploration budget to bottleneck atomic tasks to improve the efficiency of long-horizon RL. 2.2 ONLINE REINFORCEMENT LEARNING IN REAL WORLD 

Online reinforcement learning for real-world manipulation is constrained by interaction costs and safety risks. To reduce reliance on real-robot trial and error, some research focuses on learning action-conditioned world models and optimizing policies through imagined interactions (Team et al., 2025; Li et al., 2025a; Zhang et al., 2026c; Jiang et al., 2026a). WoVR (Jiang et al., 2026b) addresses prediction errors and their accumulation to improve the reliability of model-generated rollouts for VLA reinforcement fine-tuning , while PlayWorld (Yin et al., 2026) and RISE (Yang et al., 2026b) learn world models from autonomous robot interactions and transfers policies trained through model-based reinforcement learning to real hardware. Although world models can effectively capture short-term dynamics, long-horizon predictions still exhibit visual distortions and action–state inconsistencies. Other studies adopt an explicit Real2Sim2Real method, combining real-scene reconstruction with physics simulation to provide interactive environments for policy optimization (Li et al., 2025b; Wang et al., 2026). GSWorld (Jiang et al., 2025) integrates Gaussian-on-Mesh assets with physics engines to support visually faithful policy training, while RialTo (Torne et al., 2024) constructs digital twins of real-world environments and uses reinforcement learning in simulation to improve policy robustness. However, constructing environments requires coordinating scene reconstruction, physical asset preparation, and control alignment, making it complex and costly to reproduce. AtomicRL introduces an automated Real2Sim2Real pipeline that uses only a single demonstration to reconstruct a real-world manipulation scene as an interactive simulation environment for long-horizon online RL. The trained policies are then transferred to physical robots for evaluation.

We consider a language-conditioned partially observable Markov decision process for long-horizon robotic manipulation. At each decision step t, the robot receives an observation ot, which may include third-person images, wrist-camera images, and proprioceptive states, and outputs an action chunk at = ( at, 1, . . . , a t,H a ), where Ha denotes the chunk length. A long-horizon instruction is decomposed into an ordered sequence of K atomic subtasks, G = ( g1, . . . , g K ). We denote the active subtask index by zt ∈ 1, . . . , K . For nonterminal transitions, the semantic-boundary indicator is defined as bt = I[zt+1 ̸ = zt], indicating whether execution of at leads to a subtask transition. Our objective is to maximize full-task success under a fixed environment-interaction budget by improving credit assignment and directing exploration toward bottleneck subtasks. 3.2 SEMANTIC -A WARE POLICY OPTIMIZATION

We develop an automated Real2Sim2Real reinforcement learning pipeline that unifies interactive environment construction, policy optimization in simulation, and deployment and evaluation on physical robots. Given a single multimodal real-world demonstration τreal , metadata m specifying the robot model and supplementary object observations Daux where needed, the pipeline constructs a simulation environment aligned with the physical system, a shared observation–action interface specification, and a set of reproducible randomized initial layouts: 

(Esim , C, S0) = B(τreal , m, Daux ), (10) where Esim is the reconstructed interactive simulation environment, C specifies the shared obser-vation–action interface, S0 is a set of reproducible randomized initial layouts, and B denotes the pipeline workflow. The generated environment is then integrated into the RLinf (Yu et al., 2025) distributed training framework, where SAPO and targeted exploration optimize the initial policy to obtain θRL = Optimize( θ0; Esim , C, S0). Following pre-deployment validation, the optimized policy is deployed on the physical robot through the same interface specification C for task evaluation.

We conduct policy-learning ablations on LIBERO-LONG using a weaker SFT checkpoint trained with fewer demonstrations than those used in the main evaluations. This few-shot initialization allows us to examine the contribution of each component under a less proficient initial policy. 

Reward shaping and SAPO. To investigate the effect of reward granularity, we use the PCC module to construct process-level dense rewards and subtask-level rewards, and compare them under both PPO and SAPO. As shown in Tab. 4 (a), denser rewards do not always yield better performance: under SAPO, relatively sparse subtask-level rewards outperform process-level dense rewards. When the policy success rate is low, dense rewards provide stronger exploration guidance and accelerate early learning. As the policy improves, however, their benefits diminish, while persistent auxiliary 8rewards may dilute the terminal success signal and shift optimization toward local gains. Motivated by this observation, we use linear annealing to gradually reduce the PBSR weight, providing stronger dense-reward guidance early in training and emphasizing the sparse terminal reward later. 5 CONCLUSION 

In this work, we introduce AtomicRL, a VLA RL post-training framework for long-horizon robotic manipulation centered on atomic task structure. To address temporal credit interference and im-balanced atomic subtask mastery, AtomicRL uses PCC to estimate task progress and completion boundaries, SAPO to regulate the cross-boundary propagation of TD residuals based on subtask execution outcomes, and BATE to shift task sampling and local exploration toward insufficiently mastered bottleneck subtasks. We further develop a Real2Sim2Real pipeline that connects real-task reconstruction, simulation-based RL, and real-robot deployment, enabling policy improvement in sim-ulation environments with fast parallel interactions and reproducible resets. A.1 FUTURE WORK AND LIMITATIONS 

AtomicRL improves temporal credit assignment across atomic tasks through SAPO and concentrates exploration resources on insufficiently mastered bottleneck subtasks through BATE. Although the experimental results demonstrate its effectiveness in long-horizon robotic manipulation, the current approach still has several limitations. • AtomicRL improves the utilization of interaction data through more effective credit assignment and exploration-resource allocation, but does not fundamentally address the overall efficiency of online RL training. Even with open-source RL frameworks such as RLinf (Yu et al., 2025) and SERL (Luo et al., 2025), online RL still requires substantial environment interaction and training time, particularly in real-robot settings where interaction and exploration costs remain high. Reducing the interaction required for effective policy learning, improving online RL training efficiency, and lowering the cost of real-robot training therefore remain important directions for future work. • Our experiments primarily focus on long-horizon tabletop manipulation tasks and initialize RL from SFT policies. The effectiveness of AtomicRL on more open and dynamic tasks requiring stronger embodiment generalization remains to be further evaluated. Recent Agentic RL ap-proaches, such as HarnessVLA (Zhang et al., 2026b) and AtomBridge (Pang et al., 2026) have shown potential for open-world tasks and embodiment generalization, providing an alternative direction for long-horizon robotic manipulation. A promising direction is to transfer AtomicRL’s use and optimization of atomic task structure to Agentic RL frameworks, enabling robots to dynamically form and adapt atomic task structures in more open environments and potentially improving the generalization and training efficiency of long-horizon manipulation. Training Setup. For all LIBERO and real-world experiments, we train PCC offline on annotated demonstrations. PCC uses a frozen SigLIP visual encoder with approximately 400M parameters, initialized from the π0.5 visual backbone, together with lightweight trainable temporal and prediction modules. For data annotation, we adapt the annotation pipeline of AtomicVLA (Zhang et al., 2026a) to generate fine-grained annotations for demonstration trajectories. We annotate 150 trajectories for LIBERO and 50 trajectories per real-world task, which are subsequently used for offline PCC training. The trained PCC is kept fixed during RL post-training. All RL post-training experiments are implemented in the RLinf framework. All training is performed on eight NVIDIA H200 GPUs, while inference is conducted on a single NVIDIA GeForce RTX 5090 GPU.
