# Abstract 

General-purpose multimodal models have shown promise as direct robot action policies, yet whether they grasp the physics underlying real-world interactions and can apply that under-standing to guide action remains underexplored. We investigate this question using contact-rich manipulation as a representative testbed, examining tactile understanding as a concrete instance of this broader capability. Contact-rich manipulation requires interpreting tactile feedback and adjusting actions after obstruction. Existing learned tactile policies acquire these capabilities through specialized pretraining and per-task optimization, with substantial data and training costs. We present GPT-as-Tactile-Policy, which summarizes expert demonstrations into task guidance and tactile references for contact assessment and corrective action. GPT-6-Astra combines this context with multimodal observations and interaction history to generate control commands and adjust actions through contact feedback. Without additional robotics-specific pretraining or per-task policy fine-tuning, our method achieves 93.8% mean success across five UniVTAC tasks. Overall performance is comparable to state-of-the-art policies, with higher success on selected tasks. On Insert Hole, tactile feedback improves success from 31% to 99%. Execution analysis further demonstrates contact-guided probing and correction, supporting manipulation progress through explicit expert experience and online feedback. Corresponding authors. arXiv:2610.28457v1 [cs.RO] 20 Oct 2026 1. Introduction 

The role of general-purpose large models in robotics is expanding from task planning and skill selection (Brohan et al., 2023) and control program generation (Liang et al., 2023) to direct action decisions. Recent evaluations of GPT-6-Astra show that a general-purpose multimodal model can generate numerical robot actions from observations, task instructions, and execution history, using feedback to determine subsequent behavior (Team et al., 2026a). These studies progressively connect general knowledge to robot action, raising a fundamental question: do general-purpose multimodal models grasp the physics underlying real-world interactions, and can they apply that understanding to guide their actions? Exploring this question requires examining how models reason the physical consequences of their actions and use interaction feedback to adjust subsequent behavior (Huang et al., 2022; Liu et al., 2023). Tactile understanding provides a representative test case for a model’s grasp of physics, because interpreting tactile feedback involves making sense of physical phenomena such as contact, deformation, and constrained motion. We therefore choose contact-rich tasks such as insertion and alignment as our experimental setting (Huang et al., 2025; Bi et al., 2026). Even after identifying a target and reaching the contact location, a robot can be obstructed by local pose errors and hidden geometric constraints (Huang et al., 2024; Xue et al., 2025). External vision provides scene layout and visible object motion, but offers limited access to deformation and motion constraints at the contact interface (Chen et al., 2026; Higuera et al., 2026). In this setting, tactile feedback allows us to examine whether models can connect the observed physical effects to their own actions and use that understanding to guide subsequent control. Existing research has developed learned policies that integrate touch into contact-rich manip-ulation. Representative approaches include ACT with the UniVTAC encoder (Chen et al., 2026), the tactile-predictive N0-VTLA (Team et al., 2026c), and the tactile-native world-action model N0-TWAM (Team et al., 2026b). These approaches acquire contact representations and manipulation capabilities through representation learning, pretraining, and downstream policy optimization. Their use of expert experience therefore involves training dedicated representations or updating policy parameters for downstream tasks. This motivates exploring an alternative: making expert manipulation experience directly available to a general-purpose model as task guidance, without an additional task-specific policy training stage. ## 2. Related Work 

2.1. Language Models as Robot Policies 

Language models have been used to translate pretrained task knowledge into robot behavior through skill selection, program generation, and motion planning. SayCan Brohan et al. (2023) combines language-model estimates of task relevance with value-function estimates of skill feasibility to select skill sequences for long-horizon manipulation. Code as Policies Liang et al. (2023) generates policy programs that process perception outputs and parameterize control primitives, using program logic to express motion parameters, conditional behavior, and feedback loops. Instruct2Act Huang et al. (2023a) maps multimodal instructions to Python policy programs that invoke perception, planning, and action interfaces. RoboScript Chen et al. (2024) emphasizes the deployability of generated programs by integrating perception, motion planning, and control through a unified interface for simulation and real robots. VoxPoser Huang et al. (2023b) grounds language-inferred affordances and constraints in visual observations through composable 3D value maps, which support closed-loop trajectory generation through model-based planning. These approaches connect language-model knowledge to robot execution through different intermediate representations. Execution feedback further enables language models to revise their decisions based on observed outcomes. Inner Monologue Huang et al. (2022) incorporates success detection, scene descriptions, and human interaction as language feedback to update task plans during execution. REFLECT Liu et al. (2023) constructs hierarchical summaries of robot experiences from multisensory observations, uses a language model to explain failures, and provides these explanations to a language-based planner for corrective planning. These studies incorporate the consequences of previous actions into subsequent decisions. Another line of work investigates language models that generate explicit action parameters. Natural Language as Policies Mikami et al. (2024) reasons over textual descriptions of tasks and scene objects to generate coordinate-level parameters for pick-and-place or sweeping actions, reducing reliance on intermediate policy code. GPT-as-Policy Team et al. (2026a) systematically evaluates GPT-6-Astra as an embodied policy across multiple domains, examining numerical action generation, cooperation with learned policies, and feedback-driven adaptation. Following this direct action-decision paradigm, We study this direct-policy paradigm in contact-rich manipulation, focusing on how a general-purpose multimodal model uses visual–tactile feedback to interpret contact and select subsequent actions. 

2.2. Visuo-Tactile Manipulation Policies 

Visuo-tactile manipulation combines scene observations with local contact feedback to support fine-grained perception and control. VITaL Pretraining George et al. (2025) investigates visuo-tactile pretraining for imitation learning, showing benefits for both policies that use touch 4during execution and policies deployed without tactile input. 3D-ViTac Huang et al. (2024) integrates visual and tactile observations into a unified 3D representation that preserves their spatial structure and relationships, and combines this representation with diffusion policies for fine-grained manipulation. Reactive Diffusion Policy Xue et al. (2025) introduces a slow-fast hierarchy that combines latent action-chunk generation with high-frequency tactile feedback control, allowing the policy to respond to contact changes during chunk execution. These studies investigate how tactile information supports skill learning and execution through representation learning and feedback-aware control architectures. Recent work further integrates tactile sensing with the task knowledge of vision-language-action (VLA) models. VLA-Touch Bi et al. (2026) keeps the base VLA fixed while using a pretrained tactile-language model to provide semantic feedback for high-level planning and a diffusion-based controller to refine actions using tactile signals. Tactile-VLA Huang et al. (2025) combines tactile sensing with hybrid position-force control to connect vision-language knowledge to physical interaction; its CoT variant further explores tactile-feedback-driven fail-ure analysis and strategy adaptation. OmniVTLA Cheng et al. (2026) addresses heterogeneous tactile inputs through a dual-path tactile encoder and semantically aligned representations.

Together, these approaches incorporate touch into semantic understanding, policy adaptation, and action generation at different decision levels. Another line of work uses predictions of physical interactions to support manipulation. Visuo-Tactile World Models Higuera et al. Our work studies a complementary setting in which a general-purpose multimodal model serves as a direct action policy with its parameters fixed. Task knowledge is supplied through in-context guidance, and the model combines this guidance with visual–tactile observations and execution history to select subsequent actions. Our focus is on how the model interprets contact feedback and uses it to make online action decisions, complementing research on learned visuo-tactile representations, control architectures, and predictive models. 

## 3. Method 

3.1. GPT-as-Tactile-Policy 

GPT-as-Policy uses a general-purpose multimodal model as a closed-loop robot action policy, selecting actions from the task instruction, observations, and interaction history (Team et al., 2026a). In its direct-control formulation, GPT generates numerical end-effector and gripper targets, which a generic inverse-kinematics controller converts into robot motion; new observa-tions after execution return to the model. The model therefore selects the next operation using available observations and feedback from preceding actions. 5For contact-rich manipulation, we incorporate bilateral tactile feedback and task guidance summarized from expert demonstrations into this decision process. As shown in Fig. 1A, the framework comprises offline experience summarization and online feedback control. Offline, GPT-6-Astra jointly analyzes expert actions, contact changes, and task progress to construct fixed context C𝜏 for task 𝜏 . Online, the same model serves as the sole action policy, combin-ing task guidance, visuo-tactile observations, and robot proprioception to interpret current contact responses and select actions. The resulting policy uses both fixed task experience and observations and history that evolve throughout the current interaction. We formulate this policy as conditional action generation. At step 𝑡 , the observation is denoted by 𝑜 𝑡 = (𝑉 𝑡 , 𝑠 𝑡 , T𝑡 ), where 𝑉 𝑡 contains scene and wrist-camera images, 𝑠 𝑡 denotes the robot’s proprioceptive state, and T𝑡 comprises tactile observations from fingertips. 3.2. Task Knowledge Construction from Expert Demonstrations 

To provide task guidance for the online policy in Section 3.1, we construct fixed task context from expert demonstrations before execution. These demonstrations contain sequential visual and tactile observations, robot states, and expert actions, recording changes in contact and task progress during manipulation. As shown in Fig. 1A, GPT-6-Astra jointly analyzes these sequences, relating contact changes and object motion before and after actions to summarize how experts assess progress, select adjustments, and evaluate their effects. The resulting guidance forms the textual Task Skill 𝐾 𝜏 .The Task Skill organizes this experience into task objectives, manipulation principles, evi-dence for stage transitions, and corrective strategies for obstructions. It specifies which obser-vations to examine and how they should inform subsequent actions. For example, increasing deformation without object advancement during insertion prompts retraction to relieve contact, followed by a positional or angular adjustment evaluated through subsequent feedback. This knowledge gives tactile changes operational meaning, helping the model determine when to advance, when to adjust, and which feedback to examine when assessing an adjustment. 3.4. Closed-Loop Action Generation from Interaction Feedback 

During online execution, the decision policy 𝜋 𝜃 assesses manipulation progress using the current observation 𝑜 𝑡 and interaction history H𝑡 . It constructs action 𝑎 𝑡 from the latest measured robot state. After checks on command bounds and format, a generic inverse-kinematics controller executes the target for one to five control steps. A new observation 𝑜 𝑡 +1 is then acquired. Short execution horizons allow prompt contact assessment and adjustment of subsequent motion. Guided by Task Skill 𝐾 𝜏 , the policy determines whether alignment or grasp adjustments are needed. It then generates the next action 𝑎 𝑡 +1.When observations leave the adjustment direction uncertain, the decision policy 𝜋 𝜃 actively explores to acquire additional contact information. Exploratory actions and their ob-served outcomes are incorporated into the interaction history H𝑡 . Subsequent decisions therefore build on accumulated interaction evidence to progressively refine manipulation. 

## 4. Experiments 

We compare our method with visual, visuo-tactile, and world-action baselines on five UniVTAC tasks and conduct a tactile ablation on Insert Hole. 74.1. Experimental Setup Tasks and data. We evaluate on five contact-rich manipulation tasks from UniVTAC (Chen et al., 2026): Insert Hole , Lift Can , Grasp Classify , Lift Bottle , and Pull-out Key . These tasks cover geometric alignment, grasped-object manipulation, constrained motion, and tactile classification. For each task, we use 50 expert demonstrations, split at the episode level into 40 for knowl-edge construction and 10 for offline validation. From the construction subset, GPT-6-Astra analyzes contact changes and object motion before and after expert actions to distill the Task Skill for progress assessment and manipulation adjustment. It also extracts tactile references preserving bilateral contact distributions and local deformation patterns. Together, these com-ponents form the task context, which is fixed before formal evaluation and used across all test episodes of the same task. 

Baselines. We compare against four methods previously evaluated on UniVTAC. E0 (Zhan et al., 2026)and 𝜋 0.5 provide a vision-only baseline to assess manipulation under contact con-straints (Intelligence et al., 2025). ACT + UniVTAC Encoder uses pretrained visuo-tactile representations (Chen et al., 2026). N0-VTLA is a state-of-the-art policy with latent tactile predic-tion (Team et al., 2026c). N0-TWAM jointly models future vision, touch, and actions (Team et al., 2026b). 

Policy configuration. We use GPT-6-Astra as the decision policy 𝜋 𝜃 , with reasoning effort fixed to xhigh . The same model and reasoning configuration is used across all tasks. Each test episode starts an independent model session, with interaction history H𝑡 updated within the episode and reset between episodes. Observations and action execution follow Sections 3.3 and 3.4, respectively. 

Evaluation protocol. Our evaluation comprises 100 fixed test episodes per task, for a total of 500 episodes. Episode outcomes are determined by the environment’s native success criteria. We report per-task success rates and the average over the five tasks, assigning equal weight to each task. Terminated episodes that do not satisfy the success conditions are counted as failures.

## 5. Conclusion 

To enable general-purpose multimodal models to use contact feedback for action adjustment, we present GPT-as-Tactile-Policy, which combines expert-derived task guidance with online tactile observations. Expert experience provides task guidance for contact assessment and 11 manipulation adjustment. Online tactile feedback provides evidence for evaluating action outcomes and selecting subsequent adjustments. Without additional robotics-specific pretraining or per-task policy fine-tuning, our method achieves overall performance comparable to state-of-the-art policies across five UniVTAC tasks, matching or exceeding the compared methods on selected tasks. Tactile ablation and execution analysis further demonstrate the role of contact feedback in local probing, directional adjustment, and restoring progress after obstruction. These results establish explicit task knowledge combined with online interaction feedback as an effective route to efficient contact-rich manipulation.
