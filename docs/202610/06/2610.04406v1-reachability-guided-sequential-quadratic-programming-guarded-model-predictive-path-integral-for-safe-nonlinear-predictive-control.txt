# Reachability-Guided Sequential Quadratic Programming-Guarded Model Predictive Path Integral for Safe Nonlinear Predictive Control 

Alexandre Didier 1, Jason J. Choi 2, Namhoon Cho 3, Claire J. Tomlin 4 and Melanie N. Zeilinger 1

Abstract — Safe robot control often requires combining long-horizon performance optimization with hard state and input constraints, but existing approaches tend to address this trade-off partially. Sampling-based model predictive control (MPC) methods such as model predictive path integral (MPPI) are effective in handling nonlinear and nonconvex environments, yet their finite-sample rollouts and unconstrained weighted-average update can return an unsafe control. Deterministic nonlinear MPC can explicitly incorporate constraints, but its real-time safety and performance depends strongly on warm starts and local convergence. Hamilton–Jacobi reachability (HJR) provides rigorous safety certificates, but offline value-function computation remains practical only for reduced-order models. We propose ReSQ-MPPI, a reachability-informed generation– refinement architecture that combines these complementary strengths. An HJR value function computed offline for areduced-order model guides online MPPI sampling toward safe, promising trajectory candidates. The MPPI solution is then refined by a small number of sequential quadratic pro-gramming (SQP) iterations in a full-order MPC problem. A key observation is that the standard MPPI inference step is an unconstrained weighted least-squares problem; ReSQ-MPPI replaces it with a constrained MPC refinement that recovers the MPPI update when it is feasible and minimally modifies it otherwise. Simulations in cluttered navigation and autonomous racing environments demonstrate that ReSQ-MPPI improves safety and performance over standalone MPPI, MPC, and reachability-filtered sampling-based control baselines. 

I. I NTRODUCTION 

Model predictive control (MPC) has become a powerful framework for solving complex decision-making problems in robotics, including whole-body control and locomotion [1], mobile manipulation [2], and autonomous driving [3]. A key advantage of MPC is its ability to naturally incorporate safety requirements, such as collision avoidance and actuator limits, as constraints within the optimization problem, making it well suited for safety-critical robotic applications. However, one of the crucial challenges with MPC for systems with complex dynamics is the complexity of nonlinear optimiza-tion that it entails inherently to be solved online. 

Optimization-based methods , particularly those based on sequential quadratic programming (SQP), have emerged as promising approaches for efficiently solving nonlinear MPC problems [4], [5]. However, these methods remain suscepti-ble to infeasibility and rely heavily on a good initialization

When the solver fails to find a feasible solution within the limited number of optimization iterations in real-time, the resulting control input may violate safety constraints and compromise system safety. On the other hand, sampling-based MPC has emerged as an effective framework for approximately solving nonlin-ear optimization problems. Model predictive path integral (MPPI) and other variational inference MPC frameworks pose optimal control as a variational inference of the dis-tribution of optimal control trajectories [6]. While these methods can effectively handle complex nonlinear dynamics and constraints within the finite prediction horizon, they lack principled mechanisms for ensuring long-term safety, such as recursive feasibility guarantees. Furthermore, as the system dimensionality increases and the feasible region becomes increasingly complex and nonconvex, a finite set of sampled trajectories may fail to contain any feasible solution. Finally, another key limitation of sampling-based MPC for safety is that, although individual sampled trajectories may each satisfy the safety constraints, the final control solution obtained by aggregating these samples can still violate safety during the inference step [7]. Another school of control methods for safety assurance is to employ safety certificate functions [8], typically computed offline. These methods verify a control invariant set and sub-sequently synthesize an online feedback controller that guar-antees the system remains within this set, thereby satisfying the prescribed safety constraints. Hamilton-Jacobi reachabil-ity (HJR) [9] and control barrier functions (CBFs) [10] are prominent methodologies for designing safety certificates. However, both approaches suffer from poor scalability with respect to system dimensionality and the complexity of the dynamics, making them most suitable for reduced-order models. For high-dimensional, full-order robotic systems, safety is often achieved through robust formulations and layered control architectures [11]; however, this approach inherently entails additional conservatism in the resulting closed-loop system due to margins addressing model errors. Motivated by the limitations of existing approaches, this work proposes a new safe MPC framework, ReSQ-MPPI, that combines three key components: (1) an offline-computed HJR safety certificate based on a reduced-order model, (2) an MPPI that generates an initial solution candidate by sampling trajectories guided by the safety certificate, and (3) a small number of SQP iterations that refine the candidate solution to satisfy the constraints of the full-order system dynamics. arXiv:2610.04406v1 [cs.RO] 3 Oct 2026 Fig. 1. Our key observation is that the inference step in MPPI, which aggregates sampled control sequences into a single control solution, is an unconstrained weighted least-squares optimization. Consequently, even if all sampled trajectories individually satisfy the safety constraints, the aggregated solution is not guaranteed to remain safe. Previous works [12], [7] address this issue by introducing a post-processing safety filter that projects the aggregated control onto the safe set. In contrast, our key idea is to unify control aggregation and safety enforcement into a single optimization problem. Specifically, we formulate the inference step as a constrained 

weighted least-squares problem within the SQP framework of nonlinear MPC. Below, we detail how the proposed framework overcomes the individual limitations of existing SQP-based MPC, MPPI, and safety certificate methods. 

ReSQ-MPPI vs. SQP & Sampling-based MPC: HJR-guided MPPI generates an initial solution that closely ap-proximates the long-horizon optimal solution while account-ing for safety. This warm start substantially improves the convergence and robustness of SQP. Unlike conventional MPPI, the proposed SQP formulation incorporates safety constraints directly into the control aggregation step, ensur-ing that the final control solution satisfies the desired safety requirements. Furthermore, the control sequence refined by SQP is used as the nominal input sequence for the MPPI at the next sampling instant, establishing a bidirectional coupling between MPPI sampling and SQP refinement. This interconnection significantly reduces the likelihood that ei-ther MPPI fails to sample a feasible trajectory or SQP fails to converge to a feasible solution within the limited number of optimization iterations. 

ReSQ-MPPI vs. HJR: While conventional numerical HJR cannot be applied to higher-dimensional systems without neural network approximations, MPPI and MPC can impose hard safety constraints based on full-order dynamics. 

A. Related Works a) Predictive Control for Safety Assurance: MPC has long been regarded as one of the principal frameworks for safety due to its ability to explicitly incorporate state and input constraints into the online optimization. In classi-cal MPC, safety is typically established through recursive feasibility , ensuring that the optimization problem remains feasible at every sampling time by designing terminal con-straint sets that are control invariant. However, constructing such terminal sets is often challenging, particularly for complex nonlinear systems and safety constraints. Moreover, the resulting nonlinear optimization can be computationally demanding and may fail to converge to a feasible solution in real-time. Predictive safety filters (PSF) [13] have extended MPC to a supervisory safety layer that minimally modifies a nominal controller while maintaining safety through recur-sive feasibility. More recently, sampling-based MPC methods such as MPPI have emerged as alternatives for optimization-based MPC and PSF for safety [7], [14]. Nevertheless, because these methods rely on a finite set of sampled trajectories, they generally lack formal recursive feasibility guarantees [12]. Consequently, when feasible trajectories are not sampled or the final aggregated control input does not preserve feasibility, long-term safety is not ensured. 

b) Safety Certificates + Sampling-based MPC: To im-prove the safety of sampling-based MPC, various approaches are proposed which integrate safety certificates into the trajectory sampling process. The methods in [15], [12], [16] integrate CBFs with MPPI; however, the employed CBFs are typically simple distance-based functions that do not certify control invariance. The work of [7] integrates HJR least-restrictive safety filtering with MPPI. However, the approach is limited to low-dimensional system models because of the poor scalability of HJR. To improve scalability, recent methods have proposed usage of learning-based certificates, including neural CBFs [17] and neural approximations of HJR value functions [14]. While these approaches can be applied to higher-dimensional systems, formally verifying the safety guarantees of learned certificates remains an open challenge [18]. More recently, [19] uses CBFs to encourage safety during the training of a generative model that produces the proposal distribution for MPPI.

One way to improve the safety of MPPI is to bias the trajectory sampling process using safety certificates. Existing approaches achieve this by either augmenting the trajectory cost in (4a) with barrier or value-function penalties, or by rejecting unsafe samples through safety filtering [12], [7]. Neither of which guarantees closed-loop constraint satis-faction as discussed in [7]; thus, we leverage the HJR-based safety filtering to generate safe trajectory samples as proposed in [7], named Dualguard-MPPI . Specifically, for each sampled input sequence ujk, at time step i, if the state at the next time step xji+1 |k is not within S, we override uji|k

with the safe optimal control input us(xji|k), making sure that the trajectory stays within the maximal invariant set. The main limitation of this approach is the poor scalability of the computation of the HJR value function V . Due to the curse of dimensionality [9], we can only use a reduced-order model of f for its computation, which results in errors in the computed maximal safe set S and the safe controller us.Thus, the safety for the full-order system is compromised with this approach itself, as we will see in our results.

METHOD In this section, we introduce our method, Reachability-guided SQP-guarded model predictive path integral (ReSQ-MPPI) as illustrated in Figure 1. The aim is to augment HJR-guided MPPI as described in Section II-D with constraint satisfaction guarantees. We achieve this through a best of both worlds approach, where MPPI allows escaping local optima through its sampling-based solution approach, the potentially unsafe solution of which we then refine using SQP to achieve a high performance safe control framework for non-convex optimal control problems. We propose three interconnections between MPPI and MPC through SQP: (A) recovering the MPPI solution through a reference tracking MPC formulation, (B) using the MPPI solution as a warm-start for SQP iterations, and (C) lastly feeding back the converged SQP solution as the nominal input sequence in MPPI at the next sampling time.

B. MPPI as Warmstart for SQP 

While the MPPI update rule can be recovered through a predictive optimization problem as shown in Section III-A, the suboptimality of the MPPI solution grows second-order with the input sampling uncertainty [24]. Thereby, the MPPI solution may lose accuracy if the samples are under large noise perturbations, or the samples are too sparse in high-dimensional state space. In order to remedy these effects, we propose refining the MPPI solution by using it as the initial iterate in a sequential quadratic formulation. By minimizing the same objective in MPPI as in the following SQP, the MPPI solution can be guided towards the closest local KKT point. The tracking cost in (9) can then additionally be used as a regularizer to ensure local optimization of the objective in environments with multiple local optima such as, e.g., the cluttered environment simulation in Section IV. 

Remark 4: In [25], it is shown that the MPPI update corre-sponds to a preconditioned gradient step on the performance objective. By using the MPPI solution as a warmstart to an SQP, we apply a gradient step followed by SQP updates (which correspond to Gauss-Newton steps in the uncon-strained setting based on the Hessian approximation) with respect to the control objective. Investigating the convergence properties of this approach is an interesting research direction for future work. 

C. SQP Solution as MPPI Nominal Sequence 

The MPPI update rule in (5) requires a nominal input sequence to which the weighted average of the randomly sampled input trajectories is added to. As this nominal input sequence biases the distribution of random samples for exploration, this nominal sequence can play a large role in the closed-loop performance of MPPI. The third element of our approach is to use the time-shifted optimal solution of the SQP as the nominal solution of MPPI at the next time step. This interaction, together with the first two elements, closes the loop between MPPI and SQP. CONCLUSIONS We propose ReSQ-MPPI, a control framework leveraging Hamilton-Jacobi reachability for safe reduced-order model-based sample generation in MPPI, whose optimal control sequence is enhanced for safety and performance through sequential quadratic programming iterations. We leverage the MPPI solution as a reference in a tracking MPC formulation and as a warmstart for the SQP iterations, feeding back the optimal SQP solution as the nominal MPPI input. We show that the proposed approach can alleviate local optimality issues in nonlinear MPC formulations as well as improve the safety and performance of MPPI in extensive simulation studies, including navigation in a cluttered environment as well as racing with static obstacles. The results are addition-ally validated in a real-time vehicle simulation.
