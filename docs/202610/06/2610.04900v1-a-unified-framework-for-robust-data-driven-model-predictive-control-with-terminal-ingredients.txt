# A Unified Framework for Robust Data-Driven Model Predictive Control with Terminal Ingredients 

Zhaohua Yang, Nan Bai, Pengyu Wang, and Ling Shi, Fellow, IEEE 

Abstract —This paper proposes a unified framework for ro-bust data-driven model predictive control (MPC) with terminal ingredients for unknown linear time-invariant systems. Based on Willems’ fundamental lemma, a single offline input-output trajectory is used as an implicit model, while online implemen-tation only requires past input-output measurements. Bounded measurement noise is considered in both offline and online output data. The framework consists of an offline stage, where robust terminal ingredients are designed directly from noisy data, and an online stage, where these ingredients are incorporated into a one-step MPC scheme. Recursive feasibility of the proposed framework and practical exponential stability of the closed-loop system are established. Numerical results illustrate the perfor-mance tuning capability of the terminal set, with particularly pronounced benefits for short prediction horizons, and show that the proposed framework achieves performance comparable to data-driven MPC without terminal ingredients while requiring a reduced prediction horizon and less online computation. 

Index Terms —Data-driven control, model predictive control, robust control. 

I. I NTRODUCTION 

Model predictive control (MPC) has become one of the most successful advanced control methodologies due to its ability to explicitly handle system constraints while achiev-ing desirable closed-loop performance [1, 2]. Over the past decades, a comprehensive theoretical foundation has been developed for MPC, including recursive feasibility, closed-loop stability, constraint satisfaction, and performance guar-antees [1, 2]. In particular, terminal ingredients have been extensively investigated to establish closed-loop stability for constrained systems [3], while robust MPC techniques have been developed to maintain stability and constraint satisfaction in the presence of disturbances and model uncertainties [4]. These theoretical developments, together with advances in numerical optimization, have made MPC a well-established framework for the control of constrained dynamical systems. Despite its theoretical maturity and practical success, con-ventional MPC fundamentally relies on an explicit model of the controlled system to predict its future behavior. In practical applications, however, constructing an accurate model can be difficult, time-consuming, or even impractical. This has moti-vated the development of data-driven control approaches that aim to design controllers directly from measured data, thereby bypassing system identification. This behavioral characterization provides an appealing alternative to model-based prediction and has become a key tool for the development of data-driven control methods [6–9]. Based on behavioral representations of system trajectories, an early data-driven predictive control scheme was proposed by Yang and Li [10], where a reduced Hankel matrix con-structed from input-output data was employed for predic-tion and controller design. Coulson et al. subsequently pro-posed the data-enabled predictive control (DeePC) framework [11], which directly optimizes over input-output trajectories parametrized by measured data. However, closed-loop proper-ties such as recursive feasibility and stability were not estab-lished in these early data-driven predictive control schemes. To address this issue, Berberich et al. [12] incorporated terminal equality constraints into data-driven MPC and established recursive feasibility and exponential stability in the noise-free case, as well as practical exponential stability under bounded output measurement noise. The design of more gen-eral terminal ingredients was later investigated in [13], where a terminal cost and a terminal set were constructed directly from noise-free input-output data to guarantee exponential stability. More recently, Bongard et al. [14] established stability and robustness guarantees for a data-driven MPC scheme without terminal ingredients, where closed-loop stability is ensured by choosing a sufficiently long prediction horizon. Beyond these fundamental developments, data-driven MPC has also been extended to specialized settings, including changing setpoint tracking, resilient control, and event-triggered control [15– 17]. A recent overview of systems-theoretic guarantees and developments in data-driven MPC can be found in [18]. Despite these advances, a gap remains between robustness analysis and terminal ingredient design. In particular, systems-theoretic guarantees for robust data-driven MPC with gen-eral terminal ingredients have not been established. Such an analysis is particularly important since MPC with and with-out terminal ingredients represent two classical stabilization philosophies. The former establishes stability by incorporating suitable terminal costs and terminal sets, whereas the latter relies on a sufficiently long prediction horizon, as pursued in [14]. In this sense, a robustness analysis of data-driven MPC with general terminal ingredients provides a complementary counterpart to the terminal-free framework in [14]. Establishing such guarantees, however, introduces several nontrivial challenges. First, suitable terminal ingredients need to be designed directly from noisy data while remaining arXiv:2610.04900v1 [eess.SY] 4 Oct 2026 2

valid for the unknown underlying system. Although [13] provides a data-driven design of terminal ingredients, its main theoretical development assumes noise-free measurements, while the noisy-data extension discussed therein relies on an additional nontrivial condition on the measurement noise. Second, recursive feasibility requires constructing a feasible solution at the next time step from the current optimal so-lution. Such a construction needs to be carefully coordinated with the properties of the terminal ingredients. Finally, the resulting candidate solution must lead to a suitable Lyapunov decrease condition. While this step follows relatively standard arguments in the noise-free case, measurement noise causes discrepancies between predicted and actual trajectories and substantially complicates the closed-loop stability analysis. Motivated by these observations, this paper develops a robust data-driven MPC framework with terminal ingredients and establishes rigorous closed-loop guarantees. The main contributions are summarized as follows. 1) We develop a unified robust data-driven MPC framework with general terminal ingredients. The proposed scheme consists of two stages. Offline, a terminal controller, a terminal cost, and a terminal set are designed directly from noisy input-output data. Online, these terminal in-gredients are incorporated into a regularized data-driven MPC problem, which is implemented in a standard one-step fashion (Theorem 1 and Algorithm 1). 2) We propose a new candidate solution construction for the robustness analysis of data-driven MPC. Unlike existing constructions [12, 14], both the optimal in-put and output trajectories are simultaneously shifted, after which the terminal controller is used to append the terminal input-output pair and a slack variable is introduced to absorb the mismatch induced by noisy data. Based on this construction, we establish recursive feasibility under one-step implementation and prove practical exponential stability of the resulting closed-loop system (Proposition 2 and Theorem 2). 3) Numerical studies on an inverted pendulum model il-lustrate three features of the proposed framework. First, the terminal set level provides a tuning mechanism for balancing convergence speed and closed-loop perfor-mance. Second, compared with terminal equality MPC, the benefit of the terminal set is particularly obvious for short prediction horizons, whereas the two schemes exhibit similar behavior as the prediction horizon in-creases. Third, compared with data-driven MPC without terminal ingredients [14], the proposed scheme achieves comparable closed-loop performance with a shorter pre-diction horizon, thereby trading additional offline termi-nal design for reduced online computational effort. We organize this paper as follows. Section II introduces the preliminaries and formulates the control problem. In Section III, we present the proposed robust data-driven MPC scheme, develop a data-driven design of robust terminal ingre-dients, establish recursive feasibility and practical exponential stability, and discuss its relation to existing data-driven MPC schemes. Section IV provides numerical simulations illustrat-ing the influence of the terminal set size and prediction horizon and comparing the proposed scheme with data-driven MPC without terminal ingredients. Section V concludes the paper.

A. System Description 

In this paper, we consider a discrete-time LTI system: 

xk+1 = Ax k + Bu k,yk = Cx k + Du k, (1) where xk ∈ Rnx , uk ∈ Rnu , and yk ∈ Rny denote the system state, control input, and system output at time step k,respectively. We make the standard assumption that (A, B ) is controllable and (A, C ) is observable. Throughout this paper, we assume that the system matrices A, B, C, and D are unknown, but we can access the noise-free control input uk

Our goal is to design a data-driven controller that stabilizes a prescribed equilibrium while satisfying input constraints. The formal problem formulation will be provided in Section II-D.

In model-based MPC, the system dynamics are explicitly used to characterize the set of admissible input-output tra-jectories. H. Discussion and Comparison with Existing Data-Driven MPC Schemes 

The proposed formulation unifies existing terminal-constraint data-driven MPC schemes. Specifically, the terminal equality formulation in [12] is recovered when r = 0 , whereas the noise-free terminal set formulation in [13] is recovered when ¯ε = 0 . Beyond this theoretical unification, the intro-duction of a terminal set also provides additional flexibility in closed-loop performance. Compared with the terminal equality constraints adopted in [12, 16], a nonzero terminal set level 

r relaxes the terminal requirement and can avoid overly 8

aggressive control actions, thereby yielding smoother control inputs and improved closed-loop performance. Hence, r can be interpreted as a performance-tuning parameter that provides a trade-off between the restrictiveness of the terminal condition and the resulting closed-loop behavior. This effect will be demonstrated numerically in Section IV-A. Second, our formulation avoids the nonconvex constraint 

∥σ(t)∥∞ ≤ ¯ε(1 + ∥α(t)∥1) employed in [12] for the the-oretical analysis. Instead, the noise-dependent regularization term λσ 

> ¯εβσ

∥¯σ(t)∥2 increasingly penalizes the slack variable as 

¯ε decreases and thus drives ¯σ(t) toward zero in the small-noise case. The same regularization mechanism could also be incorporated into [12] without changing its stability argument. Finally, compared with data-driven MPC without terminal ingredients [14], our framework does not rely on a sufficiently long prediction horizon to establish closed-loop stability and instead only requires the lower bound L ≥ l + nx. This allows a substantially shorter prediction horizon to be employed and can therefore reduce the online computational burden. The price is an additional offline step for designing the terminal ingredients. Such a trade-off between offline and online com-putation is particularly attractive in networked and resource-constrained control systems, where substantial computational resources may be available offline while online computation is subject to real-time limitations. C. Comparison with MPC Without Terminal Ingredients

This paper develops a unified robust data-driven MPC framework with terminal ingredients for unknown LTI sys-tems under noise-free input and noisy output measurements. Robust terminal ingredients are designed directly from offline data, and a one-step MPC implementation is established. The proposed scheme guarantees recursive feasibility and practical exponential stability under suitable conditions. Numerical re-sults demonstrate that the terminal set level provides a useful trade-off between convergence speed and closed-loop perfor-mance. In particular, the proposed terminal set formulation is advantageous for short prediction horizons and can achieve performance comparable to long-horizon terminal-free MPC with reduced online computational effort.
