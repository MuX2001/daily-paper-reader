## Abstract 

Policy mirror descent (PMD) has a mature finite-time theory in discounted Markov decision processes (MDPs), but less is known in the average-reward setting, a more natural objective for many control applications. We give a finite-time, finite-sample analysis of PMD in ergodic average-reward MDPs built around a single master recursion that governs convergence for any critic, without external regularization. Its specializations yield linear rates for exact, inexact-tabular, and linear function approximation (LFA) updates, with a superlinear regime for exact PMD. We complement these convergence results with end-to-end sample complexities of order t3mix /ε 2 in both tabular ( |S||A| -dependent) and LFA ( d-dependent) settings. Our LFA sample complexity sharpens the prior best t5mix mixing dependence to 

t3mix , and matching information-theoretic lower bounds establish that the critic’s 

t3mix /ε 2 sample complexity is unimprovable in both settings. 

## 1 Introduction 

Policy optimization, the process of updating an agent’s decision rule to maximize expected long-term reward, is a workhorse of reinforcement learning applications [e.g., 1, 41]. It underlies classical policy-gradient [36] and natural-gradient methods [15] as well as widely used algorithms such as TRPO [30] and PPO [31]. A large and rapidly growing theory explains the behavior of these methods in discounted Markov decision processes (MDPs), including finite-time finite-sample guarantees [2, 17, 32], function approximation extensions [2, 3, 7, 40], and linear convergence results for policy mirror descent (PMD) and its natural-gradient instantiations [8, 14, 17, 38, 42]. However, many continuing-control applications are more naturally modeled by the average-reward criterion, where performance is determined by the long-run steady-state behavior rather than geometrically discounted rewards. This objective appears throughout ergodic control and operations research, including resource allocation, scheduling, queueing, and communication networks [6, 12, 16, 22, 26, 28, 33]. To make the distinction between discounted and average-reward MDPs concrete, let ck denote the cost incurred by a policy at time k. First, discounted PMD analyses exploit the initial state dis-tribution to lower bound state visitation uniformly. In average reward, initial conditions are transient, so controlling the stationary distribution shift becomes a new challenge. Second, discounting yields a finite effective horizon Hγ := 1 /(1 − γ) and admits unbiased value estimates via geometric stopping times. In average reward, all times are weighted equally, so there is no such stopping distribution, and truncating the horizon introduces a bias that must be controlled through mixing to stationarity.

Contributions. In this paper, we develop a finite-time, finite-sample theory of policy mirror descent (PMD) in average-reward Markov decision processes (MDPs): • Master recursion. We establish a master recursion (Lemma 3.7) that governs PMD for any critic, from which all downstream results follow as specializations. • Linear and asymptotic convergence. We prove linear convergence for exact, inexact-tabular, and linear-function-approximation updates (Corollary 4.3; Theorems 4.2, 5.6). For the tabular case we further sharpen prior worst-case rates with an asymptotic-rate analysis, and our non-tabular result is, to our knowledge, the first to converge linearly to the optimum up to critic error. • End-to-end sample complexity. We give end-to-end sample complexities for the tabular and non-tabular settings (Theorems 4.8, 5.11), sharpening the best known non-tabular mixing-time dependence from t5mix to t3mix and strengthening tabular guarantees from in-expectation bounds on a random iterate to high-probability bounds on the last iterate. The non-tabular guarantee needs only a single trajectory, a strictly weaker oracle than the generative model the tabular guarantee uses. • Matching lower bounds. We establish information-theoretic lower bounds via Le Cam and Assouad reductions (Propositions 4.7, 5.9), showing that both the mixing-time and accuracy dependence of the critic’s sample complexity are unimprovable. 

## 2 Related work 

Recent work on policy optimization for average-reward MDPs spans several partial results. Sun et al. [34] prove linear convergence of robust PMD for average-reward MDPs under squared Euclidean geometry; specialized to zero robustness radius, this partially overlaps with our exact-update special-ization (Theorem 4.2 with εQ = 0 ) but uses a stricter step-size growth condition, and does not address general Bregman geometry, inexact critics, function approximation, or sample complexity. Li et al. [21] give stochastic first-order methods with O(1 /√T ) unregularized rates and linear convergence up to an eO(1 /T ) stochastic term only under strongly convex regularization ; their non-tabular sample complexity on a single trajectory (their Corollary 3) has t5mix mixing dependence. Kumar et al. [16], Murthy and Srikant [23] establish O(1 /T ) rates for projected policy gradient and for natural policy gradient (NPG) and mirror-descent-like methods, Grosof et al. [13] obtain an O(1 /√T ) rate for NPG on countably-infinite-state queueing systems, and Murthy et al. [24] develop performance bounds for approximate policy iteration; their bound for a fixed-step mirror-descent update keeps an error floor that does not vanish with the critic error. Bai et al. [5], Ganesh et al. [10, 11] study general-parameterized average-reward policy gradient, achieving polynomial convergence and regret rates. Concurrent work Lee and Ryu [19] extends policy gradient to multichain MDPs via α-clipped PMD, with sample complexity eO(ε−6)1; this setting-generalization line is complementary to our sharper eO(ε−2) analysis in the ergodic case. Against this backdrop, we provide a unified PMD analysis under general Bregman geometry spanning exact, inexact-tabular, and linear-function-approximation updates. We improve on prior tabular results along multiple axes (general Bregman geometry, inexact critics, asymptotic-rate analysis, high-probability last-iterate sample complexity) and, to our knowledge, establish the first linear convergence of non-tabular average-reward PMD to the optimum up to critic error, sharpening the prior best t5mix mixing dependence to t3mix . Matching information-theoretic lower bounds on the critic’s sample complexity (Propositions 4.7, 5.9) establish tightness in both settings. On the step size schedule. A natural concern is that aggressive step sizes would amplify critic errors in the PI limit. But step size and critic error decouple in PMD: unlike stochastic gradient descent (SGD) — where each step injects noise scaled by ηt — here the critic’s error is fixed, and ηt only controls how aggressively each update commits to that (possibly biased) direction. Larger ηt speeds up convergence but leaves the error floor unchanged; Lemma 3.7 formalizes this, and Figure 4(a) (Appendix F.3) verifies it empirically. With a constant step size, the one-step inequality behind Lemma 3.7 gives an O(1 /T ) rate in expectation to the same error floor for a uniformly random iterate; with an exact critic, the same holds for the last iterate (Appendix B.7). This matches the 

O(1 /T ) rates of Kumar et al. [16] and Murthy and Srikant [23]. Growing step sizes are what make the rate linear (Lemma 3.7(i)). Numerical overflow is also mild: with T = eΘ( ¯ χ log(1 /ε )) iterations, the growth condition gives ηT = poly(1 /ε ). Finally, neither running the update (2) nor attaining the asymptotic rate of Lemma 3.7(ii) requires knowing χ or ¯χ. 5.2 Sample complexity 

We now show how to satisfy Assumption 5.3 with finite samples, i.e., how to drive εstat arbitrarily small, by minimizing the objective in (12) with SGD on noisy samples. Average reward introduces two obstacles, and our choices on both axes differ from the nearest prior work. 

Obstacle 1: accurate critics for a linear-rate actor. Our actor (Thm. 5.6) converges linearly, in eO( ¯ χ) steps with growing step sizes, whereas the sublinear actor of Li et al. [21, Cor. 3] needs 

eO(κ2/ε 2) steps, where κ2 bounds the second moment of their critic outputs and is eO(t2mix ) up to conditioning and approximation terms. Their many small steps average out the critic noise, so each critic call only needs to control bias, which their exploratory variance-reduced temporal difference (EVRTD) critic does at a cost of eO(t3mix ) transitions, up to conditioning factors, on a single trajectory, adding up to t5mix . To retain our linear rate with few, large actor steps, we require a critic with small MSE at each step, E∥wt − w⋆t ∥2Σt ≤ ε2stat , and perform the averaging inside the critic. We achieve this through averaged SGD [4, Thm. 1] on rollout labels collected along a single trajectory. The labels’ eO(t2mix ) second moment plays the role of κ2 for our SGD steps, but each label costs only 

eO(tmix ) transitions instead of an EVRTD critic call, which gives t3mix rather than t5mix .

Obstacle 2: unbiased samples without a discount factor. The averaged SGD of Obstacle 1 needs independent pairs (s, a ) ∼ νt with unbiased labels of Qt(s, a ). Discounted analyses [40] obtain both from geometric stopping, which samples state–action pairs exactly from the discounted occupancy and yields unbiased Q-estimates. Average reward weights all times equally, so it has no such discount-induced stopping distribution. Instead, we approximate both through mixing. We run the policy along a single trajectory that alternates between burn-ins of m transitions (distinct from τ of §4.2) and rollouts of H transitions. For the pairs, we take (sk, a k) at the end of the 

k-th burn-in. By (11), each pair’s conditional distribution given the past is close to νt; a coupling argument lets averaged SGD treat the pairs as independent, up to the burn-in error (App. D). ## 6 Conclusion 

We developed a finite-time, finite-sample theory of policy mirror descent for ergodic average-reward MDPs around a master recursion (Lemma 3.7) whose two-part structure separates a transient ¯χ-regime from an asymptotic χ-regime, the latter empirically reached after a brief warm-up. The tabular and LFA specializations sit at opposite ends of a methodological trade-off: tabular gives high-probability t3mix rates under a generative model with |S||A| dependence (Theorem 4.8); LFA gives |S||A| -free t3mix rates on a single trajectory but in expectation (Theorem 5.11). Matching lower bounds (Propositions 4.7, 5.9) establish that the critic’s t3mix /ε 2 dependence is unimprovable in both settings. Interesting future directions include high-probability LFA, the multichain extension, and regret guarantees. 10 Acknowledgments and Disclosure of Funding

Extension to unichain MDPs. Assumption 3.1 can be weakened to the unichain condition (each induced kernel Pπ has a unique recurrent class) while retaining aperiodicity, at the cost of additional constants, and the mechanism is visible in our analysis. For the convergence results , whenever the iterates remain interior (automatic under KL, where the update is multiplicative), each path mismatch 

χt stays finite. What is lost is the a priori uniform bound χt ≤ d−1 of Lemma 3.2, so a uniform path bound ¯χ must be imposed or verified rather than inherited; once it is supplied, the recursion of Lemma 3.7 applies verbatim. For the sample bounds , Lemma 4.4 is proved under Assumption 3.1, so in the unichain setting uniform mixing is instead imposed as an assumption, as in the mixing unichain setting of Li et al. [21]. With tmix then also absorbing the hitting time of transient states, the sample bounds are stated exactly as before. In both halves the change is in constants, not in the form of the results. By contrast, the multichain case (some induced kernel Pπ has more than one recurrent class) requires additional analysis, as in Lee and Ryu [19].
