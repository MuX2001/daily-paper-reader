Abstract 

Robotic manipulators operating over long durations often experience uneven joint degra-dation, which causes the weakest actuator to fail prematurely, leads to unplanned downtime, and results in significant operational losses. Traditional motion-planning algorithms do not account for joint health conditions and therefore tend to exacerbate this imbalance during extended operation. To address this challenge, this study introduces the RUL-aware RRT*, a motion-planning method that incorporates joint remaining useful life information into the planning process and adaptively adjusts joint usage in response to evolving health condi-tions. The method is evaluated across three representative scenarios, namely the Full Health Scenario (FHS), the Heterogeneous Degradation Scenario (HDS), and the Local Degradation Scenario (LDS). The results show that the RUL-aware RRT* effectively suppresses degra-dation imbalance, delays the emergence of bottleneck failures, and improves the long-term reliability of the robotic system during extended operation. These findings demonstrate that integrating health feedback into motion planning provides a practical and robust pathway for enhancing the durability and operational resilience of manipulators subject to continuous wear. 

Keywords: Remaining Useful Life (RUL); motion planning; RUL-aware RRT*; adaptive weight-ing; industrial robotic manipulator. 

# 1 Introduction 

In modern manufacturing, industrial robots have become essential equipment for achieving au-tomated production and flexible manufacturing(Rüßmann et al., 2015). As the most critical component of industrial robots, multi-degree-of-freedom robotic arms rely on multiple joint mo-tors that must operate in close coordination to perform complex, continuous, and high-precision tasks (Fan et al., 2019). However, in practical operation, motion planning is typically optimized for geometric efficiency or energy performance (Noreen et al., 2016; Feng et al., 2024), while differences in joint health conditions and the resulting uneven motion usage across actuators are often overlooked. This leads to uneven motor utilization (Raviola et al., 2021), where certain joints may experience accelerated degradation due to high workloads or frequent movements, eventually becoming the weakest link of the system (Kot et al., 2021). Once such a motor fails, the entire robot must be shut down, resulting in unplanned downtime and seriously affecting production continuity and equipment reliability (Lei et al., 2025; Mahyari and Locker, 2018; Raviola et al., 2021). This issue, in which local degradation triggers overall system failure, has become one of the key challenges in the reliability management of industrial robots. To address such issues, extensive research has been conducted in recent years in both academia and industry within the fields of condition monitoring and predictive maintenance

By leveraging remaining useful life (RUL) prediction and digital twin technologies, researchers are able to monitor equipment health in real time, predict the degradation trends of critical components, and make informed maintenance decisions accordingly (Mourtzis et al., 2023; Borgi et al., 2017). These approaches have significantly enhanced the intelligence of maintenance man-agement, shifting it from a reactive mode to a proactive strategy (Molęda et al., 2023). However, most existing studies remain at the monitoring and scheduling layers, where the focus lies in sensing system health and determining maintenance timing (Kumar et al., 2023). Their outputs, such as health indicators, degradation trajectories, or maintenance schedules, are seldom inte-grated into the motion-planning layer, preventing health information from directly influencing path generation or motion decisions (Schacht-Rodríguez et al., 2018). In other words, although health information is effectively perceived, it is rarely incorporated into the robot’s path gen-eration and motion decision-making processes, thereby limiting the realization of a closed-loop predictive maintenance framework. On the other hand, sampling-based motion planning algorithms, such as RRT, RRT*, and PRM, provide a flexible framework for incorporating multidimensional constraints into the plan-ning process (Kingston et al., 2018; Zhang et al., 2025; Luo et al., 2023). In recent years, various studies have introduced factors such as energy and consumption (Alam et al., 2023), collision risk (Liu et al., 2023), safety distance (Mainprice et al., 2011), battery endurance (Chitre and Sinha, 2023) into the cost functions of these algorithms, thereby achieving a comprehensive op-timization of efficiency, safety, and reliability. These works demonstrate that path planning can be applied not only for geometric feasibility analysis but also holds great potential for integrating health-related factors. However, existing research has not yet systematically incorporated the uneven degradation conditions across multiple motors and their imbalanced RUL distribution into planning models, leaving robotic systems lacking health awareness at the path-planning level. Based on the above analysis, three major research gaps can be identified. First, traditional path-planning algorithms often lead to uneven motor degradation, as they optimize only ge-ometric or efficiency-related objectives without considering motor health conditions. Second, predictive maintenance techniques largely remain at the monitoring and scheduling levels, with-out direct integration into the motion-planning process. Third, existing path-planning studies mainly focus on energy efficiency or collision avoidance, while overlooking the uneven degrada-tion conditions across multiple motors and the resulting lifetime bottleneck. To address these challenges, this study proposes an RUL-aware RRT* motion-planning algo-rithm that aims to redistribute joint-level motion usage, enhance health awareness, and mitigate lifetime bottlenecks. The proposed algorithm is developed on the basis of the traditional RRT* framework (Karaman and Frazzoli, 2011), whose original cost function considers only the path length, while RUL-aware RRT* extends it by introducing an RUL-aware cost function that incorporates joint health information into the path-cost formulation, thereby enabling deep in-tegration of health awareness into the motion-planning process. In addition, two key mechanisms are designed within the framework: (1) an Online RUL Update Mechanism,which dynamically updates the current remaining useful life (RUL) of each joint before each motion-planning pro-cess; and (2) an RUL-driven Adaptive Joint Weighting Mechanism, which adaptively balances motor loads under different degradation conditions to achieve health-aware motion planning. In multi-scenario experiments conducted on a UR5 six-degree-of-freedom robotic arm, includ-ing the Full Health Scenario, the Heterogeneous Degradation Scenario, and the Local Degrada-tion Scenario, the results demonstrate that the proposed method achieves balanced degradation, enhances health-awareness, and extends the first-replacement time of critical motors, thereby realizing planable predictive maintenance. This study provides a new perspective for achieving health awareness and lifetime balancing at the task-execution level of industrial robots, and lays the foundation for future research on reliability-aware motion planning. 2Figure 1: Conventional path planning with RRT* (open-loop framework) The implementation and experimental data are available at https://github.com/HelpLee/ MotionPlanning_RULRRTstar .

# 2 Background and Preliminaries 

Sampling-based motion planning algorithms have been widely adopted in robotic systems due to their ability to handle high-dimensional configuration spaces without requiring explicit geo-metric representations of obstacles. Among these methods, the Rapidly-exploring Random Tree (RRT) algorithm and its optimal variant RRT* have become the most prominent frameworks for generating feasible and near-optimal trajectories efficiently. RRT* is particularly suitable for robotic manipulators, as it incrementally constructs a connected tree in the configuration space while refining the solution toward the global optimum through local rewiring. Its asymp-totic optimality and probabilistic completeness make it a robust foundation for motion planning under complex kinematic constraints. We build upon the standard RRT* algorithm, a sampling-based motion-planning method that incrementally constructs a connected tree G = ( V, E ) in the configuration space X ⊂ RJ .The planning problem is formulated as finding a collision-free path within the free configuration space Xfree ⊂ X that connects a given start configuration xinit ∈ X free to a goal region Xgoal ⊂Xfree . Starting from xinit , RRT* repeatedly samples random states, extends the tree toward these samples, and rewires the connections to minimize the cumulative path cost while ensuring that the tree eventually reaches Xgoal . Through this iterative process, the algorithm converges asymptotically to an optimal solution while maintaining probabilistic completeness. The overall procedure is summarized in Algorithm 1, and more implementation details can be found in the original work by Karaman and Frazzoli (Karaman and Frazzoli, 2011). # 3 Methodology 

This section introduces the overall structure of the proposed RUL-aware motion planning frame-work, which extends the standard RRT* algorithm by incorporating joint health information into the planning and feedback process, forming a closed-loop architecture. 

3.1 Overview of the RUL-aware Motion Planning Framework 

Figure 2 provides an overview of the proposed closed-loop framework for path planning with RUL-aware RRT*. Instead of treating motion planning as a purely geometric problem, we embed the planner into a health-aware loop that repeatedly updates the joint Remaining Useful Life (RUL) and adapts the planning cost accordingly. For each task, the robot starts from a given pair of start and goal configurations and computes a collision-free path while explicitly considering the degradation state of each joint. The overall process establishes a closed-loop interaction between planning, execution, and health monitoring. On the left, an online health-parameter module collects health and operational feedback after each path execution, such as joint usage, temperature or vibration measurements, and other condition-monitoring signals. From these data, the module estimates the joint RUL j

using any available degradation or prognostic model. A mapping function then converts the estimated RUL j values into adaptive joint weights γj , which quantify how much each joint should be penalized in the planning cost. This process can be further divided into two key mechanisms: the Online Joint RUL Update Mechanism and the RUL-driven Adaptive Joint Weighting Mechanism, which are responsible for the dynamic estimation of joint lifetime and 5Figure 2: RUL-aware path planning with RRT* (closed-loop framework) the adaptive computation of health-based weighting, respectively. The detailed formulations of these mechanisms are presented in Sections 3.3 and 3.4. In the centre and on the right, the RUL-aware RRT* planner integrates RUL j and γj into its cost function while preserving the standard RRT* pipeline of sampling, steering, optimal parent selection, rewiring, and timeout checking. The detailed definition of the RUL-aware cost formulation, which combines geometric efficiency with joint health awareness, is presented in Section 3.2. During each iteration, the planner evaluates candidate edges using an RUL-aware cost that discourages excessive motion on highly degraded joints and promotes a more balanced distribution of joint-level motion usage, while still favouring short and feasible paths. After executing the planned path, new health and operational feedback are collected, and the RUL and weight parameters are updated for the next planning episode. Therefore, RUL and

3.3 Online Joint RUL Update Mechanism

Rather than treating RUL estimation as an isolated prognostic task, this mechanism integrates the health-update process into the motion-planning loop, enabling the planner to continuously adapt to the evolving degradation of each joint. After every plan-ning–execution cycle, the robot collects health and operational data (e.g., joint motion, load, 7temperature, vibration), which reflect the current wear state of each actuator. These feedback signals are used to update the joint-level remaining useful life (RUL), ensuring that the next planning iteration starts with the most recent health information. The updated RUL values are then converted into adaptive joint weights γj , which directly influence the RUL-aware RRT* cost function and consequently reshape the motion decisions. 3.4 RUL-driven Adaptive Joint Weighting Mechanism 

Based on the updated joint RUL values obtained from the online update mechanism, an adaptive joint weighting process is applied to reflect the current health condition of each actuator in motion planning. In the standard RRT* formulation, all joints are treated equally in the cost computation, which overlooks the fact that different joints may degrade at different rates. To overcome this limitation, the proposed mechanism adjusts the relative weights of joints according to their remaining useful life (RUL), assigning higher weights to joints with lower RUL and lower weights to healthier ones. This allows the planner to distribute motion effort more reasonably among the joints while keeping the overall cost strength unchanged. 3.5 Integration with RRT 

The proposed RUL-aware mechanisms are integrated into the standard RRT* algorithm through modifications to its three key stages: Expansion, Parent Selection, and Rewiring. These changes allow the planner to consider both geometric efficiency and joint health during tree construction, while maintaining the sampling-based nature and asymptotic optimality of RRT*. During the Expansion stage, the nearest node is no longer determined purely by Euclidean distance in the configuration space. Instead, the selection process incorporates the RUL-aware cost, enabling the planner to favor neighbors that lead to smoother joint utilization and lower cumulative degradation. This adjustment prevents the planner from repeatedly extending the tree through heavily degraded joints, thereby reducing stress concentration on weak actuators. In the Parent Selection stage, the accumulated cost from the start configuration to each candidate parent node is evaluated using the health-aware cost formulation described earlier. This allows the planner to choose a parent not only based on geometric proximity but also on the health condition of the joints involved in the connecting path. Consequently, motion paths that impose less effort on degraded joints are naturally preferred. During the Rewiring stage, the same health-aware cost is used to re-evaluate local connections in the tree. By checking whether the total cost of neighboring nodes can be reduced under the RUL-aware criterion, the planner incrementally refines the tree structure toward paths that achieve both motion efficiency and balanced joint usage. This ensures that even after the initial connection, local adjustments continue to optimize the distribution of joint-level motion usage as the tree grows. 9Figure 3: Experimental 6-DOF robotic arm used for validation After each planning cycle, the updated RUL values and joint weighting coefficients γj are fed back to the next iteration, forming a closed loop between motion planning and condition monitoring. Through this continuous feedback, the planner dynamically adapts to the robot’s evolving health condition, ensuring that motion trajectories remain both efficient and reliability-aware over time. 

# 4 Case Study 

This case study provides a comprehensive evaluation of the proposed RUL-aware RRT*, focusing on its effectiveness in mitigating degradation imbalance across multiple operational scenarios. Through controlled experiments, we quantify its impact on reliability, failure progression, and long-term task execution capability. 

4.1 Experimental Setup 

The proposed RUL-aware motion planning framework is implemented and evaluated within a simulation environment to ensure controllability and repeatability of the experiments. All im-plementations are carried out in the ROS Melodic framework using the MoveIt motion planning package integrated with the Open Motion Planning Library (OMPL). A 6-DOF UR5 robotic manipulator is used as the test platform, providing sufficient kinematic complexity to evaluate joint-level health adaptation. The proposed planner is embedded in OMPL as a customized RRT* variant that incorporates the RUL-aware cost formulation and adaptive joint weighting mechanisms introduced in Sections 3.2–3.4. All planning and execution processes are visualized and simulated in RViz, which offers collision checking, trajectory interpolation, and real-time visualization of the robot’s joint motion. Since the experiments are performed entirely in simulation, no real sensor measurements such as torque, temperature, or vibration are collected. Instead, the health state of each joint is abstracted through its angular displacement, which reflects the cumulative mechanical usage over repeated operations. After each pick-and-place task, the incremental joint angle changes are recorded and used as feedback to update the estimated Remaining Useful Life (RUL) of each joint through the online update mechanism. This simulation-based feedback design allows controlled testing of the planner’s adaptive behaviour without introducing uncertainty from real sensor noise. In this way, the angular usage serves as a surrogate for physical degradation, enabling the evaluation of how the planner dynamically responds to health variations across multiple planning and execution cycles. Both the baseline RRT* and the proposed RUL-aware RRT* share identical sampling strate-gies, step sizes, and connection radii to ensure fair comparison. 4.2 Test Scenarios 

To comprehensively evaluate the performance of the proposed RUL-aware motion planning framework, three groups of experiments, as listed in Table 1, are designed to simulate different joint degradation conditions and verify the planner’s adaptability under varying health states. All experiments are conducted on the same UR5 manipulator introduced in Section 4.1, and each task follows a pick-and-place motion, where the start and goal configurations are randomly generated within the robot’s reachable workspace. Each scenario represents a distinct reliability condition, covering uniform aging, uneven wear, and localized failure. # 5 Conclusion and Future Work 

This study examined the effectiveness of the RUL-aware RRT*, a motion-planning method that integrates joint-level remaining useful life (RUL) information into the planning process and adaptively adjusts joint usage based on evolving health conditions. Comprehensive evaluations under three representative scenarios, namely the Full Health Scenario (FHS), the Heterogeneous Degradation Scenario (HDS), and the Local Degradation Scenario (LDS), show that the RUL-aware RRT* consistently improves system reliability compared with the baseline RRT*. In the Full Health Scenario, where all joints begin with identical lifetimes, the RUL-aware RRT* maintains balanced degradation throughout execution. It achieves higher minimum re-maining lifetime RU L min , smaller variation in joint RUL, and improved system lifetime recovery ratios Grec , while requiring only a small increase in total lifetime consumption. In the Heterogeneous Degradation Scenario, the benefits are more substantial. The method effectively protects initially weak joints and reduces the amplification of health disparities. It produces higher bottleneck lifetimes, more uniform degradation distributions, and lower CV RUL ,resulting in a healthier system state at the end of execution. In the Local Degradation Scenario, which represents run-to-failure conditions, the RUL-aware RRT* significantly extends the system’s operational duration. By limiting the motion assigned to the degraded joint, it increases the first failure task index kfail by 48–56% across different degradation parameters and achieves meaningful relative lifetime gains Glife . The degradation balance at failure is also improved, as reflected by smaller values of CV fail RUL .Although the RUL-aware RRT* consistently improves reliability, the magnitude of improve-23 ment varies with the degradation shape parameter p. The gains become larger when degradation is more sensitive to accumulated motion usage, indicating that health-aware planning is partic-ularly important in systems where small increases in joint usage lead to disproportionately large degradation. Even in milder degradation regimes, the method still provides noticeable benefits. Overall, the results demonstrate that the RUL-aware RRT* effectively balances degradation, delays the formation of bottlenecks, and improves the lifetime performance of robotic systems operating under wear. By incorporating health feedback directly into the planning process, the method provides a practical and robust approach for enhancing long-term system reliability. As a direction for future work, we plan to extend the RUL-aware RRT* to multi-robot settings in which several manipulators collaborate on shared tasks. Such systems introduce additional challenges in task allocation, motion coordination, and cross-robot health interaction. To address these complexities, we aim to incorporate agentic AI and reinforcement learning to enable adaptive motion allocation, coordinated planning, and dynamic health-aware decision making across multiple robotic arms. This direction has the potential to further enhance long-term system reliability in complex, multi-agent operational environments.
