## ABSTRACT 

Joint embedding predictive architectures (JEPAs) predict future latent represen-tations without reconstructing observations, enabling world models to focus on high-level semantic dynamics. However, a JEPA can preserve high dimensional visual information while discarding information about the physical consequences of actions. We call this failure mode causal dynamics information collapse and propose action-grounded vision-invariance latent (AVL) to prevent this collapse. We first use the executed action as an auxiliary dynamics anchor that encourages the model to preserve dynamics information, and then use a vision-invariance pathway which aligns perturbed and clean latent predictions without discarding dynamics information, forcing the model to fully understand and utilize causal dynamics information. We validate AVL on four robotic control tasks (TwoRoom, PushT, OGBench Cube, and Reacher), showing that it substantially improves success rates under visual perturbations while preserving clean-environment performance. We further evaluate physical consequence alignment, clean–noisy dynamics consis-tency, and the causal effect of targeted transition subspace erasure. Collectively, these results indicate that dynamic information causally relevant to planning is preserved from collapse under AVL. 

## 1 INTRODUCTION 

World models learn to predict how an environment evolves, providing a compact substrate for planning and control (Ha & Schmidhuber, 2018). Joint-embedding predictive architecture (JEPA) offers one way to achieve this goal. Instead of reconstructing future observations, a JEPA model predicts future latent representations directly (Assran et al., 2023). This design sidesteps pixel-level reconstruction and focuses on learning abstract predictive structure, making it attractive for world modeling. A central challenge in JEPA training is preventing representation collapse, where all inputs map to a narrow region of latent space. Existing work addresses this at the representation level: variance, covariance, and distributional regularizers penalize degenerate latent statistics (Bardes et al., 2022); EMA-based teacher networks provide a slowly moving prediction target that resists instantaneous collapse (Assran et al., 2023; Bardes et al., 2024); JEPA world models such as L EWM combine joint-embedding prediction with SIGReg regularization to prevent geometry-degenerate latent embeddings (Maes et al., 2026; Balestriero & LeCun, 2025). These mechanisms are effective at preventing global collapse and make the learned representations remain high dimensional, non-constant, and predictive on average. However, preventing global collapse does not guarantee that the latent preserves the information needed to distinguish how different actions causally change the physical state. Two distinct actions that produce different physical state changes may yield nearly identical predicted latent transitions. We refer to this failure as causal dynamics information collapse .We introduce action-grounded vision-invariance latent (AVL) to mitigate this collapse. The vision-invariance pathway then exposes the model to clean and visually perturbed observations while freezing the action head. The perturbed latent is aligned with the clean latent, but the action loss prevents this alignment from being achieved by simply discarding information, making the model fully use causal dynamics information to predict the future latent. Our method substantially mitigates causal dynamics information collapse and improves closed-loop planning performance. On TwoRoom, we lift predicted-to-physical consequence alignment (Spearman ρ) from 0.35 to 0.53 and reduce normalized regret from 0.152 to 0.061 , while clean–noisy ranking consistency increases from 0.89 to 0.98 . In closed-loop planning, success rate under Gaussian policy input noise ( σ=0 .10 ) improves from 15 .3% to 81 .5% on PushT and from 65 .7% to 88 .7% on TwoRoom. Under saturation shift ( +0 .35 ), it increases from 14 .0% to 62 .0% on PushT and from 

54 .8% to 86 .7% on TwoRoom. We also improve OGBench Cube (from 73 .2% to 78 .2% under clean) and remain competitive on Reacher. These results suggest that global non-collapse is only a starting point, a useful world-model latent should preserve causal dynamics-relevant physical structure. Our main contributions are: • We formulate causal dynamics information collapse , a failure mode in which high di-mensional visual latents insufficiently preserve action-conditioned physical change, and operationalize it with controlled experiments tied to physical outcomes. • We introduce action-grounded vision-invariance latent (AVL) that targets the collapse: the action-grounded pathway grounds state-conditioned latent transitions with executed actions to preserve dynamic information in latent space, and the vision-invariance pathway aligns perturbed and clean latents while freezing the action head, forcing the model to understand and utilize causal dynamics information to predict future latent. • We validate AVL across TwoRoom, PushT, Reacher, and OGBench Cube, showing that it substantially reduces success-rate degradation under Gaussian, brightness, and saturation perturbations while preserving clean environment performance, and provide mechanism-level experiments showing that the preserved dynamic information is causally meaningful. 

## 2 RELATED WORK 

Latent world models. World models learn a compressed simulation of the environment for planning and control (Ha & Schmidhuber, 2018). Early latent dynamics models reconstruct observations from learned states and train policies in latent imagination (Hafner et al., 2019; 2020; 2025; Wu et al., 2023). Reconstruction-free alternatives predict future latent features directly: DINO-WM plans on top of frozen DINOv2 features (Zhou et al., 2025; Oquab et al., 2024), PLDM combines joint-embedding prediction with VICReg-style collapse prevention and inverse dynamics (Sobal et al., 2025), and L EWM introduces SIGReg regularization for stable end to end training from pixels (Maes et al., 2026; Balestriero & LeCun, 2025). 

Joint embedding predictive architecture. JEPA predicts target embeddings from context em-beddings without pixel reconstruction and has become a prominent framework for self supervised representation learning (LeCun, 2022; Assran et al., 2023). A central challenge in JEPA training is 2Under review as a conference paper at ICLR 2027 representation collapse, where all inputs map to a narrow latent region. Early preventions rely on architectural asymmetry: I-JEPA uses an EMA target encoder and masked context prediction so that the context encoder cannot trivially copy the target (Assran et al., 2023). V-JEPA extends this to video with spatiotemporal masking (Bardes et al., 2024), and V-JEPA 2 scales the same recipe to large-scale video understanding and planning (Assran et al., 2025). Others use explicit statistical regularizers: VICReg penalizes latent variance, invariance, and covariance (Bardes et al., 2022), and LeJEPA provides a provable non-collapse guarantee through spectral regularization (Balestriero & LeCun, 2025). L EWM uses SIGReg to maintain non-degenerate latent statistics in world model (Maes et al., 2026), Sub-JEPA adds subspace Gaussian regularization (Zhao et al., 2026), and SD-JEPA decomposes the latent into progression and content subspaces (Thil et al., 2026). Concurrent works such as SMWM and Delta-JEPA expect inverse-dynamics method alone to prevent collapse. (Ivashkov et al., 2026; Zhang et al., 2026). However, these methods mostly address collapse at the level of global latent statistics: variance, rank, covariance, or distributional non-degeneracy. Our work targets this gap: we study how to prevent the selective loss of causal dynamics information in JEPA world models, an information collapse directly limits model performance.

The remaining experiment evaluation protocols and metrics are detailed in Appendix B.1. 4.2 IMPLEMENTATION DETAILS 

Training configurations. We train on an NVIDIA RTX 5090 GPU. The action-grounded pathway is trained for 10 epochs with AdamW, learning rate 5 × 10 −5, weight decay 10 −3, bf16 mixed precision, and eight data loader workers. The vision-invariance pathway is initialized from action-grounded weights and trained for 2 epochs with AdamW, learning rate 10 −5, the same weight decay and precision, and batch size 128. Both pathways use λ1 = 0 .09 for SIGReg, matching the original LeWM configuration, and λ2 = 5 for the action loss. On TwoRoom, the action-grounded sweep gives success rates of 85 .2% , 87 .2% , 92 .8% , and 92 .6% for λ2 ∈ { 0.05 , 0.5, 5, 10 }, respectively. This sweep supports the choice λ2 = 5 : increasing the action weight from 0.05 to 5 improves success by 7.6 percentage points, while λ2 = 10 does not improve further. In the vision-invariance pathway the action head is frozen while its loss remains active. The measured training time of both pathways is 41.7 minutes per epoch on PushT, 15.3 minutes per epoch on TwoRoom, 53.4 minutes per epoch on Reacher, and 61.9 minutes per epoch on Cube. ## 5 CONCLUSION 

We studied how to prevent causal dynamics information collapse in JEPA world models. Our AVL method uses action anchoring and constrained clean–perturbed alignment to preserve and exploit action conditioned physical structure. ## B ADDITIONAL EXPERIMENTAL DETAILS 

B.1 MECHANISM -LEVEL PROTOCOLS 

For the counterfactual experiment, we sample 100 matched simulator states and evaluate the same 12 candidate action sequences at every state, with each candidate spanning 5 action blocks. The simulator executes the shared candidate bank to obtain physical consequences. Spearman correlation and normalized regret measure ordering agreement and the physical cost of model selection; top-k

inclusion is reported as a secondary selection metric. For clean–noisy consistency, Gaussian noise with σ = 0 .05 is applied only to the current policy observation, while the physical rollout, goal, and success criterion remain clean; three noisy repeats are evaluated per state. Clean–noisy ranking consistency measures whether candidate ordering is preserved; nuisance drift measures perturbation-induced transition change at fixed action, and the action-to-nuisance ratio compares action-induced separation with that drift. Candidate-transition erasure uses the same predicted candidate responses and compares targeted removal of leading principal directions with random orthogonal removal. B.2 COUNTERFACTUAL PROTOCOL 

The archived mechanism experiment uses 100 matched simulator states and 12 shared candidate action sequences per state. Each candidate spans five action blocks, and each sequence covers 25 physical low level steps. Predicted latent responses, target latent responses, and two dimensional physical responses are saved for every state–candidate pair. Simulator restoration was audited over 1,200 renders per model with zero non-exact restorations and zero pixel difference. States are sampled from the held-out TwoRoom trajectory pool with a fixed seed; the shared action bank is reused for both models and consists of 12 sequences of five action blocks. Images use the model’s standard ImageNet normalization after any evaluation-time uint8 corruption. The physical oracle is the candidate with the largest physical response norm; regret is the oracle-minus-chosen response difference. All erasure branches rescore the same cached candidate responses.
