# MagServo: Uncertainty-Resilient Hierarchical Magnetic Servoing via Learned Latent Representations 

Yuhan Tan 1,2,†, Yameng Zhang 2,3,†,∗, Pei Liu 2,4, Yao Zhong 2, Zhongliang Jiang 2,∗

Abstract — Magnetic navigation provides contact-free and line-of-sight-independent feedback for robotic systems, yet ex-isting approaches typically rely on explicit pose estimation or direct use of raw magnetic measurements, making accurate control susceptible to modeling errors, measurement noise, and disturbances. This work presents MagServo, a hierarchical learning-based framework for robust 6-DoF magnetic servoing directly using the learned latent magnetic feature. MagServo learns uncertainty-resilient magnetic representations through masked reconstruction and captures state-dependent interac-tion dynamics between robot motion and latent magnetic tran-sitions without analytical magnetic models or explicit Jacobian supervision. Based on the learned dynamics, a hierarchical con-troller combines nonlinear model predictive control for coarse approach with local Jacobian inversion for precise fine regula-tion. Extensive physical experiments demonstrate submillimeter and subdegree accuracy, achieving mean terminal errors of 0.386 mm and 0.479 ◦ for 6-DoF pose reaching. MagServo further outperforms a localization-based control baseline in complex trajectory tracking and maintains robust performance under unseen magnetic-source configurations without retrain-ing. A supplementary video of the real-robot experiments is available at https://youtu.be/rZt1NUP1Mr0 .

I. I NTRODUCTION 

Magnetic localization and navigation technologies have attracted increasing attention due to their non-contact, real-time, and line-of-sight-independent nature, enabling reliable operation in visually occluded or confined environments [1], [2], [3], [4]. Their radiation-free nature further makes them particularly well suited for medical applications, particularly robot-assisted endoscopic examinations and minimally in-vasive procedures [5], [6]. In these cases, visual feedback is often compromised due to poor lighting conditions or physical obstructions between the observer and the target, such as a robotic capsule for gastrointestinal examination [2], [7]. Magnetic navigation methods can be broadly divided into two categories: explicit localization-based navigation and direct raw magnetic data-based servoing. The former requires explicit estimation of the robot end-effector pose from real-time magnetometer measurements using an accurate mag-netic model. Navigation is then achieved by minimizing the error between the estimated current pose and the desired pose. To establish the mapping between the magnetic flux

Considering the inher-ent nonlinearity and modeling uncertainties of this mapping, Son et al. adopted a quadratic measurement model for online calibration [11]. To further improve pose estimation accuracy, numerical optimization methods, such as particle swarm optimization [12] and the Levenberg-Marquardt algo-rithm [13], are commonly employed to minimize the discrep-ancy between the measured and model-predicted magnetic fields. While these model-based approaches offer strong physical interpretability, they often require careful system calibration and computationally intensive iterative optimiza-tion, limiting their efficiency for real-time applications [14]. Recently, to reduce the complexity associated with pa-rameter identification and iterative optimization while im-proving localization accuracy, learning-based methods have emerged to directly estimate robot poses from magnetometer measurements. Su et al. proposed AMagPoseNet for 6-DoF pose estimation of an annular magnet, where PoseNet is pretrained using data generated from a mathematical model and CaliNet adapts the learned mapping to the real magnetic field using a small amount of experimental data [15]. This model-assisted learning strategy substantially reduces the need for large-scale real-world data collection. Similarly, Zhao et al. employed differential magnetic signals and a arXiv:2610.06046v1 [cs.RO] 5 Oct 2026 precisely calibrated magnetic model to generate large-scale simulated data for training a deep network, enabling accurate and real-time pose estimation [16]. Yang et al. proposed PRPosNet, which combines CNN-based local feature extrac-tion, coordinate attention, and Transformer-based modeling to capture both local spatial characteristics and long-range dependencies among magnetic sensors [17]. Beyond direct pose regression, recent studies have increasingly incorpo-rated physical priors into neural models to improve accuracy and generalizability. Shen et al. proposed a physics-informed residual network with a dual-branch architecture to explicitly correct discrepancies between the ideal magnetic dipole model and real magnetic measurements [18]. To account for measurement noise in practical magnetometers, Xie et al. 

augmented ideal magnetic data generated from a theoretical model with Gaussian noise and trained PoseNet on the synthetically corrupted measurements, achieving localization errors of approximately 1.54 mm in position and 2.24 ◦ in orientation [19]. Instead of explicitly estimating the robot pose, direct raw magnetic data-based servoing uses magnetometer mea-surements as feature descriptors and directly actuates the robot end-effector to minimize the corresponding feature errors [20]. This eliminates the need to map magnetic flux density measurements into Cartesian poses. However, direct servoing requires a Jacobian matrix that relates robot motion to variations in magnetometer measurements, which is challenging to derive analytically. To address this issue, Su et al. pioneered an online Jacobian estimation approach, in which the Jacobian matrix is vectorized as a state variable within a state-space formulation and estimated online using a Kalman filter (KF) [21]. Building upon this formulation, Tan et al. and Edelmann et al. developed model-free control strategies for magnetically actuated endoscopes [22], [23]. However, the performance of such methods strongly depends on appropriate characterization of the process and measure-ment noise statistics used by the KF. To relax the reliance on predefined static noise models, adaptive approaches have been introduced to dynamically update the process and measurement noise covariances during online control [24], [25]. Nevertheless, these approaches primarily estimate the Jacobian from feature variations and robot motion at the current control step, making the estimation susceptible to instantaneous measurement noise and disturbances. To fur-ther improve servoing stability, Zhang et al. introduced a Jacobian consistency assumption and incorporated a mov-ing window of historical feature-error and actuation data, which are linearly combined with current observations to achieve more robust Jacobian estimation [20]. However, this approach remains susceptible to environmental disturbances and transient measurement drift induced by sensor noise. To address these challenges, we introduce MagServo ,a hierarchical learning-based framework for robust and accurate magnetic servoing. MagServo learns uncertainty-resilient magnetic representations through masked recon-struction and captures their motion-conditioned dynamics without requiring analytical magnetic models or explicit Jacobian supervision. The representation and dynamics are further jointly refined through multi-step rollout learning, establishing a robust, control-aware latent space for magnetic servoing. Leveraging the learned dynamics, MagServo com-bines nonlinear model predictive control (MPC) for rapid coarse approach with local Jacobian inversion for precise fine regulation. By directly translating latent magnetic dis-crepancies into corrective robot motions, MagServo bypasses explicit magnetic-to-pose estimation and enables accurate closed-loop 6-DoF servoing. The contributions of this work are summarized as follows:  

> •

We propose a direct magnetic servoing algorithm that uses learned latent magnetic representations as control features, eliminating explicit pose estimation and an-alytical magnetic modeling while remaining robust to measurement noise and disturbances.  

> •

We introduce MagServo, which jointly learns latent magnetic representations and state-dependent interac-tion dynamics and integrates them into a hierarchi-cal coarse-to-fine controller, combining nonlinear MPC with local Jacobian inversion for accurate and reliable magnetic servoing. To evaluate MagServo, we conduct extensive physical exper-iments on 6-DoF pose reaching, complex trajectory tracking, and unseen magnetic-source configurations to assess its ac-curacy and generalizability. MagServo achieves submillime-ter and subdegree accuracy, with mean terminal errors of 0.386 mm and 0.479 ◦ for pose reaching. Its effectiveness is further demonstrated by substantially outperforming the localization-based baseline and maintaining robust perfor-mance under unseen magnetic-source configurations without retraining. II. S YSTEM OVERVIEW AND PROBLEM FORMULATION 

A. System Configuration 

As shown in Fig. 1, the system couples a robot manipulator carrying two permanent magnets with a stationary 4 × 4

array of three-axis magnetometers. The axially magnetized cylindrical magnets are rigidly attached to the end-effector, with their magnetization axes separated by 120 ◦ to reduce the rotational ambiguity of a single axisymmetric magnet. As the end-effector translates and rotates relative to the array, the resulting changes in the measured field provide feedback for regulating its motion. The magnetic measurements are transmitted to a host com-puter, where the controller computes incremental Cartesian commands for the robot. After each increment is executed, a new observation is acquired to update the next command. This closed-loop interaction enables the robot to approach a desired magnetic observation through successive corrections. The relation between robot motion and magnetic feedback is learned from recorded data, without requiring an analytical magnetic field model.

This section evaluates the proposed method on a physi-cal robotic platform through single-pose reaching, complex trajectory following, and robustness against disturbances introduced by an additional magnetic source. Tracking accu-racy is further compared with a magnetic localization-based navigation baseline. Performance is quantitatively assessed in terms of Cartesian pose errors and control efficiency. 

A. Experimental Setup 

The experimental platform comprises a 7-DoF RealMan 75 manipulator (RealMan Intelligent Technology, China) and a stationary 4 × 4 magnetic sensor array. A 3D-printed end-effector fixture holds two identical N38 NdFeB cylindrical magnets, each 10 mm in diameter and height, with their magnetization axes separated by 120 ◦ (see Fig. 1). This non-parallel dual-magnet configuration breaks the rotational symmetry of a single axially magnetized cylindrical magnet, thereby reducing ambiguity in 6-DoF pose estimation [20]. The sensor array consists of 16 LIS3MDL tri-axial magne-tometers with a spacing of 60 mm , a measurement range of ±16 Gs , and root-mean-square noise levels of 3.2, 3.2,and 4.1 mGs along the x, y, and z axes, respectively. An STM32H743 microcontroller acquires the sensor mea-surements and transmits them to the host computer via serial communication at up to 20 Hz . After preprocessing, the magnetic measurements serve as the feedback signal for tracking throughout all experiments. B. Performance on 6-DoF Pose Reaching 

To evaluate the performance of the proposed magnetic servoing algorithm, we first conduct a single-pose reaching task. A target pose is arbitrarily selected within the sensing workspace, and the corresponding magnetic measurements are recorded as the reference for servoing. To assess robust-ness to different initial conditions, 12 trials are performed from different initial poses. These poses combine transla-tional offsets of ±25 mm along the x and y axes with offsets of −15 mm , 0, and 15 mm along the z axis relative to the target pose. In each trial, the proposed servoing algorithm continu-ously generates corrective motions toward the target until the predefined latent convergence criterion is satisfied. The control hyperparameters are set to H = 3 , ϵenter = 0 .3,and ϵexit = 0 .5. Fig. 4(a) visualizes the executed trajectories from different initial poses toward the same target pose, while Fig. 4(b) shows the magnetic field measurements at selected iterations of a representative trial. As servoing progresses, the measured magnetic field distribution gradually approaches the target distribution, indicating convergence toward the desired pose. As the initial motion is unconstrained, the end-effector may initially deviate from the target; nevertheless, the closed-loop controller progressively corrects the motion and ultimately converges to the target. The final translational and rotational errors converged to 0.52 mm and 0.55 ◦,respectively, after 104 steps. D. Performance on Unseen Magnetic-Source Configurations 

Magnetic servoing should remain robust to magnetic-source configurations unseen during training, as magnetic field characteristics may vary across locations, over time, or due to environmental disturbances. To enhance this ro-bustness, sensor readings are randomly masked during AE training in this study, encouraging the network to reconstruct incomplete magnetic observations and learn latent represen-tations resilient to such variations. To evaluate this capability, an additional magnet, identical to the original two, was mounted on the robot’s sixth joint, altering both the magnetic field distribution and its response to robot motion. The same spiral and raster trajectories were then tested using newly acquired reference sequences, while the learned AE model remained unchanged. As shown in Table III, MagServo successfully completed both trajectories under the unseen three-source configuration. For the spiral, the additional source moderately increased the tracking errors, while requiring a comparable number of actions (684 vs. 673). Interestingly, the raster trajectory exhibited lower errors in most components and required fewer actions (824 vs. 894), indicating that the additional source does not necessarily degrade servoing performance. Overall, all mean absolute errors remained below 0.50 mm 

and 0.41 ◦ in the three-source setting. These results demon-strate that MagServo can accommodate substantial changes in magnetic-source configuration without retraining, while maintaining submillimeter and subdegree navigation accu-racy. V. C ONCLUSION 

In this work, we presented MagServo , a hierarchical learning-based framework for robust and accurate 6-DoF magnetic servoing. Rather than relying on explicit pose estimation or analytical magnetic models, MagServo directly uses uncertainty-resilient latent magnetic representations as control features. A three-stage learning strategy progressively learns robust magnetic representations and state-dependent interaction dynamics and jointly refines them for closed-loop control. Leveraging the learned dynamics, hierarchi-cal control combines nonlinear MPC for coarse approach with local Jacobian inversion for precise fine regulation. Extensive physical experiments demonstrated submillime-ter and subdegree accuracy, with mean terminal errors of 

0.386 mm and 0.479 ◦ for 6-DoF pose reaching. MagServo further outperformed localization-based control in complex trajectory tracking and maintained robust performance under unseen magnetic-source configurations without retraining.
