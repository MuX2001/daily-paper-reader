# Admissibility-Preserving Control for Multi-Input Systems with Joint Capacity Constraints 

Saurabh Kumar, Lohitvel Gopikannan, Shashi Ranjan Kumar, and Abhinav Sinha 

Abstract —This paper addresses the control of multi-input strict-feedback nonlinear systems subject to a joint capacity constraint , in which the admissible input set is a coupled subset of the individual actuator limits. Unlike existing constraint-handling methods that enforce actuator bounds channel by channel and may unnecessarily suppress admissible control directions, we develop an Anisotropic Joint-Admissibility-Preserving Input Realization (AJ-APIR) framework that ex-plicitly exploits the geometry of the joint constraint. The proposed realization constructs a state-dependent gain matrix whose spectral decomposition separates the commanded input into normal and tangential directions relative to the constraint boundary. The normal component is attenuated as the boundary is approached, while the tangential component is preserved, which allows the admissible control effort to be redistributed without loss of tracking authority. Integrated with a backstep-ping controller, the AJ-APIR framework guarantees forward invariance of the joint admissible set for all time. We establish exponential convergence of the tracking error to zero together with uniform boundedness of all closed-loop signals, and charac-terize the resulting command-demand behavior under the joint constraint. Simulation results for a representative second-order, two-input nonlinear system subject to a power-budget constraint demonstrate the efficacy of the proposed method to enforce the joint input constraint. 

Index Terms —Safety-critical control, admissibility-preserving control, control system synthesis, joint capacity constraint, nonlinear control, multi-input systems. 

I. I NTRODUCTION 

In many practical multi-actuator systems, actuator channels share a common physical resource, such as the electrical bus shared by control surfaces and propulsion in fixed-wing UAVs, the thermal path shared by joint motors in redundantly actuated manipulators, or the prime mover shared by azimuth thrusters in marine surface vessels. This physical coupling gives rise to a joint constraint on the collective input that independent per-channel bounds fail to capture. In practice, the aggregate demand may breach the shared operating limit even when every individual channel remains within its own admissible range. Consequently, to maintain the collective input within the jointly admissible set at all times while ensuring that the system output tracks a prescribed reference trajectory remains an open challenge concerning safety-critical control.

The problem of designing feedback controllers for non-linear systems subject to actuator constraints has attracted sustained research attention for more than three decades. Nevertheless, the systematic treatment of coupled actua-tor constraints, particularly without unnecessarily sacrificing admissible control authority, remains an open problem. A foundational body of work addresses this problem through anti-windup augmentation of pre-designed linear controllers, in which a secondary compensator is introduced to recover closed-loop performance when the primary loop saturates [1], [2], [3]. Although systematic and amenable to convex synthesis, anti-windup methods are generally developed for linear nominal plants and do not readily accommodate the state-dependent gain structure of nonlinear strict-feedback systems. A parallel stream exploits the recursive structure of strict-feedback dynamics through backstepping [4], with constraint handling achieved by confining each virtual control to a bounded feasible set. The authors in [5] showed that augmenting a standard backstepping design with auxiliary dynamics whose equilibrium tracks the saturated channel yields a complete Lyapunov analysis for tracking under asymmetric saturation, and the construction was subsequently extended to uncertain multi-input systems in [6]. It is worth noting that these treatments bound each channel separately, so that the feasible set they enforce is, by construction, a Cartesian product. Barrier Lyapunov functions (BLFs) provide an alternative mechanism for enforcing constraints in strict-feedback sys-tems by introducing a potential that grows without bound as a constrained variable approaches its prescribed limit, thereby preventing the trajectory from reaching the boundary. The authors in [7] established asymptotic tracking for single-output systems subject to output constraints using BLFs. In [8], this framework was discussed for full-state constraints, while [9] combined BLFs with adaptive neural networks to address uncertain strict-feedback systems. However, standard BLF constructions assign an independent barrier potential to each constrained channel, resulting in a Lyapunov function composed of decoupled terms. Consequently, they do not directly capture the inter-channel coupling induced by a joint capacity constraint. Control Barrier Functions (CBFs) [10] provide a unified framework for barrier-certificate and Lyapunov-based stabil-ity analysis, with a quadratic program (QP)-based safety filter applicable to a broad class of nonlinear systems. The safety-critical control framework in [11] accommodates arXiv:2610.00533v1 [eess.SY] 30 Sep 2026 general smooth constraint sets, including joint constraints, by incorporating barrier-function gradients into a pointwise QP. The authors in [12] demonstrated the compatibility of such constraint-handling mechanisms with backstepping for relative-degree-one input constraints. Nevertheless, the QP formulation modifies the nominal control according to the Euclidean minimum-norm criterion, which is effectively direction-blind with respect to the geometry of the joint constraint. Near a joint constraint boundary, this may unnec-essarily attenuate components of the commanded input that are tangent to the boundary and therefore do not contribute to constraint violation. Thus, constraint enforcement need not be isotropic, yet this geometric structure does not appear to be explicitly exploited in the existing CBF-based formulations. A further class of methods relies on control allocation to map a scalar or low-dimensional virtual control into a higher-dimensional actuator command subject to box and joint constraints, typically by solving a weighted least-squares problem or quadratic program at each instant [13], [14], [15], [16]. The relationship between control allocation and optimal control was discussed in [17], while a dynamic counterpart was developed in [18]. Control allocation de-couples the outer-loop control design from the constraint-satisfaction problem, making it attractive for redundant plat-forms. However, the feasibility of the allocation problem depends on the actuator authority available at each instant and is therefore not guaranteed during large-signal transients. Moreover, the stability of the interconnected outer-loop and allocator dynamics generally requires a separate analysis. Governor and predictive architectures [19], [20], in contrast, address feasibility at the planning or reference-generation level by reshaping the reference or optimizing over a finite horizon, respectively, but at a potentially high computational cost for fast actuation loops. The Admissibility-Preserving Input Realization (APIR) framework, introduced for strict-feedback systems with asymmetric single-channel actuator constraints [21], adopts a dynamical-systems perspective on constraint satisfaction. Rather than modifying a nominal controller through a point-wise optimization or treating constraint enforcement as a separate allocation layer, APIR embeds admissibility directly into the physical input dynamics through a differential re-alization whose gain vanishes at the constraint boundary. A distributed extension to networked systems with directed communication graphs was subsequently developed in [22]. These works establish an intrinsic alternative to optimization-based safety filters [10], [11] and allocation-based constraint handling [16], in which admissibility is enforced through the dynamics of the realized input itself. However, the existing APIR construction [21] is funda-mentally channel-wise, that is, its realization is formed from independent scalar admissibility factors and therefore applies to admissible sets that are Cartesian products of individual actuator ranges. This indicates that it may not directly address a coupled input envelope whose boundary depends on the collective actuator state. This may appear as a limitation, which is nonetheless shared by standard channel-wise satura-tion and backstepping- based controllers [5], [6] and by BLF formulations that impose separate barrier terms on individual constrained variables [7], [8], [9]. CBF-based formulations can represent general smooth joint constraint sets [10], [11]. However, their standard optimization-based realization mod-ifies the nominal command through a pointwise safety filter rather than embedding the joint admissibility mechanism directly into the input dynamics. Likewise, control-allocation methods explicitly account for coupled actuator limitations, but do so by introducing a separate allocation layer between the outer-loop controller and the physical actuators [16]. It must be noted that this scheme may be effective for dis-tributing control effort among constrained actuators, but the architecture separates constraint satisfaction from the closed-loop analysis. However, the feasibility must be maintained by the allocation problem, and the effect of the allocation on the stability and tracking properties of the overall closed-loop system warrants a separate analysis. To the best of our knowledge, the existing literature does not provide a dynamic realization that simultaneously enforces a non-Cartesian joint input envelope and preserves admissible control authority in directions tangent to its boundary. The current work extends the notion of the admissibility-preserving control framework [21] from individual actuator constraints to coupled multi-input constraints. We replace the channel-wise scalar realization with a matrix-valued, geometry-aware realization that differentiates between con-trol directions that may risk joint admissibility and those that remain feasible. In particular, the proposed AJ-APIR selectively suppresses the component normal to the joint constraint boundary as it is approached, while preserving the tangential component. Thus, the proposed realization en-forces the shared input envelope inherently while avoiding the unnecessary attenuation of admissible control redistribution that arises from direction-independent constraint handling. Our main contributions are summarized as follows:  

> •

We propose an anisotropic joint-admissibility-preserving input realization (AJ-APIR) for multi-input strict-feedback nonlinear systems subject to coupled joint capacity constraints. This is unlike the channel-wise methods [5], [6], which cannot express a coupled set.  

> •

The proposed realization employs a matrix-valued gain that splits spectrally into normal and tangential com-ponents relative to the joint constraint boundary. This allows the normal component of the commanded input to be attenuated near the boundary while retaining the tangential component. Thus, the proposed strategy extends the admissibility-preserving control of [21] from channel-wise constraints to a non-Cartesian joint admis-sible set.  

> •

We integrate AJ-APIR with recursive backstepping and establish the corresponding closed-loop guarantees. In particular, we derive a compact forward-invariant subset of the joint admissible set on which the realization channel remains uniformly nondegenerate.  

> •

An AJ-APC compatibility condition is then introduced to relate the available actuator authority to the tracking demand, under which the tracking and input realization errors converge exponentially, and all closed-loop sig-nals remain uniformly bounded. We demonstrate the effect of exploiting the joint-constraint geometry through two numerical studies and an aerospace application. The first study shows that satisfying the indi-vidual actuator bounds does not ensure joint admissibility: the channel-wise isotropic realization repeatedly crosses the shared capacity boundary, whereas AJ-APIR remains inside the joint admissible set for the same commanded input. The second study considers a nonlinear two-input strict-feedback system and shows that the proposed realization maintains both the individual and joint constraints while the output converges to its desired reference trajectory. We further implement the proposed method for UAV constrained path following in 3D, which further bolsters its effectiveness. II.
