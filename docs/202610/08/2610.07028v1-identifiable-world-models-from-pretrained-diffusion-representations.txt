# ABSTRACT 

Diffusion-based world models can generate and predict trajectories in high-dimensional dynamical systems, but predictive accuracy does not imply that their latent coordinates recover the underlying state variables or causal interactions. We ask whether a frozen pretrained diffusion model can be equipped with identifiable coordinates without retraining its generative backbone. We show that auxiliary-variable nonlinear ICA guarantees can be transferred to Contrastive Diffusion Alignment (ConDA), which learns only a lightweight alignment map on top of frozen diffusion latents. Under standard TCL/GCL assumptions, the aligned repre-sentation identifies latent dynamical states up to permutation and componentwise invertible transformations, preserves the latent dynamic structural causal model, and reduces lagged graph recovery to transition-Jacobian sparsity. We evaluate TCL-, GCL-, and CEBRA-based ConDA against TDRL, CaRiNG, IDOL, tem-poral SuaVE, and iVAE across physical and robotic video systems. TCL and GCL achieve near-perfect blockwise state recovery and competitive lagged graph recovery, including exact recovery in a simulated falling-body system. In a sim-ulated bipedal robot, learned dynamics recover the sign and temporal structure of responses to held-out control perturbations. These results show that a frozen generative diffusion model can be equipped with coordinates that are identifiable, structurally interpretable, and useful for analyzing intervention-relevant dynamics. 

# 1 INTRODUCTION 

Learning meaningful representations of high-dimensional dynamical systems is a central problem in scientific machine learning. Many scientific, biomedical, and robotic systems are governed by low-dimensional latent dynamics but are observed through high-dimensional measurements. Diffusion models provide powerful generative representations of such observations (Ho et al., 2020; Rombach et al., 2022), but generative fidelity alone does not imply that their internal representations recover the variables or interactions governing the underlying system. Indeed, multiple latent parameterizations can explain the same observations (Hyvärinen & Pajunen, 1999; Locatello et al., 2019; Khemakhem et al., 2020), and predictive representations need not preserve the causal structure required for intervention, control, or scientific reasoning (Bengio et al., 2013; Schölkopf et al., 2021). Contrastive learning, in turn, can exploit temporal or auxiliary information to recover structured low-dimensional representations (Hyvarinen & Morioka, 2016; Oord et al., 2018; Chen et al., 2020; Schneider et al., 2023). This raises a natural question: can a pretrained diffusion model retain its generative capacity while supporting an identifiable and controllable latent coordinate system that preserves causal structure? Nonlinear ICA provides one route to identifiability through auxiliary variables such as time, condi-tions, interventions, or control inputs (Hyvarinen & Morioka, 2017; Hyvarinen et al., 2019; Khe-makhem et al., 2020), but its extension to high-dimensional dynamical systems represented through diffusion latents remains largely unexplored. Building on Contrastive Diffusion Alignment (ConDA) (Sandilya et al., 2026), we study identifiability and causal graph recovery in diffusion-based world models. We distinguish three representation levels: the true low-dimensional dynamical state st, the high-dimensional diffusion latent xt, and the observed frame yt. Generatively, the system follows (st → xt → yt). We use the standard density-ratio interpretation of supervised InfoNCE to connect auxiliary-aligned contrastive learning on diffusion latents with auxiliary-variable nonlinear ICA. Under the stated separability and identifiability assumptions, the learned embedding recovers the latent dynamical state up to permutation and componentwise invertible transformations. We further show that these coordinates preserve the factorization and parent structure of the latent dynamic SCM, and that the lagged causal graph is characterized by the sparsity of the inverse transition-noise Jacobian. In practice, under additive-noise dynamics, we estimate the corresponding graph using the sparsity of a learned forward-dynamics Jacobian. Empirically, we evaluate TCL-, GCL-, and CEBRA-based ConDA embeddings (Hyvarinen & Morioka, 2016; Hyvarinen et al., 2019; Schneider et al., 2023) on CausalVerse physical and robotic simulations with known ground-truth dynamics (Chen et al., 2025), as well as on Microduck, a controlled bipedal locomotion environment based on the official Pollen Robotics reinforcement-learning framework (Pollen Robotics, 2026). We assess whether these representations recover low-dimensional latent state structure and support lagged causal graph recovery; in Microduck, we additionally evaluate interventional prediction under held-out control perturbations. Overall, our framework evaluates world models beyond reconstruction and forecasting through latent identifiability, causal graph recovery, and preservation of intervention-relevant dynamics. The role of diffusion in our framework is not to claim that diffusion latents are universally superior representations for state recovery. Rather, diffusion supplies a pretrained generative world model capable of synthesizing trajectories. Our question is whether this generative representation can be equipped, without retraining the backbone, with an identifiable coordinate system suitable for causal analysis and intervention. Accordingly, zt is deliberately not a replacement generative representation: it is an identifiable coordinate chart on the frozen generative representation xt.

Contributions. (1) We formulate identifiability and causal graph recovery directly in the latent space of a frozen pretrained diffusion model, separating the underlying dynamical state st, diffusion representation xt, observed frame yt, and auxiliary-aligned embedding zt = h(xt). This decouples the generative world model from causal identification, as the diffusion backbone retains its capacity 2to generate trajectories, while the learned alignment supplies identifiable coordinates for analyzing and controlling its dynamics. (2) We establish sufficient conditions under which TCL- or GCL-based alignment on frozen diffusion latents identifies the latent dynamical state up to permutation and componentwise invertible transformations, preserves the factorization and lagged parent structure of the latent dynamic SCM, and characterizes lagged causal edges through sparsity of the inverse transition-noise Jacobian. (3) We evaluate identifiability, lagged causal graph recovery, and interven-tional prediction across physical and robotic video dynamical systems. TCL- and GCL-based ConDA variants achieve strong, and in several settings near-perfect, blockwise state recovery and support recovery of lagged causal structure, including exact graph recovery in the simulated falling-body system. On controlled Microduck dynamics, we further evaluate whether the learned representations preserve responses to held-out control perturbations. Together, these experiments provide an evalua-tion of diffusion-based world models beyond reconstruction and forecasting, using state recovery, structural recovery, and intervention-relevant dynamics. 

# 2 RELATED WORK 

World models and diffusion representations. World models learn latent representations that support prediction, planning, and control in high-dimensional environments (Ha & Schmidhuber, 2018; Hafner et al., 2019; 2020; 2023). While effective for forecasting and decision making, these representations are not generally identifiable and need not recover the underlying physical state or causal dependencies required for mechanistic interpretation and intervention. Diffusion models provide powerful generative representations for high-dimensional data (Sohl-Dickstein et al., 2015; Ho et al., 2020; Song et al., 2021), while latent diffusion models improve scalability by operating in compressed representation spaces (Rombach et al., 2022). However, diffusion representations are primarily optimized for generative fidelity rather than recovery of identifiable dynamical states or causal structure. We therefore study whether auxiliary-aligned embeddings of frozen pretrained diffusion representations can recover low-dimensional latent states and the lagged causal dependencies governing their dynamics. 

Contrastive learning and nonlinear ICA. Contrastive learning learns low-dimensional representa-tions by bringing positive pairs together while separating negative pairs (Oord et al., 2018; Chen et al., 2020; He et al., 2020). For dynamical systems, TCL exploits temporal nonstationarity to recover latent sources under nonlinear ICA assumptions (Hyvarinen & Morioka, 2016; 2017), while gener-alized contrastive learning (GCL) uses auxiliary variables to establish nonlinear ICA identifiability under broader forms of auxiliary information (Hyvarinen et al., 2019). CEBRA similarly learns low-dimensional embeddings aligned with behavioral, temporal, or neural variables (Schneider et al., 2023). More generally, nonlinear latent variables are not identifiable without additional assumptions (Hyvärinen & Pajunen, 1999; Locatello et al., 2019); auxiliary information such as time, labels, exper-imental conditions, or control inputs can provide the structure required for identifiability (Hyvarinen et al., 2019; Khemakhem et al., 2020). We build on these ideas by applying auxiliary-aligned con-trastive objectives to frozen diffusion latents and using the density-ratio interpretation of contrastive learning to connect the learned embeddings to auxiliary-variable nonlinear ICA. 

Causal representation learning and causal discovery. Causal representation learning seeks la-tent representations that support causal reasoning, intervention, and transfer (Bengio et al., 2013; Schölkopf et al., 2021). Standard learned representations may capture predictive or statistical structure without preserving the underlying causal mechanisms. Causal discovery, in turn, aims to recover directed dependencies from observational or interventional data (Spirtes et al., 2000; Pearl, 2009; Peters et al., 2017). In time-series and dynamical systems, temporal ordering and lagged dependencies provide additional structure for identifying directed interactions (Granger, 1969; Runge et al., 2019; Tank et al., 2021). However, many causal discovery methods assume access to meaningful state variables, whereas in high-dimensional systems the relevant dynamical variables may themselves be latent. We address this gap by studying when embeddings learned from diffusion representations recover identifiable latent states and preserve their lagged causal structure. Under our dynamic SCM assumptions, lagged causal edges are characterized by the sparsity pattern of the Jacobian of the inverse transition-noise map. This characterization is related to Jacobian-based causal discovery under nonlinear ICA (Reizinger et al., 2023), adapted here to auxiliary-aligned representations of frozen diffusion latents in dynamical systems. 3Positioning of this work. Our work addresses a setting largely unexplored in causal representation learning: recovering identifiable and causally meaningful states from frozen pretrained diffusion representations of high-dimensional spatiotemporal systems. Rather than learning the encoder jointly with the temporal or causal model (Yao et al., 2022; Chen et al., 2024; Li et al., 2025), we keep the diffusion representation fixed and learn only a lightweight auxiliary-aligned mapping. Building on auxiliary-variable nonlinear ICA (Hyvarinen et al., 2019; Khemakhem et al., 2020), contrastive density-ratio estimation (Oord et al., 2018), and Jacobian-based causal discovery (Reizinger et al., 2023), we show that the resulting embeddings identify latent dynamical states up to permutation and componentwise invertible transformations, preserve the factorization and lagged parent structure of the dynamic SCM, and characterize lagged causal edges through inverse transition-noise Jacobian sparsity. Empirically, TCL- and GCL-based ConDA variants achieve strong, often near-perfect, blockwise identifiability and recover lagged causal structure across CausalVerse systems, while Microduck experiments show that the learned representations also preserve the direction and temporal structure of responses to held-out control perturbations. Together, these results position pretrained diffusion representations as a reusable substrate for identifiable, structurally faithful, and intervention-relevant world models of high-dimensional dynamical systems.

# 4 EXPERIMENTS 

Experimental Setup. We evaluate identifiability and lag-1 causal graph recovery on video datasets from CausalVerse (Chen et al., 2025), which contain simulated physical and robotic systems with known ground-truth causal structure. Details of the corresponding reference graphs are provided in Appendices A.6, A.7, and A.8. We further evaluate identifiability, controlled dynamics graph recovery, and interventional prediction on videos generated in Microduck, a bipedal robotic locomotion environment based on the official Microduck RL framework (Pollen Robotics, 2026) and simulated in MuJoCo (Todorov et al., 2012). The Microduck dataset is generated using walking and standing policies and, unlike the CausalVerse Physical Fall and Robotics settings, explicitly includes externally imposed control commands. This enables us to assess whether learned representations recover not only the underlying physical state and its dynamical dependencies, but also the system’s response to controlled interventions. For CausalVerse Physical Fall and Microduck, we use 5000 videos, with 1000 held out for testing. For the CausalVerse Robotics Study, we use 2700 videos, with 540 held out for testing. We evaluate ConDA with three contrastive objectives: CEBRA (Schneider et al., 2023), GCL (Hyvarinen et al., 2019), and TCL (Hyvarinen & Morioka, 2016). We compare against five CRL baselines: IDOL (Li et al., 2025), CaRiNG (Chen et al., 2024), TDRL (Yao et al., 2022), temporal variant of SuaVE (Liu et al., 2026), and iVAE (Khemakhem et al., 2020). All methods are evaluated over five random seeds, and we report the mean and standard deviation across seeds. Our pipeline consists of three stages. First, we train a conditional latent diffusion model (cLDM) on the video data. After training, the diffusion model is frozen and used only as a pretrained 6representation model. Each video frame yt is first encoded into the latent space of the diffusion model using the Stable Diffusion VAE (Rombach et al., 2022). We then apply Rex-RK4 (Blasingame & Liu, 2026) with 8 inversion steps to the frozen diffusion model to obtain the corresponding inverted diffusion latent xt. Second, we learn ConDA representations zt = h(xt) from these frozen diffusion latents. ConDA uses temporal or auxiliary information ut through CEBRA, GCL, or TCL to recover structured latent coordinates. The resulting frame-level embeddings are then assembled into latent trajectories {zt}Tt=1 for downstream identifiability, dynamics, and causal analysis. Third, we evaluate latent-state recovery and lag-1 graph recovery from the aligned representations. For CausalVerse Physical Fall and Robotics, lagged dependencies are estimated using residual Lasso with trajectory-level bootstrap stability. For Microduck, we instead learn a controlled transition model [ ˆSt, U t] 7 → ˆSt+1 and estimate state- and control-dependent edges from the average absolute transition Jacobian. Binary graphs are obtained using the reference parent count for each child, applied consistently across methods. This fixes graph density across methods and isolates recovery of the correct parent structure from the separate problem of choosing a sparsity threshold. We therefore interpret binary graph metrics as measuring edge recovery conditional on the true parent count, and additionally report AUPRC as a threshold-free measure of edge-ranking performance. Detailed graph-recovery procedures are given in Algorithms 1 and 2 (Appendix A.11). 

Evaluation Metrics. We measure coordinate-wise identifiability by MCC, the mean absolute Pearson correlation between ground-truth state coordinates and learned embedding coordinates under optimal matching, and block-wise identifiability by R2, the variance of ground-truth state blocks explained by nonlinear regression on zt. For graph recovery, we report F1, accuracy, structural Hamming distance (SHD), AUPRC, and structural intervention distance (SID); formal definitions are provided in Appendix A.9. We evaluate interventional prediction in Microduck using 100 matched simulator trajectories. Starting from the same state at t = 50 , we compare do (ux = ±0.25) and 

do (uy = ±0.17) with the zero-command baseline, holding the other commands at zero, and roll out each intervention for 25 steps (1 s at 25 Hz). The intervention values lie within the empirical training distribution (Fig. 4). For a command uc and its target velocity v, we define

# 5 RESULTS 

Identifiability and Graph Recovery. Table 1 summarizes latent-state identifiability and lag-1 graph recovery across CausalVerse Physical Fall, CausalVerse Robotics Study, and Microduck. On Physical Fall, ConDA-TCL and ConDA-GCL achieve near-perfect block-wise recovery ( R2 ≈ 1) and exact graph recovery (F1/AUPRC = 1 , SHD/SID = 0 ), while iVAE attains the highest MCC ( 0.714 ). On the Robotics Study, ConDA-TCL and ConDA-GCL achieve the highest MCC among the ConDA variants ( 0.679 and 0.683 ) with R2 = 1 .000 ; iVAE performs slightly better on the graph-recovery metrics, whereas ConDA-TCL/GCL remain competitive. On Microduck, ConDA-GCL attains the highest MCC ( 0.629 ), while ConDA-TCL gives the strongest graph recovery among the reported methods in F1 ( 0.769 ), accuracy ( 0.888 ), SHD ( 0.111 ), and SID ( 0.076 ), with AUPRC comparable to iVAE. Overall, ConDA-TCL and ConDA-GCL provide consistently strong performance across both state recovery and lag-1 dependency recovery, although no single method dominates every metric and dataset. Figure 2 visualizes the corresponding binary adjacency matrices. The exact recovery obtained by ConDA-TCL and ConDA-GCL on Physical Fall is clearly visible, while the Robotics and Microduck results show that the ConDA variants preserve much of the sparse reference structure despite several remaining false positive or missed edges. 

Block-wise vs. Coordinate-wise Recovery. Across the three datasets, ConDA-TCL and ConDA-GCL exhibit substantially stronger block-wise R2 than coordinate-wise MCC. This indicates that the learned representations retain nearly all information needed to reconstruct the underlying state, even when individual latent coordinates are not in one-to-one correspondence with physical state variables. We first evaluate our framework on the Physical Fall video dataset from CausalVerse (Chen et al., 2025). This dataset simulates objects undergoing free-fall motion under gravity. The system evolves in discrete time, and the underlying state variables describe the object’s position and velocity. For each spatial coordinate, the temporal transition dynamics are given by 

vt+1 = vt + g∆t, ht+1 = ht + vt∆t + 12 g(∆ t)2,

where ht denotes position or height at time t, vt denotes velocity, g is gravitational acceleration, and

st = [ h1,t , h 2,t , h 3,t , v 1,t , v 2,t , v 3,t ] .

The corresponding dynamic causal graph contains three types of directed time-lagged dependencies: 

ht → ht+1 , vt → vt+1 , vt → ht+1 .

Thus, each future position depends on its current position and velocity, while each future velocity depends on its current velocity. We use gravity and time as auxiliary variables. Because the transition mechanism is known analytically, the Physical Fall setting provides a controlled benchmark for evaluating both source identifiability, through alignment between the learned representations and the true latent states, and dynamic causal graph recovery, through comparison of the estimated lagged adjacency matrix with the known graph. World models and latent dynamical representations. World models learn latent representations that support prediction, planning, and control in high-dimensional environments (Ha & Schmidhu-ber, 2018; Hafner et al., 2019; 2020; 2023). These models have achieved strong performance in reinforcement learning and sequential decision-making, where the primary objectives are typically reconstruction, forecasting, reward prediction, or control. However, predictive accuracy does not ensure that the learned latent state corresponds to the underlying physical or causal state of the system. This distinction is especially important in scientific settings, where intervention and mechanistic interpretation require representations that are not only predictive but also identifiable and structurally meaningful. Our work studies whether frozen diffusion representations can be transformed into latent states that recover the underlying dynamical variables and their directed dependencies. 

Diffusion models and latent representations. Diffusion models are powerful generative models for high-dimensional data, including images and videos (Sohl-Dickstein et al., 2015; Ho et al., 2020; Song et al., 2021). Latent diffusion models improve scalability by performing generation in compressed representation spaces rather than directly in pixel space (Rombach et al., 2022). Although these representations often contain rich semantic and structural information, they are primarily optimized for generative fidelity rather than identifiability or causal interpretability. Contrastive representation learning. Contrastive learning learns structured representations by bringing positive pairs together while separating negative pairs (Oord et al., 2018; Chen et al., 2020; He et al., 2020). The information captured by the learned embedding depends strongly on how positive and negative pairs are constructed. In dynamical settings, time-contrastive learning (TCL) uses temporal nonstationarity to recover latent sources under nonlinear ICA assumptions (Hyvarinen & Morioka, 2016; 2017), while generalized contrastive learning (GCL) uses auxiliary variables to obtain identifiability under broader forms of side information (Hyvarinen et al., 2019). CEBRA similarly learns low-dimensional embeddings aligned with behavioral, temporal, or neural variables (Schneider et al., 2023). In our setting, these objectives serve as auxiliary-alignment mechanisms applied to frozen diffusion latents, and we evaluate whether the resulting embeddings recover identifiable states and support downstream causal structure recovery. 

Nonlinear ICA and identifiability with auxiliary variables. Nonlinear latent-variable models are generally non-identifiable without additional assumptions (Hyvärinen & Pajunen, 1999; Locatello et al., 2019). Nonlinear ICA provides conditions under which latent sources can nevertheless be recovered from nonlinear observations. In particular, auxiliary information such as time, labels, experimental conditions, or context can provide sufficient structure for identifiability (Hyvarinen & Morioka, 2016; 2017; Hyvarinen et al., 2019; Khemakhem et al., 2020). These results motivate our theoretical analysis. We connect auxiliary-aligned contrastive learning on diffusion latents to density-ratio estimation and auxiliary-variable nonlinear ICA, and use this connection to establish sufficient conditions for identifying latent dynamical states from frozen pretrained representations. 

Causal representation learning. Causal representation learning seeks latent representations that support causal reasoning, intervention, and transfer (Bengio et al., 2013; Schölkopf et al., 2021). A central challenge is that representations may capture predictive or statistical regularities without 20 preserving the variables and mechanisms required for causal reasoning. Recent work therefore studies conditions under which learned coordinates correspond to causal variables or preserve structural mechanisms. Our analysis contributes to this direction by showing that componentwise identifiable dynamical coordinates preserve the factorization and lagged parent structure of the underlying dynamic SCM. This links latent identifiability directly to preservation of causal structure in time-dependent systems. 

Causal discovery in dynamical systems. Causal discovery aims to recover directed dependencies from observational or interventional data (Spirtes et al., 2000; Pearl, 2009; Peters et al., 2017). In time-series and dynamical systems, temporal ordering and lagged dependencies provide additional structure for identifying directed interactions (Granger, 1969; Runge et al., 2019; Tank et al., 2021). Many such methods, however, assume that the observed variables already correspond to meaningful system states. In high-dimensional observations such as video, these variables may instead be latent, making causal discovery dependent on the quality of the learned representation. We address this issue by first establishing conditions for identifiable state recovery and then characterizing lagged causal edges through sparsity of the inverse transition-noise Jacobian. This connection is related to Jacobian-based causal discovery under nonlinear ICA (Reizinger et al., 2023), but is developed here for auxiliary-aligned representations of dynamical systems and time-lagged causal structure. 

Causal representation learning for scientific dynamical systems. Recent work has increasingly connected causal representation learning with scientific dynamical modeling. Yao et al. (2024) study the integration of identifiable causal representations with dynamical-system models for scientific applications, while Sun et al. (2025) develop identification theory for causal representation learning from multimodal biomedical observations. More broadly, Camps-Valls et al. (2023) review methods for discovering causal relations, equations, and scientific laws from observational data. Our work is complementary, focusing specifically on frozen pretrained diffusion representations of high-dimensional dynamical systems and evaluating whether auxiliary alignment recovers latent state structure, lagged causal dependencies, and intervention-relevant dynamics. 21
