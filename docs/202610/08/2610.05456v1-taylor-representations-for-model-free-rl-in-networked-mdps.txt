## ABSTRACT 

In Networked Markov Decision Processes, transition dynamics are often unknown and the state–action space grows rapidly with the number of agents. In this set-ting, Taylor representations naturally approximate Q-functions, but a naive order-

n expansion over N agents requires Θ( N n) coefficients. We justify these ex-pansions under smooth expected future local rewards with controlled derivatives. Under this condition, finite-speed information propagation and discounting imply that local-critic Taylor coefficients decay exponentially with the graph distance to the farthest agent involved. Discarding distant-agent coefficients and marginal-izing then yield scalable local Taylor representations with a bound controlled by graph locality. Building on these representations, we propose a scalable model-free actor–critic algorithm, establishing finite-sample critic and near-stationarity guarantees for a linear LSTD critic. We then introduce a more expressive neu-ral TD parameterization. Unlike prior constructive spectral methods, our ap-proach covers settings without access to a known local dynamics map, such as hidden switched linear–quadratic regulation. Across three control benchmarks, our method matches or outperforms spectral baselines while scaling efficiently to large graphs. 

## 1 INTRODUCTION 

Networked Markov Decision Processes, in the context of multi-agent reinforcement learning (MARL), provide a natural framework for cooperative control in large interconnected systems, in-cluding communication networks, traffic systems, queueing networks, and other cyber-physical sys-tems (Qu et al., 2020b; 2022; Chu et al., 2020). They nevertheless face two central challenges: the transition kernel is often unknown and the joint state–action space grows rapidly with the number of agents, making large-scale control challenging. To address the latter, in finite state–action spaces, Qu et al. (2020b; 2022) exploit the exponential decay property of local Q-functions, whereby the influence of distant agents decreases exponentially with graph distance. This enables localized Q-function and policy-gradient approximations. Lin et al. (2021) extend this principle to stochastic non-local dependencies through a more general decay property, while related guarantees also hold for average-reward objectives (Qu et al., 2020a). Related locality phenomena have also been studied in mean-field MARL and networked policy optimization (Gu et al., 2025; Zhang et al., 2023; Liang et al., 2025) and exponential sensitivity decay arises in graph-structured optimization and nonlinear optimal control (Shin et al., 2022; Na & Anitescu, 2020; Na et al., 2022). In continuous state– action spaces, however, localization alone is insufficient: even a local critic remains an unknown continuous function that must be represented and learned. For continuous networked control, recent work addresses this representation problem through spa-tial structure (Olsson et al., 2024) and spectral representations (Ren et al., 2025b; Wang et al., 2026). A main setting targeted by the spectral approaches is one in which each local transition follows a known dynamics map with additive Gaussian noise. Spectral representations, together with finite-dimensional features such as random Fourier features and the exponential decay property mentioned above, then yield scalable critic approximations with explicit guarantees (Ren et al., 2023; 2025a; Rahimi & Recht, 2007). However, this approach can be restrictive: the transition kernel is typi-cally unknown, and canonical network-control problems need not belong to this class of dynamics. 1arXiv:2610.05456v1 [stat.ML] 4 Oct 2026 Switched linear–quadratic regulation (LQR) with an unobserved global mode (Costa et al., 2005; Ogura et al., 2018), for instance, is not captured by such dynamics as it induces state– and action-dependent noise, shared across agents and temporally correlated through the Markov mode. Ren et al. (2025b) also propose a model-free variant named Spectral-SAC, but without the correspond-ing guarantees of their random Fourier features approach. In contrast, we focus here on the fully model-free setting, where the transition dynamics are unknown and only rollout data are available. Beyond the setting of networked MDPs, a complementary route to tractable function approximation is smoothness. Taylor representations have been developed for continuous-state MDPs (Braverman et al., 2020; Xu et al., 2020; Maran et al., 2024a), with Maran et al. (2024a;b) showing how reg-ularizing properties of the Bellman operator can justify polynomial representations. However, the poor scalability of a naive Taylor representation stems from its isotropy: the analysis places all Tay-lor coefficients on equal footing, so an order-n expansion over N agents generally requires Θ( N n)

coefficients. Moreover, much like the dynamics assumptions of prior constructive spectral methods, the smoothness assumption of (Maran et al., 2024a) need not hold in canonical network-control set-tings such as switched LQR with unobserved operating modes, as we show in Appendix E. In our analysis, we instead require smoothness of expected future local rewards in the initial state. 

Our contribution. Our key observation is that, in networked MDPs, Taylor coefficients are not equally important: they themselves inherit graph locality. Finite propagation and discounting imply that derivatives involving an agent at graph distance d decay exponentially with d. This allows us to truncate the global Taylor expansion to monomials supported on N κi , the κ-hop neighborhood of agent i. After marginalizing distant variables, the resulting polynomial is equivalently a Taylor representation of a localized critic with only Θ( |N κi |n) state monomials. Using rollout-based cen-troids as expansion points, we first obtain a linear-in-parameters Taylor Centroid critic and a scal-able actor–critic with finite-sample critic and near-stationarity guarantees. We then generalize the parameterization with neural networks. Unlike prior constructive spectral approaches, our method requires neither knowledge nor a structural specification of the transition kernel. We benchmark it across three control problems of increasing difficulty. Table 1 summarizes how our contributions relate to prior work. ## 7 CONCLUSION AND LIMITATIONS 

We showed that graph locality turns otherwise ambient-dimensional Taylor representations into scal-able local critics without the transition-model access required by prior constructive spectral ap-proaches. This yields a model-free actor–critic for continuous networked MDPs applicable beyond prior frameworks. While experiments show competitive, scalable performance, some limitations also suggest directions for future work. Our LTC guarantee assumes well-conditioned LSTD sys-tems, whose characterization requires complementary analysis. Moreover, the Taylor-coefficient function class is unknown a priori, precluding sharp control of ¯εlin and requiring empirical feature selection. The policy-optimization guarantee reaches a stationary point of J, which may not be a global maximum. K-medians can also be challenging in high-dimensional state spaces. Finally, extending finite-sample critic guarantees to NTC is an important but more challenging direction. 9REFERENCES

## B DEFINITION OF THE ENVIRONMENTS 

This appendix provides the complete description and motivation of the experimental benchmarks used in Section 6. The three environments are deliberately complementary. Networked LQR pro-vides a controlled setting in which the exact critic is quadratic and the assumptions underlying the constructive spectral baseline are satisfied. It is also a canonical environment for the random Fourier feature setting studied by Ren et al. (2025b). Switched LQR introduces potentially unob-served operating regimes through σt and therefore tests the ability of the representation to exploit regime structure without access to the transition model. Finally, the Kuramoto oscillator removes the linear–quadratic structure altogether and tests Taylor Centroid critics on nonlinear graph-coupled dynamics.

B.2 ENVIRONMENT 2: S WITCHED LQR WITH AN UNOBSERVED OPERATING MODE 

Many engineered systems do not evolve around a single stationary operating point. Their dynamics can change abruptly because of faults, topology changes, communication conditions, workloads, or external operating modes. Switched LQR systems with Markovian switching provide a classical model for such phenomena (Costa et al., 2005). They have been used, for example, in fault-tolerant flight control (Gray et al., 2003), power systems subject to abrupt faults or topology changes (Soli-man & Shafiq, 2015), automotive systems with switching communication conditions (Yin et al., 2017), and networked control under correlated packet losses (Xie & Xie, 2008). This setting is particularly relevant to the motivation behind Taylor Centroid critics: a physical system may repeatedly move between a small number of qualitatively different operating regimes even though the underlying state space is continuous. This is the motivation behind the centroids around which our Taylor expansions are performed.

Role of the Kuramoto oscillator for the benchmark. Unlike LQR, the transition dynamics con-tain nonlinear sinusoidal interactions and the critic is non-quadratic.
