Abstract 

This paper proposes a local recursive least squares (RLS) estimation strat-egy for discrete-time adaptive fuzzy control of nonlinear systems represented in quasi-Linear Parameter Varying (qLPV)/Takagi–Sugeno (TS) form. Un-known nonlinear terms are approximated by a constant-consequent TS fuzzy model, and the consequent parameters are updated by a membership-function-weighted RLS law with a forgetting factor. The proposed estimator keeps a different covariance for each rule, considerably reducing the memory footprint of the least-squares updates. The adaptation is also simplified since each rule is only adapted when it is active. From these properties, we are capable of showing that the adaptation law ensures bounded local adaptation errors. Building upon this adaptation law, Linear Matrix Inequality (LMI) synthe-sis conditions are presented for matched, sector-bounded and norm-bounded unknown nonlinearities, guaranteeing ultimate uniform boundedness of the adaptive control system in closed loop. Three numerical examples are pre-sented to illustrate the adaptive control conditions in the three cases: aplanar manipulator with unknown gravity direction, a two-tank system with unknown coupling, and a Brushless DC (BLDC) motor acting as thrust for an efficiency vehicle.

1. Introduction 

Adaptive control aims to develop control laws for uncertain systems with controllers capable of adapting while ensuring closed loop stability and per-formance [1, 2]. Its methods can usually be divided into direct adaptive con-trol, which directly estimates the controller gains [3, 4], and indirect adaptive control, which estimates unknown parameters or dynamics that are then used in the control law [5, 6]. In continuous-time adaptive control, the adaptation laws usually arise from the parameter adaptation errors in the time-derivative of the Lyapunov function, from regression errors, or from a combination of both (leading to composite adaptive control laws) [7, 8]. Due to the fact that in discrete-time the parameter adaptation errors no longer appear linearly in the Lyapunov function analysis, discrete-time adaptation laws are more commonly derived from regression errors, and discrete-time adaptive control cannot be seen as a simple extension of continuous-time adaptive control [9]. One standard approach to estimating unknown nonlinearities is to employ universal approximators in the adaptive control loop [10], such as neural networks [4] and fuzzy systems [11, 6], since they allow unknown functions to be replaced by a set of unknown parameters and an approximation error (which can be made arbitrarily small over a compact operating region). In this regard, Takagi-Sugeno fuzzy systems [12] can be quite interesting since, given a set of fixed membership functions and consequents that are linear in the adjustable parameters, the resulting model will be linear in these adjustable parameters. In addition to this, their rule-based structure provides an inherent decomposition of the approximation into local models, which naturally motivates local parameter estimation [13]. Unmodeled dynamics, disturbances, approximation errors, and measure-ment noise may prevent parameter estimates to remain close to the ideal value, potentially leading to parameter drift in the adaptation laws when no robustness is incorporated into them. This is particularly relevant in the case of universal approximators, as an exact representation of the unknown nonlinearities is generally not available, and a nonzero approximation error might remain [10]. In this case, recursive least squares (RLS) might be more appealing than gradient-based adaptation, since the least squares objective function naturally reduces the parameter estimation sensitivity against these errors [14, 15]. It, however, is not particularly suitable for the long-term operation 2of adaptive control systems, as over time, it becomes harder and harder to adapt (since it weighs all data it has received over time equally), and a for-getting factor modification is usually employed for it to retain its adaptation capabilities [16]. This modification introduces a new problem, though. If the regressors used in recursive least squares are not persistently exciting, the covariance may grow without limit, or winds up . Even though the prediction error is mathematically proven to still converge to zero [16, Proposition 6], numerical instability caused by an ill-conditioned covariance matrix might lead the estimated parameters to diverge. In that regard, approaches such as Dynamic Regression and Mixing (DREM) [7, 17] and Concurrent Learning [15] allow for softer excitation conditions, whereas approaches like Variable Direction Forgetting [16] avoid the covariance wind up altogether by only applying forgetting in the direction of new incoming regressors. Even though these are interesting approaches, all of them generally incur a higher computational cost for the adaptation law, and they still share the same problem as the standard recursive least squares method for large esti-mation problems: the size of the covariance matrix grows quadratically with the number of estimated parameters. Note that this can be quite a problem when using universal approximators, as the number of estimated parameters can grow rapidly, given a desired approximation error and the number of input dimensions for the approximator. With that in mind, and inspired by the local model networks [18] and evolving Takagi-Sugeno (TS) models [13], this paper proposes a local model estimation using constant consequents (for which any regressor signal is ensured to be persistently exciting for the active rules) and a variation of the recursive least squares with separate covariances for each rule, leading to linear instead of quadratic growth for the covariance. Another limitation present in the literature is the system structure for which adaptive fuzzy controllers have been developed. A large portion of the results deals either with feedback linearizable or strict feedback systems and relies on either directly canceling the estimated nonlinearity and obtaining a linear-like closed loop system [19, 20] or using a backstepping control law [21, 22, 11, 6]. This work, instead, relies on the fact that a large class of nonlinear systems can be represented using Takagi-Sugeno fuzzy models, for which Linear Matrix Inequality (LMI) conditions are available for analysis and synthesis problems [23, 24]. Inspired by nonlinear consequent models [25, 26, 27], we allow for sector-bounded mismatched nonlinearities while still ensuring a uniform bound on the closed loop states. Motivated by out-put regulation theory [28], we also propose, for norm-bounded mismatched 3nonlinearities, a feedforward term that approximately rejects the effect of the estimated nonlinearity on a desired output. Given these discussions, this articles contributions are as follows: 

• A novel local model estimation for fuzzy TS models approximating un-known nonlinearities in nonlinear systems is proposed, which ensures that each rule’s parameters and covariance are only updated if its mem-bership function is active, along with an asymptotic bound on the local adaptation errors (Theorem 1). 

• A set of LMI synthesis conditions is presented that ensures the closed loop system is ultimately uniformly bounded with regard to the approx-imation error for the case of matched nonlinearities (Theorem 2) and sector-bounded nonlinearities (Theorem 3). 

• A set of LMI synthesis conditions is presented that ensures the closed loop system is ultimately uniformly bounded with respect to the approx-imation error and the adaptation error (independently of the adapta-tion law used) for the case of norm-bounded nonlinearities (Theorem 4) and the design of a feedforward term that, when used in conjunc-tion with said conditions, ensures that approximately renders a desired output immune to the estimated nonlinearity (Theorem 5).

6. Conclusion and future works 

This paper proposed novel conditions for the project of discrete-time adaptive fuzzy control laws for nonlinear systems in a qLPV/Takagi-Sugeno form. In these laws, fuzzy TS models are employed to approximate unknown nonlinearities in the system, and a local recursive least squares estimation (based on the TS model structure) that only updates a rule’s parameter and covariance while it is active was proposed, together with a stability analysis of this adaptation strategy (Theorem 1). In order to allow for a wider range of systems than the usual ones in the literature (strict feedback or matched nonlinearities), different sets of LMI conditions were proposed to design the adaptive control laws for the cases of matched (Theorem 2), sector-bounded 40 0 5 10 15 0510 0 5 10 15 0510 Figure 10: Comparison of the ¯Tload estimation of the different RLS estimators. The top plot shows the estimation in the scenario using the local RLS proposed in this paper (dashed red line) against the exact ¯Tload (solid blue line). The bottom plot show the estimation in the scenario using the global RLS with a forgetting factor of 0.95 (dashed magenta line) against the exact ¯Tload (solid blue line). 

(Theorem 3) and norm-bounded (Theorems 4 and 5) unknown nonlineari-ties. These are capable of finding, offline , control gains that, when employed together with the proposed adaptation law, guarantee ultimately bounded responses in closed loop. Three examples were presented to illustrate the three different cases for the unknown nonlinearities, as well as different aspects of the proposed con-trollers. The first example, a planar manipulator on a rotated plane (with an unknown rotation), illustrates that the adaptive controller is capable of obtaining a tighter ultimate bound than a controller that is robust against the nonlinearity (as the approximation error will usually be smaller than the bound on a nonlinearity). The second example, a two tank system with an unknown coupling between the tanks, illustrates that the adaptive control law is capable of accommodating a larger uncertainty than a purely robust controller. And finally, the third example, a BLDC motor being used as the thrust for an efficiency vehicle, illustrates that the proposed local adaptation 41 laws advantage in overcoming the covariance wind-up problem compared to a standard recursive least squares with a forgetting factor. Though the proposed adaptation law is capable of adapting the nonlin-earity model online recursively, it does so by only changing the parameters of the consequents of the rules. An evolving TS model [13] would be ca-pable of adding and removing rules from the fuzzy model rule base while it is evolving, allowing for greater flexibility and possibly better approxima-tion capabilities. As such, an interesting future direction of this research is to adapt it to employ evolving TS models instead of regular TS models. Though there are other LMI-based adaptive controllers using evolving TS models in the literature [34], they often focus on the case in which all of the dynamics are unknown and require an LMI condition to be run online. By focusing on certain unknown functions in the systems, we should be capable of designing the controller gains by a set of LMI conditions that are run offline, as is done in this paper. Another drawback of the proposed controllers and adaptation laws is that they require full state information in order to work. As such, a very interesting future research direction would be to study how to adapt these conditions to the output feedback case by employing state observers.
