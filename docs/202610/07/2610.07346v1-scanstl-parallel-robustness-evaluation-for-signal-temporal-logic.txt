# ScanSTL: Parallel Robustness Evaluation for Signal Temporal Logic 

Gokhan Alcan 

Abstract —Repeated evaluation and differentiation of Signal Temporal Logic (STL) robustness can become a computational bottleneck in robot planning and control. Sequential temporal re-currences limit parallelism, while dense masking increases mem-ory requirements. We propose ScanSTL, which combines associa-tive temporal aggregation with parallel scans and ordered block reductions. Eventually and Always use range extrema, while inclusive strong Until composes compact segment representations. A common range engine handles bounded and shifted intervals, including the guards required before delayed Until witnesses. Each exact temporal operator computes complete robustness traces with linear work and storage and logarithmic parallel depth on uniformly sampled finite signals. An open source JAX implementation supports automatic differentiation, batching, and compilation. We compare ScanSTL with STLCG and STLCG++ using CPU and GPU operator benchmarks and nine composed specifications. Across these nine specifications at 512 samples, ScanSTL achieves geometric mean speedups of 243 × for forward evaluation and 104 × for gradient computation over STLCG++ in JAX on the CPU. On an RTX 5090 GPU, ScanSTL evaluates unbounded Until over more than two million samples with median times below 0.1 ms for forward evaluation and 0.25 ms for gradient computation. Simulated escort and patrol experiments with a robot dog further demonstrate faster repair of violating plans and greater solver capacity in model predictive control. Supplementary materials, videos and codes can be found at project website: https://alcanlab.github.io/ScanSTL/ 

I. I NTRODUCTION 

# IN many robotic applications, successful task execution requires satisfying conditions on both motion and the times at which events occur. A robot may need to always avoid obstacles, eventually visit regions within deadlines and remain near a person until the person reaches safety. Its quantitative semantics assign a robustness value whose sign indicates satisfaction or violation and whose mag-nitude provides a margin [2], [3]. This makes a specification useful for verifying and optimizing trajectories. STL has consequently been incorporated into model pre-dictive control (MPC) [4] and optimization using smooth robustness objectives [5], [6]. Automatic differentiation propa-gates robustness through robot dynamics to control inputs [7]. II. R ELATED WORKS 

A. Control Using STL Robustness 

STL robustness guides control synthesis [2], [3]. Raman et al. [4] encoded STL constraints in mixed-integer MPC, while subsequent work reduced the number of binary variables [11]. Direct robustness optimization supports nonlinear dynamics through smooth approximations [5], [6]. Arithmetic-geometric mean robustness accounts for satisfaction beyond the most critical sample [12], robustness-to-go prevents past bottlenecks from obscuring differences between future plans [13], and GradSTL [14] provides smooth STL semantics and derivatives for neurosymbolic reasoning and learning. These approaches develop control formulations or robustness objectives for opti-mization and learning. ScanSTL addresses the complementary cost of repeatedly evaluating robustness and its gradients within optimization. 

B. Exact Monitoring and Associative Algorithms 

Early approaches to efficient STL monitoring reused compu-tations across overlapping temporal windows. Donz´ e et al. [15] combined temporal decompositions with sliding extrema al-gorithms [16]. Deshmukh et al. [17] developed robustness intervals for incomplete traces, and RTAMT [18] translates bounded future formulas into past formulas for incremental monitoring in discrete and dense time. TLTk [19] parallelizes monitoring on CPUs and GPUs with linear trace storage for a fixed formula, but its reported time bound retains a temporal window factor. Rodionova et al. [20] interpreted Boolean MTL as filtering, with separate quantitative kernel scores that differ from spatial robustness. Jakˇ si´ c et al. [21] compute seman-tic distances invariant to formula rewrites through weighted automata, whose size can be exponential in the formula. Mamouras et al. [9] established associative Since/Until pairs and ordered window aggregation with constant amortized work per sample. Their online monitor already achieves linear total work for a fixed formula, but does not develop input deriva-tives or logarithmic parallel depth across time. ScanSTL adapts the local pairs to inclusive Until and combines Blelloch’s scan and recurrence construction [10] with block prefix and suffix reductions [22], [23]. 

C. Differentiable and Parallel STL Evaluation 

Leung et al. [7] introduced STLCG to express STL robust-ness as a computation graph, enabling backpropagation for trajectory optimization, parameter fitting, and learning with logical constraints. Its temporal operators reuse intermediate results through recurrent updates, but the resulting dependen-cies limit parallel evaluation along the trace. Kapoor et al. [8] developed STLCG++ to expose this temporal parallelism by applying masks to expanded signal tensors. The framework supports automatic differentiation in JAX and PyTorch. This construction improves parallel utilization at the cost of larger intermediate arrays. When temporal windows grow with the trace, storage is quadratic for Eventually and Always and cubic for Until, whose witnesses each require a prefix condition. These two designs therefore highlight a tradeoff between sequential dependencies and memory use in differentiable robustness evaluation. ScanSTL combines associative scans and ordered range reductions to compute each exact temporal operator with linear work and storage and logarithmic parallel depth, while retaining automatic differentiation in JAX [24]. We use STLCG and STLCG++ as baselines to evaluate how this formulation addresses the temporal dependencies of recurrence and the storage cost of dense masking. III. P ROBLEM FORMULATION

Each subformula produces a length-T robustness trace, so a formula is evaluated recursively over its syntax tree, with every node returning a trace. The development therefore begins with unbounded Eventually and Always, where the associative-scan structure is visible directly. Bounded ranges and strong Until are then built from the same associative viewpoint.

We presented ScanSTL, a unified evaluator that combines associative scans and ordered range reductions for exact STL robustness. The formulation computes complete traces for Eventually, Always, and inclusive strong Until with bounded and shifted intervals, requiring linear work and storage and logarithmic parallel depth per operator. Its JAX implementa-tion supports automatic differentiation for optimization and control. The benchmarks demonstrate substantial computational gains, particularly for Until and composed specifications. Simulated robot experiments show faster verified plan repair and greater MPC solver capacity. Smooth Until remains a separate quadratic reference.
