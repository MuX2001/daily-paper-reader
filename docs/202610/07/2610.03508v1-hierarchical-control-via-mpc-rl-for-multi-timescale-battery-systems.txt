Abstract: Multi-timescale systems present a fundamental challenge, where fast operational decisions must coexist with long-horizon sustainability targets. In this work, we proposed a new hierarchical control framework via Model Predictive Control (MPC) and Reinforcement Learning (RL) to separate decision-making on two distinct timescales. The high-level MPC optimizes long-horizon setpoints at the slow dynamic and on a fast timescale, a low-level pretrained RL agent tracks these setpoints in real time to maximize short-term objectives. RL is introduced to learn nonlinear control policies, without relying on model linearizations or requiring the heavy online computation from solving repeated optimal control problems. The framework is applied to a Battery Energy Storage System (BESS) operating in frequency regulation markets to balance fast profit opportunities (seconds) and slow battery degradation (weeks to months). The design employs a degradation-aware RL agent trained offline to generate safe long-horizon setpoints, and a degradation-unaware agent fine-tuned from it for fast runtime setpoint tracking. Compared to MPC baselines, the proposed approach successfully extends battery lifetime by 84% and increases operational profit by 34%. 

Keywords: Hierarchical control, Model predictive control, Reinforcement learning, Multi-timescale control systems, Battery energy storage system, Frequency regulation market 1. INTRODUCTION BESS are essential for the integration of renewable energy to ensure grid stability through the frequency regulation market (FR) (Hambissa et al., 2024). These systems store surplus renewable generation and release it later when demand exceeds supply. By rapidly charging and discharging, BESS smooths the variability of renewable energy output and helps maintain the generation–load balance enough to satisfy the frequency limits of the grid(Ma et al., 2023). These fast responses generate revenue opportunities but accelerate capacity degradation, reducing battery lifespan and reducing the return on initial investment (Vykhodtsev et al., 2022).This problem creates a trade-off between short-term revenue and long-term battery health, making it a multi-time-scale system. Their operation is crucial for maintaining long-term economic viability (Reniers et al., 2021). Recent studies show that including battery degradation in the control strategy can increase expected profits by 12% to 46%, depending on the control strategy and the chosen end-of-life limit (EOL) (Wankm¨ uller et al., 2017). To put this into practice, the control framework needs a degradation model that captures how aging limits capacity and how operating choices influence the rate of aging (Zagorowska et al., 2020). Effective control must balance short-term revenue with long-term degradation (Cao et al., 2020). Conventional rule-based or single-layer MPC schemes op-timize short-term profit but overlook degradation effects, and requires solving thousands of large-scale nonlinear pro-grams at a fast timescale. Recent studies have introduced degradation-aware MPC and RL frameworks to improve BESS operation under uncertainty (Li et al., 2022; Cao et al., 2020). RL can also handle nonlinear process dynamics without relying on model linearizations and avoids repeated large optimal-control solves (Sachio et al., 2022). However, purely RL-based methods have been tested only on a single type of BESS; robustness to measurement noise and generalization across chemistries remain unaddressed. This work introduces a new hierarchical MPC–RL control framework for a multi-time-scale system. The proposed architecture separates decision-making on two distinct timescales. The high-level MPC optimizes long-horizon, degradation-aware setpoints at the slow time-scale. On a fast timescale, a low-level pretrained RL agent tracks these setpoints in real time, learning to exploit short-term fluctuations in FR signals to maximize revenue while respecting the MPC-generated safety envelope. We evaluated three different battery systems to examine the ability of framework to generalize between different characteristics of the system. arXiv:2610.03508v1 [eess.SY] 2 Oct 2026 2. METHODOLOGY The proposed framework employs a multi-stage training pipeline to robustly initialize the RL agent before hi-erarchical MPC integration. A behavior-cloning dataset from MPC rollouts first initializes the actor-critic via supervised learning. This policy is refined in a high-fidelity environment (differential and algebraic equation (DAE) system) by coupling a single-particle model (SPM) (San-thanagopalan et al., 2006) with solid-electrolyte interphase (SEI) degradation kinetics to learn a degradation-aware policy that explicitly accounts for capacity fade. To enable real-time 2-second execution, the degradation-aware policy is distilled via anchor-based refinement into a degradation-unaware agent that tracks MPC setpoints without requir-ing online degradation states. Computationally, the low-fidelity linear energy balance model is reserved for fast MPC scheduling, while the DAE-based environment is used exclusively for offline RL training and evaluation. The framework is parameterized for multiple chemistries, including LiFePO 4 (LFP), nickel–manganese–cobalt (NMC) and nickel-cobalt-aluminum (NCA) (Forman et al., 2012; Safari and Delacourt, 2011). These chemistries differ in electrochemical, transport, and SEI-degradation parame-ters. We test controller generalizability by applying the same MPC-RL workflow across systems with different dynamic and aging characteristics. The complete workflow is illustrated in Fig. 1. 

2.1 Data Structure 

We use the Pennsylvania-New Jersey-Maryland (PJM) Interconnection dataset provided in (Cao et al., 2020). The FR signal is normalized, with a two-second resolution and a range of [ −1, +1]. When the signal is positive, the battery is asked to send power to the grid and discharge; when it is negative, the battery should charge from the grid. The dataset also contains hourly day-ahead electricity prices and incentive payments associated with providing FR capacity. 

2.2 Decision Variables 

In the FR market participation problem, the control variables are defined on an hourly schedule. The battery commits a FR capacity for hour t. During that hour, the independent system operator (ISO) may call any proportion of this band every two seconds through the FR signal, and the asset is paid the hourly FR incentive price. We include an adjustable on-site load, served from the battery to help manage state of charge (SoC), which is the fraction of usable energy currently stored in the battery. The load is assumed to remain constant on an hourly basis and does not earn direct revenue.

2.7 Hierarchical Control Strategy 

In hierarchical control strategy, we break down a complex decision problem into layers that operate on different timescales and levels of detail. The higher layer focuses on strategic goals, while the lower layer executes more detailed and faster actions (Olanrewaju and Maciejowski, 2016). Furthermore, the hierarchical framework adds modularity and improves learning efficiency even under uncertain and nonlinear battery dynamics such as high-fidelity environment. At the high level, an MPC operates on a slower time scale and optimizes degradation-aware setpoints for the FR band and grid power over a prediction horizon. We used a high-fidelity battery environment that better reflects real-world degradation and improves the prediction of long-term degradation trends. However, solving MPC in the high-fidelity environment is computationally expensive. To address this, the MPC uses a 20-minute time step and a prediction horizon for 4 hours, which limits long-term anticipation of degradation but maintains the optimization tractable. 2.8 Problem set-up 

The simulation environment was built on the work of (Cao et al., 2020), which we adapted and extended for our framework. All coding was carried out in Julia. For the MPC, the optimization problem is modeled using the JuMP package and solved with the GLPK solver. For the SL stage, the policy and value networks were implemented in Flux and trained with the ADAM optimizer over 5000 epochs. The policy network had four layers with 10, 30, 15, and 2 neurons, using ReLU activation in the hidden layers and tanh in the output layer. The critic network also had four layers with 12, 30, 15, and 1 neurons, with ReLU activation throughout. For the RL training setup, one week was taken as an episode because electricity prices follow a weekly cycle. To encourage exploration, Gaussian noise was added to the actor outputs for better training. In degradation-unaware RL, the setpoint tracking weight was set to λsp = 0 .2. The DDPG training used 

γ = 0 .9, a batch size of 160, an exploration noise scale of 0.05, and an anchor weight of 0.5. Finally, in the hierarchical MPC–RL framework, the upper-level MPC was solved using Ipopt. We used three different battery setup, LFP, NMC and NCA to test generalizability of the framework and RL agent. The maximum charge and discharge were set to 10 MW, and the number of cells was scaled to 1 MWh capacity. We compare the performance of developed FR strategies (Section 2.3 and Section 2.7). Each FR policy was simulated until the battery reached its EOL, which was defined as a 20% capacity fade. 3. RESULTS AND DISCUSSIONS Fig. 2 shows the operational profit of battery overtime for different FR policies under different chemistries. The trajectories differ in length because each simulation was run until the battery reached its EOL. The performance of the implemented FR policies has a significant effect on the bat-tery longevity and profit. MPC alone has poor performance, which indicates that its actions quickly exhaust the battery, resulting in lower cumulative operational profit and faster degradation compared to MPC-RL. The extended battery lifetime under the MPC-RL policy enables longer market participation and results in higher cumulative operational profit. While MPC–RL performed better, it required higher computational effort, averaging 15 s per action compared to 6 s for MPC. This overhead stems from the additional coordination between MPC and RL layers and high-fidelity environment. Moreover, the consistent performance of the MPC-RL policy across different battery types demonstrates the generalizability of the pretrained RL agent. Despite the varying electrochemical characteristics of each chemistry, the MPC-RL controller successfully adapts its actions to maintain stable profitability trends and slower degradation rates.

The results show that degradation-aware strategies bring significant improvements in both battery’s life and opera-tional profit. CONCLUSION This study presented a novel hierarchical control strat-egy for the participation of BESS in FR markets to balance long-term degradation control with short-term revenue. The results show that degradation-aware methods clearly outperform MPC in both lifetime and revenue.The MPC–RL framework was able to extend battery life by 84% compared to MPC. It also increased operational profit by approximately $ 80,000. Despite its advantages, the framework has some limitations. Reliance on repeated nonlinear MPC optimization increases the computational cost, and simplifications in the high-fidelity model may limit generalizability. For future work, the computational cost could be reduced by using surrogate models for the high-fidelity MPC or adaptive horizon strategies. The framework could also be extended to multi-service operation, where BESS participates in energy and ancillary service markets.
