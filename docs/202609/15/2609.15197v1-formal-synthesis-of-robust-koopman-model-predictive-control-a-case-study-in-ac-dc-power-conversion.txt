Formal Synthesis of Robust Koopman-Model Predictive Control:
A Case Study in AC-DC Power Conversion
Shun Hirose, Shiu Mochiyama, and Yoshihiko Susuki
Abstract— This letter proposes a formal synthesis of Robust
Koopman-Model Predictive Control (RK-MPC), a novel data-
driven approach to formal synthesis of systems with nonlinear
dynamics. We formulate a novel optimization problem for
RK-MPC by incorporating specifications described by Signal
Temporal Logic and prove its closed-loop performance. Effec-
tiveness of the proposed RK-MPC is evaluated by applying it
to the reliable design of an AC-DC power converter.
I. INTRODUCTION
Koopman-Model Predictive Control (K-MPC) is a data-
driven optimal control method for nonlinear systems [1].
The basic idea of K-MPC is to utilize a linear or bilinear
model of a nonlinear plant for predicting the state or output
of the nonlinear plant and to generate an optimal input. We
refer to the linear/bilinear model, guided by the Koopman
operator theory [2], as the Koopman model. K-MPC has
been successfully applied in engineering domains, including
robotics [3], chemical processes [4], and energy systems [5]:
see references therein. In [6], the authors applied K-MPC to
a real AC-DC converter motivated by More Electric Aircraft
(MEA) [7] and experimentally showed that the K-MPC
controller performs better than other existing controllers.
The so-called Robust K-MPC (RK-MPC), which is a com-
bination of K-MPC with robust control techniques, has been
reported; see, e.g., [8], [9]. The Koopman model involves
modeling error arising from the linear/bilinear approximation
of a nonlinear plant and from its estimation from finite data.
This modeling error causes a K-MPC controller to violate
constraints prescribed in the optimization problem; see, e.g.,
[8], [9]. The RK-MPC controller considers the modeling
error of the Koopman model explicitly and hence guarantees
that the closed-loop response satisfies the prescribed con-
straints. Notably, the authors of [10] propose a data-driven
modeling framework, called SafEDMD, which considers the
Koopman model with an error bound proportional to the
norm of the state and control input. This robustness guarantee
makes K-MPC more practical in terms of reliability; see [11]
for details. This reliability has been required by mission-
critical applications, including MEA as mentioned before,
where the aircraft’s electrical standards, MIL-STD-704F
[12], specify that, for example, a system’s voltage should be
maintained above a threshold even during load fluctuation.

In this letter, we propose formal synthesis of the RK-MPC
and apply it to an AC-DC converter. Formal synthesis aims
to design systems from temporal logic specifications [13].
Temporal logics, including Signal Temporal Logic (STL)
[14], [15], are able to describe complex specifications of
temporal properties and have been widely used in controller
synthesis; see, e.g., [16]–[18]. The contributions of this letter
are threefold:
1) We propose a novel formulation in the RK-MPC that
realizes data-driven formal synthesis of controllers for
nonlinear systems. Following [16], we encode STL
specifications as inequality constraints with max/min
functions. Then, we formulate an optimization problem
with both the inequality constraints from STL and the
Koopman model.
2) We prove in Theorem 2 that the proposed formulation
guarantees robust satisfaction of the STL specifications.
To ensure robustness to the controller, we tighten the
inequality constraints associated with the STL specifica-
tions by incorporating a modeling error assumed in the
SafEDMD framework. This implies that the formulation
guarantees a robust control strategy that satisfies the
STL specifications despite the modeling error.
3) We present a case study of reliable design for AC-DC
power conversion. We set the STL specifications for the
AC current and DC voltage of an AC-DC converter and
then formulate the optimization problem. Through nu-
merical simulation, we show that the AC-DC converter
controlled by the proposed K-MPC controller meets the
specification despite modeling error.
Note that the authors of [16] propose formal synthesis of
MPC for linear plants by using STL, and the authors of [17]
make it robust to disturbances. The authors of [18] realize
formal synthesis of the K-MPC by using the Koopman model
and STL. In this letter, we formally synthesize the K-MPC to
be robust to modeling error and guarantee robust satisfaction
of STL specifications, which, to the best of the authors’
knowledge, is the novelty of this letter.

B. Signal Temporal Logic
STL is a logic to formally specify temporal properties
of time-series signals [14], [15]. We treat the system state
trajectory (1) as a time-series signal and describe temporal
properties of the system’s trajectory using STL. First, we
define the predicate, which is the basic component of STL
formulae. A predicate π for the discrete-time system (1) is
defined as an inequality of the state x[k] and input u[k] at
time k, given by
π := {µ(x[k], u[k]) := Gxx[k] + Guu[k] + H ≥0} ,
where µ : Rn × Rm →R is the so-called predicate
function [15], Gx ∈R1×n and Gu ∈R1×m user-defined
row vectors, and H ∈R a user-defined scalar. A predicate
π is satisfied by x[k] and u[k] at time k if and only if
the corresponding predicate function is non-negative, i.e.,
µ(x[k], u[k]) ≥0. Second, we introduce an STL formula
by combining predicates with the following grammar: ¬
(negation), ∧(disjunction), ∨(conjunction), U[a,b] (timed
until), F[a,b] (timed eventually), and G[a,b] (timed always).
See [14] for the detailed description of the grammar. From
[17], we introduce the horizon length hφ for the STL formula
φ. At time k, the STL formula φ is evaluated on the time-
sequence of states and inputs (x[k : k + hφ], u[k : k + hφ]).
The validity of an STL formulae is evaluated with an
inequality of composition of max and min functions with
predicate functions. To do so, we introduce the robust-
ness function [15], which is a function of the predicate
functions, denoted as ρφ, for a given STL formula φ.
Suppose that φ is composed of p predicates given as
π1 = {µ1(x[k], u[k]) ≥0}, . . . , πp = {µp(x[k], u[k]) ≥
0}. Here, the robustness function ρφ depends on a set of
predicate functions, defined as M(x[k : k + hφ], u[k :
k + hφ]) := {µ1(x[ℓ], u[ℓ]), . . . , µp(x[ℓ], u[ℓ])}k+hφ
ℓ=k , and
also depends on time k. The aforementioned STL grammar
excluding negation can be expressed by a max/min function
or its combination (see [15], [16]). In addition, negation
is distributed to predicates, and negation of a predicate is
expressed by multiplying the associated predicate function
by −1 (see [17]). A formula φ is satisfied by x[k : k + hφ]
and u[k : k + hφ] at time k if the corresponding robustness
function is non-negative, that is, ρφ(M(x[k : k + hφ], u[k :
k + hφ]), k) ≥0.

mulation (5) is robust to the modeling error of the Koopman
model while satisfying the given STL formula φ. The novelty
of Theorem 2 is to prove the robust satisfaction of the STL
formula.
Remark 2: The optimization problem (5) is a mixed-

variables [16]. Furthermore, (5) can be relaxed by rewriting
hard constraints (5e) and (5f) into soft constraints if the
solution of (5) is not located [17].

excursion. Overall, the above specifications are to prevent
the trip of the AC source while maintaining the DC voltage.

We show the robustness test for the proposed formu-
lation. As mentioned in Sec. IV-B, we derived the three
Koopman models with different degrees of modeling error.
Here, we check if the proposed formulation is robust to
varying degrees of modeling error, namely, using the datasets
with different D for the modeling. Specifically, we test this
by designing the controllers in Sec. IV-C using the three
Koopman models and applying them in the fault scenario
described in the previous paragraph. TABLE I shows the test
results. The controller with c = 0.005, which we utilize in the
previous paragraph, guarantees the robust satisfaction of the
specification despite degrees of modeling error. Furthermore,
we change the parameters c = cz = cu of emax in (4), which
are treated as tuning parameters in [10], and check how these
parameters affect the control performance. In the case of
c = 0, the maximum error emax is assumed to be emax = 0,
thus the controller does not take into account the modeling
error. Consequently, the controllers with any model fail to
achieve the specification (violated). In the case of c = 0.003,
the maximum error emax is assumed to be too small, and
the controllers also fail to achieve the specification. On the
other hand, all of the controllers with c ≥0.01 fail to locate
a solution of (5). Thus, we show that the robustness and
feasibility of the proposed formulation is affected by the
tuning parameter c and, in this case study, ensured as long
as we choose the tuning parameter c as c = 0.005.
V. CONCLUSIONS
This letter proposed the formal synthesis of RK-MPC and
applied it to the AC-DC power converter. Our proposed
formulation handles the STL specification, including the de-
scription of temporal properties and theoretically guarantees
the robust closed-loop performance in terms of satisfying the
STL specification. We showed the effectiveness of formal
synthesis of RK-MPC for the AC-DC converter by compari-
son with the standard K-MPC. Furthermore, we verified the
robustness of the proposed formulation using the Koopman
models with varying degrees of modeling error. Future work
includes an experimental realization of the formal synthesis
of RK-MPC for the AC-DC converter.
