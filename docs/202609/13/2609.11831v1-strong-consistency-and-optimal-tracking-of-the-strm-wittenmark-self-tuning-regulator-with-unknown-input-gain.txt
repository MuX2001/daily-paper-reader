STRONG CONSISTENCY AND OPTIMAL TRACKING OF THE ÅSTRÖM–WITTENMARK SELF-TUNING REGULATOR WITH UNKNOWN INPUT GAIN 

ZHAOBO LIU 

Abstract. We study strong consistency and optimal tracking of the Åström–Wittenmark self-tuning regulator with unknown input gain. Existing results establish stability, optimal tracking and parameter consistency for the unmodified recursion under growth conditions on reference information. Other approaches obtain performance guarantees by adjusting the estimates used in feedback or adding decaying probing signals. For a class of minimum-phase linear systems with martingale difference noise, we establish joint guarantees for ordinary least squares with certainty-equivalent control without reference excitation, gain adjustment or added probing. For each bounded reference, average input and output energy are almost surely bounded, average squared tracking error converges almost surely to the noise variance, and all parameter estimates are strongly consistent whenever at least two parameters are estimated. For the consistency result, the key is a logarithmic lower bound on the cumulative squared difference between an auxiliary least-squares prediction of the noise and the reference. If the gain error persisted, this noise information and the actual least-squares recursion would give incompatible lower and upper bounds on the same weighted squared sum. This contradiction establishes gain convergence before stability and full parameter consistency.

Existing designs address this dependence by changing the estimator or controller, or by supplying additional excitation. Stochastic gradient [9], modified LS [22, 5] and weighted LS [2] alter the parameter update. For non-Gaussian disturbances, Filipović [8] uses a nonlinear score function in the estimation recursion to obtain stability and optimal tracking without modifying the gain matrix. On the control side, adjustments to the estimates used in feedback limit the effect of small estimated gains [24], while robust minimum variance designs use prior gain information to obtain stability and performance guarantees under model perturbations [21]. Probing inputs supply information independently of the prescribed reference [16, 7, 17, 4]. More recent work quantifies learning costs through finite time guarantees for identification and adaptive control of autoregressive exogenous systems [18] and regret bounds for minimum variance regulators combining probing with input clipping [23]. For the original LS-based STR, stability and optimal tracking are established for bounded references when the input gain is known, under suitable model and noise assumptions [12, 11, 3]. Under coprimeness and degree conditions, Guo [11] also established strong consistency of the remaining parameter estimates without requiring reference excitation. For an unknown gain and unit delay, Kumar [13] used Bayesian embedding to establish optimal tracking of bounded deterministic references and almost sure convergence of the parameter estimates to a random multiple of the true parameter vector. These results hold under his model and noise assumptions and exclude a Lebesgue null set of true parameters. An additional average energy condition on the filtered reference then yields convergence to the true parameter vector. Guo [10, 11] established stability, optimal tracking and parameter consistency, together with convergence rates, for the original controller when the reference information grows sufficiently fast. For bounded references without this growth requirement, Guo [11] obtained performance guarantees by adjusting the estimates used for control or adding decaying probing signals. We ask whether the original regulator can achieve both optimal tracking and identification of the true parameters when the input gain is also unknown, without requiring excitation from the reference. Optimal tracking alone does not imply consistency of all parameter estimates [14]. The remaining issue is whether the original recursion forces the limiting multiplicative factor to equal one without reference excitation. Resolving it would allow learning and tracking to proceed with a reference chosen solely for the control task. Under the conditions in Section 2, we prove almost sure stability and optimal tracking of the original regulator for each bounded reference. When at least two parameters are estimated, the entire parameter estimate converges almost surely to its true value. The result holds for the original LS-based STR without added probing. The results apply when B is minimum phase, A − 1 and B are coprime, and at least one of their prescribed order bounds is attained. They extend the classical strong consistency guarantee for known input gain [11] to the original regulator with unknown gain, without requiring excitation from the reference. For parameter dimension at least two, the proof first establishes gain consistency, thereby determining the common parameter scale before proving stability. We construct an auxiliary LS prediction driven by process noise whose cumulative squared difference from a bounded reference has a logarithmic lower bound. To transfer this information to the actual recursion, we use Guo’s estimates along subsequences on which the gain error stays bounded away from zero [10, 11]. These estimates apply before stability is established and, together with the LS identities, control the variation of the LS projection. If the gain failed to converge, these bounds and the noise information would impose incompatible upper and lower bounds on the same weighted energy sum. This yields gain consistency, followed by stability, optimal tracking and consistency of the remaining parameter estimates. SELF-TUNING REGULATION WITH UNKNOWN INPUT GAIN 3

Conclusion 

Under the stated assumptions, the ordinary LS-based STR with unknown input gain achieves almost sure stability and optimal tracking for each bounded reference, and strong consistency when d ≥ 2. The key observation is that persistent gain error would force incompatible bounds on the same weighted squared sum. An auxiliary LS prediction extracts information from process noise that a bounded reference cannot cancel, whereas the identities of the actual LS recursion impose a smaller upper bound. This contradiction establishes gain consistency before stability is known. Stability and optimal tracking then follow, allowing consistency of the remaining parameter estimates to be established. These asymptotic properties do not ensure finite expected control energy over a finite horizon, even with bounded continuous noise, as Appendix E.2 shows. A further question is how to obtain bounds on this expected energy while retaining the stability, tracking and consistency properties established here.
