# Policy Gradient Learning for Distributionally Robust Markov Decision Processes under Wasserstein Ambiguity 

## Yadh Hafsi ∗ Samy Mekkaoui † Huyên Pham ‡ Kaixin Yan §

## June 29, 2026 

Abstract 

We study finite-horizon Markov Decision Processes (MDPs) under distributional uncertainty in the transition kernels and develop a policy-gradient framework for Wasserstein distributionally robust control. Ambiguity is modeled by state-action dependent Wasserstein balls around nominal transition kernels, leading to a max-min control problem over randomized policies and admissible transition laws. Since the worst-case transition law depends implicitly on the policy parameters, the usual policy-gradient argument does not apply. We address this difficulty by using a Wasserstein dual reformulation of the robust Bellman recursion and analyzing its directional differentiability. This yields an explicit recursive characterization of the robust policy gradient. Building on this characterization, we propose a robust actor-critic algorithm and illustrate its behavior on discrete and continuous benchmark examples. 

MSC Classification : 90C40, 90C17, 93E20, 49L20, 68T05. 

Key words : Robust Markov decision processes; Robust reinforcement learning; Actor-Critic; Dynamic programming principle; Randomized policies; Policy gradient; Distributionally robust optimization. 

## 1. Introduction 

In many reinforcement learning and control problems, the transition law of the system is not known exactly. Even when a nominal model is available, the system may later be tested or deployed in an environment that differs from the one used for calibration. Different data sources, market conditions, or operating regimes may induce different probability measures on trajectories. As a result, a policy that performs well under one reference model may perform poorly when the underlying dynamics change. This motivates robust reinforcement learning, whose goal is to construct policies that perform well under a family of plausible transition laws rather than under a single nominal model. This issue appears naturally in many applications. In inventory control and supply chain management, demand distributions are difficult to estimate precisely and may vary over time because of seasonality, structural change, or external shocks. Robust and distributionally robust methods have therefore been used to hedge against ambiguity in demand models; see for example Bertsimas and Thiele [2006], Scarf [1958], Gallergo and Moon [1993], Kim and Chung [2024]. This has led to distributionally robust formulations under Wasserstein ambiguity Blanchet et al. In all these settings, uncertainty acts through the law of the controlled state process. In our framework, this uncertainty is represented by set-valued transition kernels Pt(x, a ), and robust optimization over these sets leads to policies that are more stable under model misspecification. We study finite-horizon robust Markov decision processes with randomized policies and set-valued transition kernels. Our main focus is on ambiguity sets defined around reference transition laws. This setting is attractive because it models local distributional perturbations in a flexible and interpretable way. At the same time, it creates a basic difficulty for policy optimization. Under general ambiguity classes, such as Wasserstein or Kullback–Leibler balls, the worst-case transition kernel is typically not available in closed form. It is selected adversarially according to the value function generated by the current policy, so changing the policy can also change the worst-case model. This makes direct differentiation of the robust Bellman recursion difficult, and generally intractable without strong regularity assumptions on the optimizer (see Remark 3.3). 

Related work. There are two main lines of literature behind our approach: robust Markov decision processes (MDPs) and distributionally robust optimization (DRO). On the robust MDPs side, the foundational papers Iyengar [2005] and Nilim and El Ghaoui [2005] proved robust dynamic programming under rectangular ambiguity sets, showing that the Bellman recursion remains tractable when uncertainty separates across state–action pairs. Since then, robust MDPs have been studied under broader forms of model uncertainty (see Lim et al. [2013] for example). MDPs under general model uncertainty are studied in Neufeld et al. [2023]. Policy gradient methods for robust MDPs have recently been developed in Wang et al. [2023], while robust Q-learning under Wasserstein ambiguity is studied in Neufeld and Sester [2024]. In a related direction, Coache and Jaimungal [2026] propose an actor–critic algorithm for robust risk-aware reinforcement learning under Wasserstein ambiguity, deriving policy gradients via the quantile representation of distortion risk measures. On the distributionally robust optimization side, a general convex framework and tractable reformulations are given in Wiesemann et al. [2014]. For Wasserstein DRO, dual reformulations and performance guarantees were developed in Mohajerin Esfahani and Kuhn [2018], while structural properties of worst-case distributions were analyzed in Gao and Kleywegt [2023]. The duality result most relevant for our work is Blanchet and Murthy [2019], which gives a scalar dual representation for Wasserstein DRO through optimal transport. Related links between Wasserstein robustness and regularization in statistical learning are established in Blanchet et al. [2019]. Sensitivity of robust values with respect to the Wasserstein radius is studied in Bartl et al. [2021], with related results under financial constraints in Sauldubois and Touzi [2024], and dynamic robustness on path space in Compoint et al. [2025]. The closest related work is Wang and Zou [2022], which studies policy gradient methods for robust MDPs under R-contamination sets. In that setting, the robust Bellman operator associated with contamination sets admits a closed-form expression. This makes the policy gradient derivation relatively direct. Under more general types of ambiguity like Wasserstein or Kullback-Leibler ambiguity, however, the worst-case transition kernel depends on the value function through an optimization problem, and differentiating through it would require regularity conditions on the optimal kernel that do not hold in general (see Remark 3.3). We instead work with the dual formulation of Blanchet and Murthy [2019], which avoids differentiating the optimal kernel directly. Our framework also differs from the robust Q-learning algorithm of Neufeld and Sester [2024], which operates in the infinite-horizon discounted setting with deterministic policies and tabular updates. By contrast, our actor–critic approach handles finite-horizon problems, optimizes over randomized parametric policies via gradient ascent and accommodates continuous state spaces through function approximation. Our main contributions. We derive directional policy-gradient formulas for finite-horizon robust MDPs with state–action dependent Wasserstein ambiguity sets. Our approach combines the robust dynamic programming recursion with a Wasserstein dual representation, thereby avoiding differentiation of the implicit worst-case transition kernel. Since the robust Bellman operator is generally nonsmooth, our main result is a one-sided directional derivative formula, which reduces to a vector-valued policy-gradient recursion under uniqueness of the dual multiplier and inner transport selector. More precisely, our contributions are as follows. 

• Robust Bellman and Wasserstein dual formulation. We formulate the finite-horizon distributionally robust control problem under state–action dependent ambiguity sets and establish the corresponding robust dynamic programming recursion. For Wasserstein ambiguity sets, we use the dual representation of Wasserstein DRO to rewrite the robust Bellman operator as an optimization over a scalar dual multiplier. 

• Directional policy-gradient representation. We prove continuity and directional differentiability of the robust value functions with respect to the policy parameter (Propositions 3.7 and 3.14). We then derive explicit one-sided directional derivative formulas for the Wasserstein robust Bellman operator and the associated value recursion (Theorem 3.15 and Corollary 3.1). When the dual multiplier and the inner transport selector are unique, these directional formulas simplify to a vector-valued robust policy-gradient recursion. 

• Robust actor–critic algorithm. We propose an actor–critic implementation based on the dual robust Bellman recursion and the derived gradient formulas. The critic approximates the robust value function, while the actor is updated using the corresponding robust policy-gradient recursion. We illustrate the method on inventory control, coin-toss, multi-armed bandit, and linear-quadratic control benchmarks, covering both finite and continuous state-space settings.

Remark 3.16 (Finite state–action spaces) When X and A are finite, the compactness and measurability conditions used above hold automatically.
